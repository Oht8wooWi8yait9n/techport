Project Description

The OSU and Raytheon Technology team will create and demonstrate a modular DC-Energy Router that not only can function as a power flow controller but also as an intelligent circuit breaker, thus realizing interconnections and power flow optimizations between multiple lunar surface power systems. During the project, the team will focus on energy routing and system stability enhancement, over current and fault current limiting, digital twin and model-predictive control, sensor placement optimization for system reliability, and concept validations with a 120 V, 10 kW prototype.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This project will provide a modular and flexible solution for control, protection and interoperability. Additionally, the project will enable fuel saving and reliability improvement, with a reduction in fault energy and collateral damage. Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Ensuring reliable power systems on the lunar surface is paramount for sustaining essential research endeavors and supporting life. Standalone or interconnected grids integrating intermittent power sources and distributed energy storage systems are vital components of lunar infrastructure. Direct current (DC) microgrids offer a promising solution for seamlessly managing power distribution across the lunar surface. Building upon our initial proposal, we have successfully accomplished all outlined objectives and milestones. Key achievements include a validated three-stage approach for planning, controlling, and protecting interconnected lunar DC microgrids, the creation of a 120-V 10-kW flexible DC energy router (FeDER) featuring Gallium Nitride (GaN) devices, the development of a power hardware-in-the-loop based test bed, and the execution of impactful demonstrations. Furthermore, we have established connections with potential end users to ensure the applicability and relevance of our solutions. In detail, the three-stage approach includes:  Graph Theory & Fault Diagnostics: We have utilized graph theory principles to analyze network topology, enhancing system resiliency. Our fault diagnostic techniques enable swift detection and isolation of faults, minimizing disruptions to the power supply.  Energy Management System (EMS): An advanced EMS has been developed to optimize energy allocation and utilization within the interconnected microgrid network. Real-time monitoring and dynamic control algorithms ensure grid efficiency under varying conditions.  FeDER-based Control & Protection (FCP): Implementation of the FeDER as a modular power management unit has been a focal point. FeDER integrates local energy storage and addresses various microgrid power management needs, including fault management, stability enhancement, and power quality improvement. Robust control and protection mechanisms ensure safe and reliable operation. Based on the project, we have demonstrated how potential interconnected lunar DC microgrids could be controlled and operated reliably and be protected and reconfigured effectively during potential faults. The outcome of the project together with other ongoing meaningful research around the world is forming a solid foundation for continued lunar exploration and research. The project team has already published 9 papers and 1 Master's thesis. At least 4 more papers and one Ph.D. dissertation are currently under preparation. 17 students have been trained during the project, including 4 female students and 1 African American student. 9 students have already graduated, all of whom are working for U.S. companies. The educational impact of this project would be long-lasting. In summary, our achievements mark significant progress towards establishing robust power systems on the lunar surface and sustaining the workforce for related U.S. industries.
