Characterization and Application of Cryogenic Power Semiconductors in Lightweight, Fault-tolerant Systems for Lunar Missions

Project Description

Solar-powered systems for lunar missions must tolerate and operate in the extreme cold of the lunar night. To ensure efficient and reliable energy conversion and control, power regulators and breakers are crucial, and their survivability at cryogenic temperatures is essential. At the heart of the power regulator and circuit breaker reside power semiconductors and corresponding power electronics technology. The fundamental research goal of the project is to holistically characterize and model target wide bandgap gallium nitride (GaN) transistors in a wide temperature range; and to develop power regulation and solid-state protection techniques empowered by such cryogenic devices in lightweight, fault-tolerant solar-powered systems for lunar missions.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The research objectives are to (i) conduct a holistic characterization of gallium nitride (GaN) transistors from diverse manufacturers with statistically meaningful sample sizes, including cryogenic temperatures; (ii) establish a temperature-dependent physics-informed model with statistical variation and key parameters’ degradation; and (iii) design and optimize i) > 99% efficiency, > 15 kW/kg regulation switch, and ii) > 99.9%, > 100kW/kg isolation switch. A three-step technical approach was devised: (i) characterization of GaN across wide temperature ranges including static and dynamic characteristics, short-circuit withstand capabilities, and reliability considering degradation; (ii) modeling considering statistical variation and device parameters’ degradation; and (iii) demonstration of GaN transistors considering the cryogenic operation, including i) regulation switches and ii) isolation switches. For the target power semiconductors, the results and key findings during the reporting period include (i) reduced on-state resistance at low temperatures, enabling more efficient operation of regulation and isolation switches; (ii) digital gate driver control enabled programmable GaN impedance in a wide temp. range, potentially for battery cell/string management; (iii) reduced leakage current at low temp., ensuring the safe operation of regulation and isolation switches in the off-state w/ breakdown; (iv) reduced third quadrant on-state voltage at low temp., enabling efficient reverse conduction of regulation switches during the PWM operation; and (v) characterization and modeling considering statistical variation are essential. The project benefits Lunar Power Hibernation with perspectives of (i) string shunt regulator between solar array and main bus through voltage control, (ii) main bus power supply for main bus controller and battery management system (BMS), (iii) isolation switch between battery and main bus, (iv) preheater power switching, (v) BMS pre-charging and cell fault isolation switching, (vi) power inhibits and avionics power distribution, and (vii) possible RF transceiver.
