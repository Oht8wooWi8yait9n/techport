Photonic Radar Technology for Earth Science

NASA TechPort Project ID: 117159
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

We propose to investigate the generation of versatile radar radio frequency (RF) waveforms using the beat frequencies of two locked lasers and then applying an external amplitude and frequency modulation to one laser.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This technology development will produce significant size, weight and power, and cost (SWaP-C) savings to future Earth Science remote sensing radar developments.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The objective of this project is to demonstrate and further mature photonic-RF technology to reduce risk for future application in cloud and precipitation radar development. The effort was focused on a rooftop demonstration and characterization of a prototype Ku-band (13.9 GHz) photonic atmospheric radar system based on a photonic transceiver circuit developed with support from the Earth Science Technology Office (ESTO) Advanced Component Technology (ACT 2020) program. Key accomplishments of this IRAD project include:1) Design of a Ku-band (13.9 GHz) photonic radar system; 2) Integration and laboratory testing of the photonic radar, and 3) Rooftop test and performance evaluation through measurements of ground level target and a thunderstorm. The experimental results demonstrate strong performance of the photonic-RF technology and indicate its growing maturity for remote sensing applications. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies. Goddard Space Flight Center’s IRAD program is managed under its Office of Chief Technologist. Activities develop concepts, reduce technology risk, and advance human capital and technological capabilities. Goddard’s IRAD program is highly competitive, opportunity-driven, and 100% strategically aligned with NASA's and Goddard's strategic priorities. A significant portion of the program is focused on Early Stage Innovations for high-risk, strategically aligned, potential high-payoff technologies that are longer-term or lower TRL.
