Nanofabrication of High Temperature Superconducting Josephson THz Mixers, Year 1

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95644
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

High Temperature Superconducting (YBCO) Josephson Junctions have historically had poor performance and reproducibility. This work is enabled by the junction fabrication process developed by a group at UCR, utilizing a focus beam of He ions (beam diameter ~1 nm) to directly write the Josephson barrier into junctions. This method enables precise tuning of the junctions which made Josephson mixers difficult to match into microwave circuits in the past. The mixers have a large IF bandwidth and the nanometer scale junctions will require only nanowatts of local oscillator power, important to move to large focal plane arrays of detectors.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The goal is to develop a high temperature superconductor Josephson mixer for THz high resolution spectroscopy. A factor of 4-5 improvement over state of the art at 2 THz compared to other low temperature detectors is expected. The junction parameters do not degrade with temperature until above 50 K means that these detectors should be more than an order of magnitude mores sensitive then available detectors for planetary missions requiring passively cooled instruments. This could be significant in enabling high resolution THz spectroscopy planetary bodies from a nearby orbit (for example comets). 50 K operating temperature would enable a Rosetta MIRO-like instrument with orders of magnitude better sensitivity to look at outer solar system objects. 4 K operation would enable a Herschel HIFI- like instrument, with large focal plane arrays of detectors (compared to single pixel) and improved integration time by an order of magnitude or better (depending on telescope size).

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This work utilizes advanced capabilities to realize an efficient THz mixer with a relatively high operating temperature. The FY19 CIF task was to develop a fabrication process for Josephson Mixers and demonstrate the ability to couple efficiently to a planar antenna. Impedance matching of the junctions to the antenna has historically been the single largest obstacle to realizing Josephson mixers with record sensitivities in parts of the Far-IR. The innovation of the He+ focus ion beam (He+ FIB) has enabled fabrication of high temperature superconducting YBCO devices down to the nanoscale. This has facilitated the direct write of YBCO Josephson junction barriers (~1 nm) into superconducting bridges. Such high precision with such small critical dimensions is favorable to produce Josephson Mixers that are well-matched to a planar antenna. In addition to the sensitivity, the nano-devices are inherently small, meaning that the local oscillator power needed to pump them is extremely low. This is particularly important for heterodyne arrays that used to map large portions of the sky, or obtain good special resolution on a nearby target (such as a comet). The devices fabricated during this effort showed very good sensitivity. Given the losses that can be accounted for (> -4dB), the noise temperature of these devices (<1000 K at 564 GHz) is second only to SIS mixers.
