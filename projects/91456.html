Project Description

The direct imaging and characterization of Earth-like extrasolar planets is one of the most prominent goals of modern astrophysics. The main technological challenge for direct imaging is to block the glare and diffraction from the star so that the faint planet light located close to the star can be seen. High performance coronagraphs are the most likely tool to overcome this technical challenge. With this NASA Space Technology Research Fellowship, I propose to develop and demonstrate an achromatic phase-shifting focal plane mask, which will provide significant improvement to almost any high performance coronagraph. An achromatic focal plane mask will provide a broader observational band, improved inner working angle and contrast, increased science yield, and reduction of required telescope diameter and complexity. More specifically, the goal of this NSTRF is to develop an achromatic focal plane mask for the Phase-Induced Amplitude Apodization Complex Mask Coronagraph (PIAACMC) (Guyon 2010). PIAACMC operates by applying a À phase shift to a portion of the on-axis starlight using a partially transmissive phase shifting focal plane mask. The use of a Lyot stop induces interference between the phase shifted and un-shifted starlight, which results in total on-axis starlight extinction. PIAACMC provides 50% throughput at 0.64 »/D and nearly 100% throughput at large angular separations. These performance characteristics are very close to the fundamental physical limit for any instrument. The focal plane mask needs to provide a constant phase shift for all wavelengths within the observing bandwidth. However, applying a phase shift is not an inherently achromatic operation. Along with Dr. Guyon and Dr. Belikov, I have developed and tested a method to create achromatic focal plane masks using a diffractive optical filtering technique (Belikov 2003). By applying a similar concept, we can develop an achromatic phase shifting mask suitable for PIAACMC. The proposed research directly addresses technology area 8.1.3.1, Starlight Suppression, and is complimentary to Dr. Guyon’s OCT funded research at the University of Arizona, which addresses wavefront control for segmented apertures. The proposed research is also complimentary to research at the Ames Coronagraph Experiment laboratory, which is an ideal location for the NASA on-site experience. The proposed mask design and demonstration will also serve as my PhD dissertation topic.

  • Closed Out: My PhD was focused on developing focal plane masks for exoplanet coronagraphy. Specifically, I developed methods to create broadband complex phase masks for the Phase Induced Amplitude Apodization technique. PIAACMC is one of the highest performing architectures for exoplanet coronagraphy, and can approach the ideal performance for any direct detection technique. The architecture is relevant for future NASA missions (WFIRST, Exo-C, etc.) and is also relevant for large aperture ground-based telescopes. The design and fabrication of the focal plane masks to operate in broadband was a new challenge. Throughout the course of my PhD, I demonstrated techniques to design the masks, fabricate the masks using electron beam lithography and etching, and correct for mask fabrication errors using wavefront control. The results of my work show that it is possible to create broadband focal plane masks for PIAACMC, and they can deliver unprecedented performance for the direct imaging of extrasolar planets. Designed, fabricated, tested, and published an achromatic size-scaling focal plane mask for exoplanet coronagraphy. This provides an immediate performance increase to coronagraph architectures which use an opaque disk focal plane mask. Developed a design algorithm for PIAA complex focal plane masks which allows the masks to be designed for broadband. Started a relationship with the Stanford Nanofabrication Facility to fabricate focal plane masks. Designed, fabricated, and measured broadband PIAA complex focal plane masks specifically for the ACE PIAA optics. Demonstrated the use of wavefront control with PIAA complex focal plane masks to correct for mask manufacturing errors. Published two first-author peer-reviewed journal papers. Published five conference proceedings papers. Presented my work at numerous conferences and meetings. Completed a PhD in Optical Sciences.
