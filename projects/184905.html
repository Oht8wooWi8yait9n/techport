Project Description

The Mars Campaign Office (MCO) Life Support Systems project Environmental Monitoring (EM) systems task objectives are to develop and demonstrate onboard monitoring, detection, and analysis capabilities that will replace the state-of-the-art (SOA) need to frequently return water samples to Earth for ground-based laboratory analysis. This effort is addressing these challenges by adopting new analytical technologies and techniques that will allow for a modular EM system architecture that integrates multiple sensing modalities to address the monitoring needs of future deep-space exploration missions. 

Spacecraft Water Impurities Monitor (SWIM): The development of an in-flight water quality monitor for organic and inorganic impurities to protect crew health and vehicle hardware.

 

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

•Provides in-flight identification and quantification of a wide range of organic and inorganic species of concern to the toxicology, Environmental Control and Life Support Systems (ECLSS), and Extra-Vehicular Activity (EVA) communities.

•Allows for near real-time sample analysis, providing on-demand data to mission managers. Knowledge of contaminant amounts and identities has a twofold impact:

•Enables cognizant, risk-based decision making with respect to go/no-go for water use (e.g. crew consumption, space suit loading). 

•Allows for sensible diagnosis of off-nominal ECLSS performance.

Water samples are distributed to the core chemical instruments - Organic Water Module (OWM) and Inorganic Water Module (IWM). These instruments perform separation-based methods of analysis to identify and quantify organic and inorganic chemical impurities in the water sample.
  • Example Data from SWIM OWM Prototype System — A chromatogram from a prototype of the Organic Water Module (OWM) within the Spacecraft Water Impurity Monitor (SWIM). A chromatogram is a plot of detector signal versus time as the OWM performs its separation-based analysis. Each peak represents an individual organic chemical that was present in the water sample analyzed.
  • Example Data from SWIM IWM Prototype System — An electropherogram from a prototype of the Inorganic Water Module (IWM) within the Spacecraft Water Impurity Monitor (SWIM). An electropherogram is a plot of detector signal versus time as the IWM performs its separation-based analysis. Each peak represents an individual inorganic species (ion) that was present in the water sample analyzed.
