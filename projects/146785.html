Project Description

The Goddard Earth Observing System (GEOS) uses a data assimilation system that relies on the Gridpoint Statistical Interpolation (GSI) system for analyzing observations. This project aims to improve the representation of GEOS in the mesosphere and lower thermosphere by introducing SABER (TIMED mission) data. We will also port the SABER capabilities of this work into the next generation data assimilation system under development now, the Joint Effort for Data assimilation Integration (JEDI).

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The aspirational goal of this project is to produce atmospheric background states up to the lower thermosphere (~110-120 km) that can be utilized by upcoming spaceflight mission opportunities, such as AWE and EZIE scheduled to be launched in late 2023 and 2024, respectively. Ultimately, this project will enable the inclusion of other heliophysics data in GEOS through follow-on efforts that will extend its capability to higher altitudes and enable its use in space weather applications.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The goal of this project has been to demonstrate that the introduction of temperature observations from the mesosphere (50-90 km) in the data assimilation cycle of the Goddard Earth Observing System (GEOS) model leads to improvements in the representation of the meteorology of the upper atmosphere. We have used temperature observations from the Sounding of the Atmosphere using Broadband Emission Radiometry (SABER) onboard of the Thermosphere Ionosphere Mesosphere Energetics and Dynamics (TIMED) spacecraft and we have successfully demonstrated that the assimilation of these observations results in consistent improvements to the modeled atmospheric state. This pilot project was successful in demonstrating that the integration of upper atmospheric observations in the GEOS system is (1) feasible and (2) leads to quantifiably improved atmospheric states. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
