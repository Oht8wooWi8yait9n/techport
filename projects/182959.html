Project Description

Through this CESO project, the KSC Cryogenics Test Laboratory (CTL), in partnership with Eta Space LLC, in Rockledge, Florida, aimed to complete integrated testing of an Electro-Chemical Hydrogen Refrigeration (ECHR) system capable of producing net positive refrigeration at liquid hydrogen (LH2) temperatures with no moving parts. Most ECHR test hardware had been initially developed by NASA KSC and commercial partner Meta Vista as part of a 2018 Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD), Center Innovation Fund (CIF) project of the same name, including design and construction of the cold-box portion of the system at the CTL, and procurement of the electro-chemical hydrogen compressor from an outside vendor. However, due to major delays in delivery of the compressor, final integrated system testing was never accomplished as part of the CIF project. Since then, the compressor was delivered to Meta Vista, and checkout testing was successfully accomplished by their affiliate Eta Space. By bringing together the cold-box hardware, which had been held in storage at the CTL (Figure 1, left and middle pictures), with the compressor at Eta Space (Figure 1 right), testing would be carried out at Eta Space’s facility. This will draw upon Eta Space’s in-house expertise in conducting many safe and successful cryogenic hydrogen test campaigns, with two primary goals: 1) demonstrate the system could achieve saturated LH2 temperatures close to the normal boiling point of 20 K while running in closed loop, with a target of 22 K, and 2) characterize the isothermal refrigeration power at the saturation point, with a target of 1 W @ 22 K.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Ways in which the ECHR concept can be used in future space missions for both government and private include liquid cryogenic zero boil off, liquefaction and storage of In Situ Propellant Production (ISPP) of cryogenic propellants, and cryocoolers for remote sensing and infrared detectors.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The project achieved the first of its primary objectives by successfully reaching hydrogen saturation temperatures on two separate occasions. This effectively proved the concept of ECHR to deep cryogenic temperatures and advanced the TRL from 3 to 4 or 5 depending on the specific application. Additionally, it is believed to be the first time that the LH2 temperature range has been accessed by a refrigeration cycle with zero moving parts—although it was discovered that Boeing explored the concept in the 1980’s, even publishing related patents in 1986 and 1988, followed by Creare in the early 2010’s, but neither effort successfully reached temperatures below 120 K. Unfortunately, the team was unable to directly quantify the isothermal refrigeration power during testing due to a variety of factors, hence fell short of the second primary objective. However, post-test analysis of the data allowed for estimates of the refrigeration power and showed that successful Test Run #1 produced an estimated isothermal refrigeration power of 0.57 W at steady hydrogen saturation temperature of 22.8 K, and Test Run #2 produced 0.78 W of refrigeration with an average temperature of 23.9 K, and a minimum temperature of 22.05 K. Many valuable lessons were also gained from testing the system that can translate to future ECHR designs, most important of which concerns the control system for the electro-chemical compressor. Due to the closed-loop configuration, any changes made, or events associated with one component in the system can have an immediate and unpredictable effect on other components. Because the particular compressor used for the project was not specifically tuned for an ECHR application, events such as pressure fluctuations from the cold-box, or changes in humidity levels inside the compressor would trigger the control system to shut down inadvertently. This was a constant struggle during testing, and could most likely be solved for future ECHR designs if the compressor and its controls are purpose-built and tuned for the application.
