Project Description

​The Super-lightweight Aerospace Composites project will advance the maturation of carbon nanotube (CNT) reinforced composites to enable their application in aerospace structures.  State of the art lightweight structures are constructed from aluminum, titanium, or carbon fiber reinforced polymer composites (CFRP), with the latter providing a strength to mass advantage over the former. The long-term goal of this project is to contribute to significant mass savings in space structures including but not limited to  CNT composite components suitable for Nuclear Thermal Propulsion (NTP) and Lunar/Mars space vehicle structural elements such as pressure vessels, heat exchanger elements and trusses.   Attainment of the greatest payoff in structural CNT composites will require the maturation of CNT manufacturing to yield commercial scale volumes and quality of CNT reinforcement not available at the inception of SAC, as well as developing optimal materials processing approaches to capitalize on the performance enhancements promised by this emerging reinforcement material.  Evidence of maturation includes building of a mechanical property dataset beyond axial tensile properties typically reported in the literature.  Thus far, transverse tensile, axial and transverse flexure, and fracture toughness properties have been generated, with compressive property generation underway.  This advancement is made possible with the availability of required CNT yarn being produced at commercial scale in collaboration with a CNT manufacturer.  Manufacturing capability has progressed to pilot scale production.  This project is conducting modeling and experimental research complementary to the modeling and processing work being carried out under the UltraStrong Composites by Design (US-COMP) NASA Space Technology Research Institute (STRI).

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

​ Preliminary analysis indicates that there is potential for ~25% mass savings if structural CNT composites can have specific tensile properties at least double those possible with CFRPs.  When benchmarked against aluminum, mass savings can potentially be on the order of ~50%.  Recent advances in systems analysis indicate that additional quantifiable benefits can be gained by enhancing stiffness of CNT composites relative to SoA composites that can be used not only for primary structures but also secondary structures.  The availability of large quantities of high performance CNT material produced in an industrial environment has made it possible not only to generate necessary mechanical properties, but it also enables the evaluation of multifunctional CNT in applications that take advantage of its multifunctional properties in applications of interest to other government agencies.  Internally, CNT composite processing optimization efforts are informing potential applications where CNT materials outperform state of the art materials being used.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Although progress was made in the development of CNT composite fabrication methods under the GCDP Nanotechnology project, there remained several critical technology gaps that had to be addressed for this emerging technology to realize its potential. Foremost among the required critical advancements for broader use of this potentially game-changing emerging material was the large-scale manufacturing of CNT materials with the appropriate mechanical characteristics, made available in volumes and formats amenable to the exploitation of the unique nanoscale properties of CNTs to yield structural materials that outperform state of the art lightweight aerospace materials. NASA Space Technology established the Superlightweight Aerospace Composites (SAC) project led by Langley Research Center within GCDP in FY 2018. Since its initiation, a major focus of the SAC project was to accelerate maturation of the CNT material to enable its infusion into relevant applications that exhibit systems-level benefits of this material. The SAC project concluded in December of FY25 fully achieving seven of its objectives and significant progress in the remaining two as described below. In commercial applications, the time required to complete a material research and development project, including its implementation in a product, typically takes about 10 years after the need has been identified. Similarly, in typical NASA missions, the window within which materials decisions for systems are made occurs many years before launch. Once materials choices are established, the opportunity for new materials insertion can be decades away. Given this backdrop for advanced materials development and considering the systems level payoffs for employing emerging materials for lightweight structures enabled by the attractive nanoscale properties of CNTs, the focus of the SAC project was translational research. Specifically, the SAC Project assessed the gaps between fundamental laboratory findings and real requirements defined by specific technical challenges to focus research efforts on closing such gaps.Following the principles of use-inspired research, the SAC project defined goals targeting CNT large-scale manufacturing gaps and the development of methods using CNT materials in structural composites to showcase mass savings that can be realized with the retention of nanoscale properties at the macroscale structural composites level. SAC project goals were to 1) mature and demonstrate carbon nanotube material production rates and 2) to mature process manufacturing of CNT composite materials to increase the mechanical properties benchmarked against carbon fiber composites.Funding to achieve the objectives of the SAC project has yielded what is essentially a new CNT material in the last five years, capable of broad applications envisioned in the early days of the nanotechnology hype. This material is different from the type more widely available on which most of published literature work has been based. The work of the SAC project has taken structural CNT beyond an academic curiosity and innovation trigger to move closer to practical utility in many applications for which its attributes yield measurable and meaningful advantages. These results are largely due to the NASA Space Technology investments to enhance the mechanical properties of continuous CNTs and improve manufacturing approaches that lead to lowering material costs, including but not limited to, automation and in-line quality monitoring.
