Robust Entry and Landing Guidance Under Dynamic Uncertainty

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95505
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Future missions to Mars that will land large payload masses to a variety of locations across the Martian surface will require the use of propulsive control during descent. This necessitates the development of new guidance algorithms since current algorithms are for aerodynamic control only, or apply to propulsive control during the final stages of descent. This project seeks to develop these new guidance algorithms, with a focus on creating algorithms that are robust to the major sources of uncertainty during Entry, Descent, and Landing (EDL). The algorithms will incorporate machine learning methods to predict the effects of un-modeled perturbing accelerations, such as wind gusts and atmospheric conditions, and will make control decisions based on the non-linear propagation of system uncertainty to the targeted conditions.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The objective of this work is to design and test the feasibility of a new robust guidance algorithm applied to EDL problems. This will create and implement a general aerospace guidance algorithm that will robustly deliver a vehicle to a desired target state in the presence of uncertainty.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This grant worked on the development of guidance and navigation algorithms to improve the robustness of landing accuracy with powered descent at Mars. In particular, we sought to develop a stochastic guidance algorithm that plans and executes its trajectory while incorporating the effect of uncertainty. This means that instead of invoking the separation principle to plan guidance commands without explicitly accounting for uncertainty, our algorithm will adapt based on the trajectory flown so far (as known by the on-board navigation system) as well as predictions about the future performance and expected uncertainty of that performance. Our approach was to extend state-of-theart numerical predictor corrector guidance methods to incorporate uncertainty. During this grant, we investigated two main components that were needed to implement this approach. The first is dealing with uncertainty in the system and the second is incorporating the uncertainty into the guidance algorithms. Dealing with uncertainty for predictor-corrector guidance algorithms largely revolves around having good uncertainty propagation capabilities. Our problems of interest make this difficult for two reasons: first, the problems are non-linear and therefore linear propagation of Gaussian uncertainty does not generally perform well; second, one of the main sources of uncertainty in EDL problems is the atmosphere which is highly dynamic and difficult to easily parameterize while maintaining the high accuracy needed for our goals. We addressed the former by investigating the use of different uncertainty quantification methods including using Gaussian Mixture Models (GMMs) and Polynomial Chaos Expansions (PCE), as well as trying to identify better representations of the state/uncertainty through quasi-initial conditions or using higher-order dynamics information. We addressed the complicated atmospheric uncertainty problem by training Long-short Term Memory (LSTM) neural networks to capture and predict the atmospheric profile based on on-board measurements. We demonstrated three approaches to incorporating uncertainty into the guidance algorithms. The first approach builds directly off the LSTM work by incorporating the LSTM model into the well-known FNPEG entry guidance algorithm. By retraining the LSTM within the closed-loop system, we were able to demonstrate a robustified guidance system as desired. Our second approach took the more direct view of building extended numerical predictor-corrector guidance algorithms that act on the sigmapoints of an unscented transform representation of the state uncertainty. Three stochastic decisionmaking algorithms were developed that allow guidance to make predictor-corrector updates to the Page 2 – Revised 02/2018 guidance commands involving uncertainty propagation. The third approach uses higher-order expansions of the dynamics using State Transition Tensors (STTs) to quickly reoptimize a trajectory with chance constraints, or to create a high-order feedback controller about a reference trajectory. While the motivating application for this grant is to improve the robustness of landing accuracy on missions using powered descent at Mars, our techniques are more broadly applicable. Improving landing accuracy depends on all aspects of the EDL problem, and our advancements have improved the robustness of entry guidance as well as powered descent. Further, since entry guidance is so closely related to aerocapture/aerobraking guidance we have improved those applications as well. Finally, almost any spacecraft guidance application could be improved and made more autonomous with the algorithms we have developed during the course of this grant.
