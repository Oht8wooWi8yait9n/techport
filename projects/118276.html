Multiscale Modeling to Enable Physics-Based Simulations of Plume-Surface Interactions with Quantified Uncertainty

NASA TechPort Project ID: 118276
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The success of future lunar and planetary exploration missions will require predictive simulation tools that capture the complex multiphase dynamics associated with rocket exhaust impingement during touchdown. As a spacecraft approaches the surface of a terrestrial body, high-speed ejection of granular material results in scouring and dust impregnation of exposed hardware, reduced visibility, and potential spoofing of the landing sensors. However, existing modeling tools are unable to simulate these environments accurately. The key objective of this project is to develop advanced physics-based models and numerical algorithms to enable predictive simulations of plume-surface interactions (PSI) under relevant landing conditions. A multiscale approach is designed to connect the flight-scale landing model to two-phase statistics obtained from direct numerical simulations (DNS) that fully capture relevant microphysics. In addition, novel uncertainty quantification (UQ) techniques will be used to measure the effect of modeling parameters on key quantities of interest associated with plume-induced cratering and ejecta characteristics.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The success of future manned and robotic space exploration missions will require accurate modeling of rocket exhaust impingement during touchdown to design for risk and develop mitigation strategies This project will enable large-scale predictive modeling capabilities to support NASA-centric Eulerian-based codes. This project examines new Mach number-dependent drag coefficients, subgrid-scale turbulence closure, and particle-phase constitutive models. Additionally, quantified uncertainty of the modeling parameters under extreme conditions relevant to landing events

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Future space missions will require understanding and predicting the complex multiphase flow from rocket exhaust impingement during landing. The objective of this project is to develop advanced physics-based models and numerical algorithms to enable predictive simulations of plume-surface interactions (PSI) under landing conditions. A multiscale approach is employed, designed to connect the flight-scale landing model to two-phase statistics obtained from direct numerical simulations (DNS) that fully capture relevant microphysics. A new simulation framework was developed that fully resolves compressible flows past collections of particles spanning subsonic to supersonic speeds. Data obtained from these simulations were used to construct the most comprehensive particle drag law to date. In addition, a novel two-equation turbulence model for compressible gas-particle flows was developed and demonstrated in an Eulerian-based two-fluid code. Concurrent to these efforts, high-fidelity simulations of plume-surface interactions were conducted using a new Euler-Lagrange method with a focus on cratering and ejecta dynamics.
