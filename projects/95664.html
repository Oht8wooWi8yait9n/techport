Characterization and Preservation of Lunar Polar Volatiles for Sample Return and ISRU, Year 1

Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

We propose to build on current JSC lunar volatiles research by developing a high-fidelity volatile-rich lunar simulant and assessing its alteration under flight-like conditions. First, we will refine an existing lunar simulant developed at JSC by adding compounds previously detected on the Moon but not included in current efforts. Second, we will subject the simulant to temperature and pressure profiles mimicking the sample collection process and expected return transit storage conditions. Third, we will assess the ideal storage and curation conditions by assessing the chemical and physical alteration of the sample under a range of conditions, including storing the simulant at 70-200K and vacuum-1 atm for up to one year.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This project is filling a gap in NASA's simulant development capabilities by providing a process for producing high-fidelity volatile-rich lunar simulant. The development and testing of this simulant will directly benefit many of the ISRU target needs. Further research beyond this IRAD would include development of ISRU and curation technologies utilizing our high-fidelity simulant. Derivatives of this simulant could be designed for specific research goals to support technology development efforts. Permanently shadowed regions in the south pole are the most scientifically interesting areas to be explored by the new human lunar exploration program and demand for this type of simulant will be high.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The goal of this project is to support lunar exploration by simulating the ice and volatile-rich materials expected to be found at the lunar poles. These materials are highly sensitive to temperature increases, which could significantly alter their chemistry and composition prior to accurately characterizing them. Our goal was to begin development of lunar volatile-rich simulants and a system for preserving them. In addition, we planned to conduct storage testing of simulant materials at different temperatures to see how warm they can get before there are irrevocably altered. During FY19, we purchased the materials needed to construct a simulated lunar environment, within which simulated volatile-rich materials could be made. We also refined the procedure for creating moderate-fidelity lunar volatile-rich materials at cryogenic temperatures. Our test setup is now largely complete, and can reach cryogenic temperatures and near-lunar pressures with active compositional monitoring of the volatiles in the vapor phase. We have purchased a negative-pressure glovebox to support handling these sensitive materials; once the glovebox arrives and is installed, we will begin the storage testing of lunar simulant materials. The results of these tests will directly feed into engineering and science requirements for ongoing lunar exploration activities.
