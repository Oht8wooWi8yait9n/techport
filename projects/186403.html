Image-Based Crater Detection

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186403
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The image-based crater detection challenge sought to advance automated techniques for identifying and characterizing craters in synthetic lunar terrain imagery. Crater detection is a vital task for planetary science. The results of the crowdsourcing initiative will be used to enable the next generation of terrain-based optical navigation. 
 
Traditional crater detection methods rely heavily on manual review and are difficult to scale across vast lunar dataset as well as use in real time. By crowdsourcing solutions through the Topcoder Community, NASA will tap into a global pool of solvers to design and optimize computer vision and machine learning algorithms that meet strict accuracy, runtime, and hardware requirements.

Participants will compete head-to-head in a Marathon Match competition. Solutions will be tested against training, testing, and evaluation datasets, with performance measured through reproducibility, robustness, and accuracy.

At the end of the project, NASA will receive validated algorithms, source code, documentation, solver white papers, and reproducibility scripts. These deliverables will expand NASA’s toolkit for lunar surface analysis, accelerating exploration and scientific discovery.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The goal was to develop a C or Python-based software tool (algorithm, neural network, etc.) capable of reliably detecting and outputting points or ellipses delineating the edges of visible crater rims in images of the lunar surface.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: To date, the PO team has had only one existing crater detection algorithm. The crowdsourced algorithms showed a significant improvement in crater detection performance right away, outperforming the existing algorithm specifically from a crater detection standpoint. All models submitted demonstrated high performance. The PO team is currently integrating and testing the new models within their overarching navigation pipelines to evaluate their impact on downstream navigation processes. Future work may involve fusing different models. The team is very happy with the variety of pre-processing and training methods employed, which offers combinable options for the future. Most models are performant enough for direct integration. Overall, the team is pleased with the level of global participation. The project owner team said that they are interested in trying to get photos from Artemis II fly-by to test the crater detection algos. These crater detection algorithms are not just applicable to manned cislunar missions, but could potentially be used for robotic, drop-off missions that support lunar outpost and space commercialization. Learned a lot from how solvers set up their models. Lots of lessons learned internally from observing how the deliverable models worked. Diversity of models in short period of time. Looking at how to improve their own model, using the top TC model in their own TRON model. Difficulty: creating a well-formulated challenge. In implementing solutions in pipeline, there are hurdles we didn't anticipate in the requirements. Great potential from solutions; but now the PO needs to go do some homework on how to implement.
