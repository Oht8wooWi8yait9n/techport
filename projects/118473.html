Stochastic Multiscale Fatigue Life Prediction Framework for Next Generation Durability and Damage Tolerance

NASA TechPort Project ID: 118473
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Structural failure due to fatigue loading remains to be a tremendous concern for air- and spacecraft that operate in extremely harsh loading and environmental conditions. Initiation of fatigue cracks constitute a very significant portion of the overall life of metallic structural components especially under high cycle and very high cycle fatigue loading conditions. Despite this observation, current durability and damage tolerance approaches primarily rely on analysis of the long crack growth regime in the design, certification and maintenance of flight hardware with only empirical treatment of initiation. Fatigue crack initiation is significantly affected by the microstructural features of the material, and exhibits high degree of variability due to the random nature of the material microstructure. In order to overcome these challenges, this project will (1) establish predictive computational models for fatigue initiation life in metallic structures that directly incorporates features of the material microstructure; (2) quantify the role of microstructure induced uncertainties and sensitivities on the variability in fatigue crack initiation life; and (3) demonstrate the validity and feasibility of the computational approaches on metallic alloys of high relevance to NASA applications.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This project aims to achieve the ability to predict crack initiation life with the level of fidelity similar to that of the long crack growth regime. This project will facilitate risk-informed durability and damage tolerance based design including initiation regime and provide significant life extension of future air and space platforms and drastic reduction of weight in fatigue-critical structural components.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The goals of this research were to devise a multiscale and probabilistic computational framework that enables microstructure-informed prediction of fatigue crack initiation in structures made of polycrystalline materials and quantify the role of material microstructure and associated uncertainties on fatigue initiation life. In order to achieve the research goals, the following specific objectives were proposed: (1) establish a multiscale approach to fatigue life prediction that concurrently couples material scale polycrystal plasticity simulation with the structural scale simulation by relying on physics-based reduced order microstructure modeling, and efficiently tracks the time evolution of microstructural deformation and damage throughout the structure and loading history; (2) devise a data-driven uncertainty quantification methodology to quantify the role of microstructure induced uncertainties and sensitivities on the variability in fatigue crack initiation life; and (3) demonstrate validity and feasibility in the context of polycrystalline metals by exercising the proposed stochastic multiscale life prediction framework. Towards achieving the overall goal and the specific objectives, this project resulted in the development of (1) a probabilistic fatigue nucleation framework that was employed to predict probabilities of failure in Titanium Alloy (Ti-6242S); (2) reduced order models for investigating the mechanical response at the scale of the polycrystal when microstructurally short cracks are present. These models led to at least two orders of magnitude reduction in computational cost relative to crystal plasticity finite element (CPFE) analysis; (3) a new microstructurally short fatigue crack propagation simulation framework based on crystal plasticity finite element method; and (4) a new computational approach that enables prediction of polycrystal microstructure morphology and manufacturing process-induced defects in the context of Laser Powder Bed Fusion additive manufacturing. Aspects of these computational tools has been verified and validated in the context of titanium alloys, Ti-6242S and additively manufactured (AM) Ti-64, as well as AM Nickel-based superalloy IN 625. Some of the outcomes of the project have been transitioned to NASA. For instance, the capabilities of the NASA-developed element-by-element conjugate gradient-based finite element solver, Femera, has been extended by the project team and transitioned back to NASA for further internal development. The reduced order microstructure models established in this project have been transitioned to NASA for use within the probabilistic multi-fidelity microstructural response prediction framework. This framework is being developed through a Transformational Tools and Technologies (TTT) Project at NASA Langley Research Center. The AM process modeling aspects of the project geared towards microstructure prediction are now being further developed as a part of the newly formed NASA Space Technology Research Institute (STRI) on Model-Based Qualification & Certification of Additive Manufacturing (IMQCAM).
