ISRU-ISRU Hydrogen Oxygen Production (IHOP-BAA)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 116390
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The ISRU-ISRU Hydrogen Oxygen Production-BAA task aims to develop and test the integrated performance of water purification and hydrogen-oxygen production components. This project will:

  1. produce a TRL 5 Ionomer-membrane Water Processor (IWP) that can purify water extracted from lunar regolith that has been captured by an upstream cold trap,
  2. produce a TRL 5 water electrolyzer that can generate hydrogen and oxygen streams from the water provided by an upstream purification processor that can operate for the length of time and survive the freeze/thaw cycles expected on the lunar surface, and
  3. integrate the water purification unit with the electrolysis unit and test the integrated subsystem over a long duration.

Currently, no water cleanup system exists to remove potential contaminants in ice and water extracted from the lunar surface. Also, no PEM electrolyzer yet exists to generate O2 and H2 from lunar water and that functions for 2 years in the lunar environment. The TRL 5 hardware delivered will meet key production metrics of 1.34 kg/hr of H2O, 0.14 kg/hr of H2, and 1.16 kg/hr O2. As of the submission date of this SPAR update, a TRL 5 Ionomer-membrane Water Processor (IWP) and a TRL 4 water electrolyzer have been achieved. However, because of technical difficulties with testing the water purification unit that contains the Ionomer-membrane Water Processor (IWP), long duration testing of the integrated IHOP subsystem composed of the water purification unit and the electrolysis unit was not achieved during the Option Period. Thus, TRL 5 of the water electrolyzer was not achieved as planned.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This is a new integrated technology/capability to enable future human exploration of the Moon and Mars that does not now exist. This technology can be used in a Lunar IRSU pilot plant to demonstrate the capability to purify water extracted from regolith to make it safe for astronauts, and also for electrolysis to produce streams of oxygen and hydrogen for propellant. This project leverages Paragon Ionomer-membrane bundle technology used in the Boeing crew transport vehicle to develop the water clean-up processor. The technology developed by this project has both Exploration & Science applicability. It provides a water purification processor upstream of water electrolyzer for a future ISRU plant that cleans up and removes contaminants observed by the LCROSS mission. The project also provides an electrolyzer to produce hydrogen and oxygen for propellant in the harsh lunar environment for a long duration using the lunar water cleaned up by the upstream purification processor.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The ISRU Hydrogen Oxygen Production (IHOP) Project aimed to develop and test the integrated performance of water purification and hydrogen-oxygen production components. The IHOP project planned to 1) produce a TRL 5 Ionomer-membrane Water Processor (IWP) that can purify water extracted from lunar regolith that has been captured by an upstream cold trap, 2) produce a TRL 5 water electrolyzer that can generate hydrogen and oxygen streams from the water provided by an upstream purification processor that can operate for the length of time and survive the freeze/thaw cycles expected on the lunar surface, and 3) integrate the water purification assembly (WIPE) with Hydrogen Oxygen Production Assembly (HOPA) containing the electrolysis unit and test the integrated IHOP subsystem. Currently, no water cleanup system exists to remove potential contaminants observed by the Lunar Crater Observation and Sensing Satellite (LCROSS) mission in ice and water extracted from the lunar surface. This project leverages Paragon Ionomer-membrane bundle technology used in the Boeing Starliner (or CT-100) crew transport vehicle to develop the water clean-up processor. Also, no Proton Exchange Membrane (PEM) electrolyzer exists to generate O2 and H2 from lunar water that will function for 2 years in the lunar environment. The IHOP hardware developed was expected to meet key production metrics of 1.34 kg/ hr of H2O, 0.14 kg/ hr of H2, and 1.16 kg/ hr O2. A TRL 5 Ionomer-membrane Water Processor (IWP) was developed and tested. A TRL 4 PEM water electrolyzer was developed and tested including freeze/thaw cycle testing. Because of technical difficulties with testing the WIPE water purification assembly that contains the IWP, the integrated IHOP subsystem composed of the WIPE water purification assembly and the HOPA containing the PEM water electrolyzer could not be tested during the option period. Thus, the PEM water electrolyzer did not achieve TRL 5 as planned.
