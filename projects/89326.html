Project Description

Autonomous systems are only capable of effective self-governing if they can reliably sense their own faults and respond to failures and uncertain environmental conditions. We propose to design a real-time, onboard runtime verification and system health management (SHM) framework called R2U2, to continuously monitor essential system components such as sensors, software, and hardware for detection and diagnosis of failures and violations of safety or performance rules during the mission of autonomous space systems, such as rovers, small satellites, or Unmanned Aerial Systems (UAS) flying in the skies of other planets. R2U2 is multi-platform and multi-architecture to address the requirements and capabilities of these embedded systems. R2U2 stands for Responsive, Realizable, Unobtrusive Unit; it is named after its three crucial properties that are currently absent from state-of-the-art SHM capabilities. Responsiveness means evaluating system health in real time, with provable timing and performance guarantees. Realizability involves being adaptable, extensible, and scalable to multiple platforms and architectures. Unobtrusiveness requires R2U2 to operate without altering crucial properties of the system: functionality, certifiability, timing, or tolerances for size, weight, power, telemetry bandwidth, software overhead. A full-scale version of R2U2, with options for hardware- and software-based implementations would have tremendous impact on the ability of autonomous space systems to perform real-time system-level reasoning about their health.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: RESPONSIVE - R2U2 will continuously monitor adherence to the safety requirements of the autonomous system in real time. Changes in the validity of monitored requirements must be detected within a tight and a priori known time bound. Responsive requirement monitoring enables responsive inputs required for real-time prognostics and intelligent decision-making, enabling mitigation of any problems encountered to avoid damage to the autonomous system and its environment. REALIZABLE - By designing both hardware and software TL observer implementations, R2U2 is adaptable to many different architectures and platforms, including operating on an FPGA, in software on the microcontroller, or in software on the flight computer. Different implementations enable scalability, extensibility, and specialization for reasoning with different software and sensor variables, with different timing guarantees. We will design R2U2 to easily integrate into existing design workflows and be usable by test-engineers without assuming in-depth knowledge of hardware design. Our design is reconfigurable so that TL observers can be updated without a lengthy re-compilation process and can be used both during testing of the autonomous system and after deployment. UNOBTRUSIVE - Our multi-architecture, multi-platform design enables effective runtime verification while respecting crucial properties of autonomous systems, including functionality (not change behavior), certifiability (provide timing guarantees and enable safety cases), timing (not interfere with timing guarantees), and tolerances (not exhaust constraints for size, weight, power, telemetry bandwidth, software overhead). Relevance to ECF Goals, Objectives, and Topics: R2U2 is an innovative, low-TRL framework for runtime verification and system health management of advanced space systems and space technologies [3]. Self-governance and observe-decide-act capabilities are only possible if autonomous systems can detect faults due to harsh environments or off-nominal conditions to enable performing tasks autonomously, without human intervention. Our new design for runtime observers is multi-platform and multi-architecture to address the requirements and capabilities of these embedded systems; their software runs on more limited embedded platforms and real-time checks must be able to analyze software and sensor values effectively without altering the timing and performance of the software or system or degrading operations, e.g. due to high overhead demanded by previous approaches to runtime verification.
