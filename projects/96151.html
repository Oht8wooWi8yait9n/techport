Multiphysics Integrated Modeling of Self-Reacting Friction Stir Welding

NASA TechPort Project ID: 96151
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

As an advanced variant of friction stir welding (FSW), self-reacting friction stir welding (SRFSW) utilizes a double-sided tool and self-contains the high forging force. This makes the process ideally suitable for joining large-scale aerospace components and provides promising solutions for in-space manufacturing. However, the current route for SRFSW process development is highly empirical. The goal of this project is to provide a high fidelity physics-based predictive model that describes process-microstructure-property relationships during SRFSW and enables virtual and rapid process optimization. More specifically, a fully microstructure-integrated thermo-mechanical process model will be developed within a hybrid solid-fluid framework, in which the material constitutive law explicitly considers dynamic recrystallization. Critical parameters will be obtained through precisely controlled thermo-mechanical material testing. Precipitates evolution and property models are further incorporated to build a holistic computational tool that inputs SRFSW tooling information, process conditions and outputs weld properties.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: In this project, high fidelity predictive models, focusing on the force controlled self-reacting friction stir welding (SRFSW) process for precipitate strengthened aluminum alloys are developed. The model integrates process-microstructure-property three levels of advanced physics-based models as a holistic computational tool, which inputs tool geometry and process parameters and outputs weld strength. The process model is to understand the weld formation mechanisms and predict the possible weld discontinuities. Two approaches have been adopted. First, a coupled thermo-mechanical computational fluid dynamics (CFD) model is developed, and the particle tracing methodology is applied to reveal the equivalent plastic strain distribution in the wake of the tool as a representation of the weld cross section geometry. At the tool-workpiece interface, shear stress boundary condition based on Coulomb-Tresca friction and velocity boundary condition are compared and evaluated, which reveals the significance of incorporating the shear layer in improving the numerical model reliability. The generation, transportation, and timescale sticking-sliding transition state of the shear layer directly affects the plasticized material flow field and accordingly weld formation. To account for the limitations of both shear stress and velocity boundary model, a novel pressure-dependent velocity boundary model based on Archard’s wear theory is derived by treating the formation of shear layer as the wear of the workpiece. Effects of tool rotating speed and welding speed on the weld cross section geometry can be robustly captured with this newly developed boundary model. At higher welding speed, the distance between the TMAZ boundary and the pin periphery at the advancing side is reduced, which corresponds to the experimental observations. This tendency could serve as a numerical criterion to predict void defect formation. In the second modeling approach, a 1D shear localization model describing the transient formation and evolution of shear layer in each tool revolution cycle is applied. The shear localization model successfully captured quality weld versus void defects formation under different weld conditions. Comparing the two approaches, the pressure-dependent velocity boundary condition accounts for the time-averaged transitioning from pure sliding to full sticking, whereas the shear localization model explicitly describes the transition from sliding to full sticking during each tool revolution. Based on the process model, a sequential precipitates microstructure evolution and yield strength property model is developed, where the thermal history extracted from the CFD modeling result is extracted as heat treatments input. The model successfully predicted the narrower stirring zone and thermal mechanically affected zone as indicated by the hardness distribution under higher welding speed. Bobbin tool FSW experiments are performed to validate the modeling results. The temperature profiles are measured by infrared camera and thermocouples and weld microstructure are examined. The large thickness of the workpiece sheet over 10mm leads to the susceptibility of tool fracture, and various designs of the tool are investigated. A new low-cost bobbin tool is designed and fabricated, which is capable of producing the typical SRFSW weld with the hourglass shape geometry and the tool life is significantly improved.
