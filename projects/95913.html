Development and Flight Test of Modernized Electronics for Mesosphere/Lower Thermosphere Fields Payload

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95913
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

For studies of the highly collisional mesosphere and lower thermosphere (50-120 km altitude), sensitive measurements of the electrodynamic environment require a small and compact measurement platform that provides minimal perturbation to the plasma environment. Since 1980, these measurements have been obtained with the “Maynard sphere” developed by Nelson Maynard at Goddard, but these spherical payloads have not been flown since 2001. Since there is an increased emphasis on measurements in this region, there is a strong need to modernize these compact electronics to provide high-resolution, low-noise measurements in a very highly miniaturized package, to support major upcoming rocket campaigns. The end goal of the product is to develop and fly a fully modernized and miniaturized measurement electronics to measure DC and AC electric fields, plasma density, and magnetic fields, in this miniaturized spherical payload.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

We anticipate that improving the Maynard sphere electronics will provide an advanced, mesospheric electric field measurement capability in a very small, spherical payload. This development effort will also enable electric field instruments to be applied to potential future Cubesat missions.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: As a result of this IRAD and a concurrent sounding rocket mission, the updated electronics were successfully designed, built, and flown in space from Wallops Island, VA in July 2022. The electronics met the specifications and provided high quality, 18-bit A/D vector electric and magnetic field data, as well as plasma density from the fixed Langmuir probe. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
