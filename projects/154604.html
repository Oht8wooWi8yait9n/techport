Compact and Rugged Mid-Infrared Multi-Gas Open Path Sensor

NASA TechPort Project ID: 154604
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

We propose a turn-key in-situ gas sensor that can reliably perform continuous measurements on airborne, ship-based, or other mobile platforms. Specifically, we emphasize that the proposed system is compact, lightweight, power efficient, and therefore can be mounted on small to mid-sized uncrewed aircraft systems. The sensor features an exposed beam path that allows ambient air circulation without active gas sampling. The proposed sensor is based on mid-infrared absorption spectroscopy using Pendars proprietary distributed feedback quantum cascade laser array (QCLA) technology. Our monolithically integrated chip-scale coherent source can withstand harsh operating conditions while offering superior wavelength and intensity stability compared to other broadband mid-infrared sources. Broadband spectral coverage (up to 500 cm-1) combined with high frequency resolution (~100 MHz) enable a multi-tasking sensor capable of detecting a multitude of gases including toxic industrial chemicals, chemical warfare agents, greenhouse gases, and volatile organic compounds. The laser source can rapidly tune over a large bandwidth to provide fast spectral measurements, enabling 100 Hz time response. The ability to deploy sensors capable of measuring multiple gases of interest on an uncrewed aircraft system or other small moving platforms contributes to lower cost measurements with increased spatial resolution. Our innovation is to fabricate 32 quantum cascade lasers on the same chip and use their combined output to target a wide range of gases through mid-infrared absorption spectroscopy. We can currently achieve up >500 cm-1 in frequency bandwidth by combining 4 such arrays while preserving the frequency precision of a single-mode laser. Comparing to a state-of-the-art broadly tunable external cavity quantum cascade lasers, our laser source module is 10-100x smaller in volume, 1000x faster in wavelength tuning speed (switch to any wavelength within the laser array in 10s of ns), and 100x lower in laser intensity noise. Moreover, the ruggedness of a monolithically integrated all solid-state laser source enables applications where the system undergoes significant vibration and is exposed to large temperature and pressure variabilities. In Phase I we focused on building a single array laser module and demonstrated a functional breadboard system with the promise of operating on mobile platforms. In Phase II, we will extend this platform to include 4 arrays to enable a 4-band QCLA integrated laser module. In addition to broadband laser source development, we will also construct a reconfigurable sensing platform, demonstrate its broadband and multi-gas sensing capabilities, and ruggedize and miniaturize the system for sensing on a small aircraft and UAVs separately. Two prototype sensors will be built—one will be delivered to NASA by the end of Phase II for integration and testing on a manned aircraft, and the second will serve as an in-house working prototype for experimentations aimed at developing a drone payload sensor.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The proposed sensor can be directly adopted for airborne missions such as on NASA’s Airborne Science aircraft fleet, uncrewed aircraft systems and balloons to provide lower cost atmospheric gas measurements. The CO2 sensing capability can be utilized to augment the space-based measurements of CO2 dry air mole fraction under the OCO-2 project. The TEMPO project can also benefit from additional ozone and NO2 measurements. We believe our proposed sensing solution can be adopted for chemical threat detection, which would be of interest to the Department of Homeland Security. The broadband sensing capability is also applicable to selective volatile organic compound detection in the air, which is of interest to markets targeting air quality measurements.
