Zero-Gravity Acoustic Mass-Gauge (ZAM)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 116382
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Propellant volume is gauged using acoustic resonances counting in a propellant tank based on spectral theory (Weyl's Law). This provides shape-invariant spectral characteristics enabling arbitrary shapes of the ullage/propellant, systematic control of accuracy, applicable to both conducting (MMH) and non-conducting propellants (Lox, LH2) and is non-intrusive. The technologies that enable the concept include sensors, actuators, electronics and data-processing and inference software. The concept of operations:

  • Tap outside tank wall (mechanical actuator)
  • This excites acoustic resonances in the liquid;
  • Resonances are detected (via accelerometers)
  • Information processed by electronics and peaks counting software,
  • Infer volume of liquid based on Weyl's law.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

  • Technology is based on shape-invariant characteristics of the spectra, which enables mass-gauging in zero-gravity conditions (where alternative approaches do not apply)
  • Minimal or no equipment is installed inside the tank
  • Applicable to both conducting (MMH) and non-conducting propellants (LOx, LH2)

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The method being developed under this task is based on excitation of the liquid component of a partially filled tank and measuring acoustic resonances including the development of actuators, processing electronics, and software. Subsequent lab testing on a range of sub-scale tanks, various working fluids, such as water and LN2 and artificial ullages was to be completed. The task funding stream was interrupted midway through the one year lifecycle and, as a consequence, failed to complete the component testing and breadboard validation. However, development of the acoustic resonance counting software to enable operation of the system was accomplished. In FY20, the task was funded as a GCD project entitled Spectral Mass Gauging (SMG) to continue the work.
