Project Description

Human space exploration requires advanced robotic systems working together with humans. Successful collaboration within human-robot teams requires each member of the team to be able to predict the actions of every other member. Robots that cannot predict the actions of their human teammates can cause dangerous collisions or otherwise hinder team task completion. To avoid such problems, robots acting around humans must be able to predict the immediate future and change their actions based on these predictions. I propose a hierarchical predictive planner for robots in human-robot teams, which provides interpretable reasoning about decisions to human operators. Using a learned attentional model for action selection, this predictive planner can visually display reasoning about the salient data used to make decisions. Initial development focuses on the Astrobee platform aboard the International Space Station, and on conditioned path planning to avoid collisions with astronauts and other moving obstacles. This work is directly applicable to Technology 4.4.3.2: Intent Recognition and Reaction.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This work contributes to the enhancement of robotic manipulation capabilities, which are crucial for NASA's space exploration and operational activities. It focuses on advancing world modeling and simulation technologies to improve interactions with both rigid and deformable objects in space and terrestrial environments. The research leverages state-of-the-art simulation methodologies that span various physical phenomena and applications while optimizing for computational efficiency. This research encompasses: The refinement of rigid body dynamics models to expedite the computation of derivatives and probability distributions, and hybridization with machine learning through residual physics learning. These improvements facilitate better parameter estimation and control mechanisms, leading to more accurate and reliable robotic manipulators. The adoption of soft object modeling through differentiable solid mechanics and machine learning-based predictive control. These techniques ensure precise parameter estimation under constraints and improve control foresight, essential for managing objects of varying properties. The utilization of GPU-accelerated parallel Discrete Element Methods (DEM) tailored to robotic sampling and excavation challenges on extraterrestrial surfaces. This method enhances the productivity and efficacy of robotic tasks in collecting and processing soil and rock samples. The validity of our methodologies is demonstrated through their application to real-world data and hardware, showcasing their practical benefits and potential contributions to NASA's mission success. This work not only advances robotic manipulation techniques but also supports the development of more autonomous, efficient, and safe robotic operations in space exploration endeavors.
