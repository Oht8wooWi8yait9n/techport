Precursor and neutral loss scans in a single ion trap and application to planetary exploration

NASA TechPort Project ID: 94045
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Precursor and neutral loss scans are general survey methods in tandem mass spectrometry that allow for the detection of molecules with similar functional groups. For example, a neutral loss scan can target hydroxylated compounds, and a precursor scan can target glycosylated molecules. Despite their utility, precursor and neutral loss scans are typically reserved for large, complex, and power-hungry multi-analyzer instruments (e.g. the triple quadrupole mass spectrometer). However, such instruments are not suitable for planetary missions due to their size and high power consumption as well as their requirement for relatively high vacuum conditions. Ion traps, on the other hand, are ideally suited for these missions due to their lower power consumption, versatility, high pressure tolerance, high sensitivity and selectivity, and capabilities for single analyzer tandem mass spectrometry. Fitting under TABS element 8.3.3 In-situ (other) instrumentation, we propose to implement precursor and neutral loss scans in a single quadrupole ion trap. Using dual orthogonal ac waveforms for precursor ion excitation and product ion ejection, we will develop methods for precursor and neutral loss scanning in both benchtop and miniature ion trap mass spectrometers. Both techniques require precise control of the trapping radiofrequency (rf) field as well as nonlinear ac frequency scanning that must be calculated using a program that will be written in-house. Precursor ion fragmentation will be controlled by mass-selectively exciting the entire precursor ion population successively at the same Mathieu q value, set by the excitation ac frequency. Simultaneously, as they are produced, product ions corresponding to a particular mass-to-charge ratio (which corresponds to a particular molecular structure) will be ejected from the trap and detected, preserving the mass information encoded in the excitation of the precursor ions. The performance of this technique will be evaluated on several fronts: precursor ion fragmentation efficiency, product ion ejection efficiency, sensitivity, limits of detection, spectral resolution, and unintended 'ghost peaks'. This technology has great benefits for planetary exploration. Survey scans will enable the discovery of molecules in different molecular classes using minimal sample and a single data-independent mass scan. This contrasts with current data-dependent methods which require more time, sample volume, several ion injections into the trap, or multiple ion traps. Furthermore, the developments proposed here would provide further evidence for the utility of single ion traps as small, power-efficient, and capable organic molecule analyzers in planetary exploration instrument suites. All of the known tandem mass spectrometry scans (precursor, neutral loss, and product ion scans) would be at the disposal of the ion trap, increasing its utility for analysis of small organic molecules. Such a set of scans would be beneficial for answering the fundamental questions about the origin of life, which may may be answered in part by detecting small organic molecules on comets and planets.

  • Closed Out: A key objective of NASA’s planetary sciences division is to detect and identify extraterrestrial biotic or prebiotic molecules in the solar system. Mars and the icy moons of Saturn and Jupiter are prime targets for study due to the abundance of water and/or carbon-containing materials on these planetary bodies. Miniature and portable mass spectrometers are key instruments for planetary exploration as they are particularly effective at molecular identification. These instruments have high sensitivity and selectivity and rapidly provide a wealth of molecular information, particularly if tandem mass spectrometry (MS/MS) is available. Although time-of-flight, sector, and quadrupole mass analyzers have all been employed in various NASA missions, the quadrupole ion trap (QIT) has recently become highly favored for future projects because of its modest vacuum requirements, high sensitivity, tolerance to electric field imperfections, and inherent single analyzer MS/MS capabilities. Even so, tandem mass spectrometry capabilities demonstrated on benchtop quadrupole ion traps – namely data-dependent MS/MS – are inefficient means of acquiring molecular data from a sample. Data-dependent MS/MS acquires dozens of scans from a sample in order to characterize the components therein. In each scan, only a single mass-to-charge ratio (which may be a single substance or a mixture of isobars) is fragmented and mass-analyzed, requiring many scans to accurately reconstruct the composition of the sample. For miniature ion traps with power, sample, vacuum, and size constraints, these scans are inefficient and inappropriate. This project aimed to develop more efficient means of analyzing planetary samples using a small, simple linear ion trap mass spectrometer. Specifically, we developed precursor ion scans, neutral loss scans, simultaneous and sequential combinations of all three main MS/MS scans (precursor, product, and neutral loss scans), and novel two-dimensional MS/MS scans not previously accessible with any prior scanning methodology on a single mass analyzer. Precursor ion scans and neutral loss scans were implemented on a benchtop linear ion trap and a homebuilt portable linear ion trap and were used to detect molecules with similar molecular functionalities (e.g. amino acids) using a single scan with minimal sample and power usage and little sample preparation. Two-dimensional MS/MS scans were also demonstrated; in these implementations the entire 2D MS/MS data domain (i.e. a full scan mass spectrum and a product ion mass spectrum for every precursor ion) was collected using a single scan. During the fellowship period, these scan methods were also conducted on a breadboard linear ion trap at NASA Goddard Space Flight Center. Precursor ion scans, neutral loss scans, and 2D MS/MS hold promise for inclusion on future planetary missions to Mars, Enceladus, and Titan in order to efficiently sort through the complex planetary samples in search of molecular indicators of life or life-like processes.
