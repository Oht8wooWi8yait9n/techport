Project Description

The overall purpose of this project is to study the development of a new articulated robotic arm aimed at improving the dexterity, modularity, strength, and torque output of current space manipulation systems. The key enabling technology for the proposed robotic arm is its novel joint, the Gear Bearing Drive (GBD). The GBD is a newly developed actuation concept based on NASA’s high-reduction planetary gearbox technology and new brushless “outrunner” motor technology. Due to its unique arrangement of planetary transmission and drive motor, the GBD is able to combine the motor, transmission and position sensing elements into a space that is volumetrically smaller than a human elbow joint. This combination produces ultra-compact actuators with incredible high torque output (more than 100 Nm), micro-precision accuracy, and strong and rugged structural integrity. These unique characteristics of the GBD facilitate the development of high payload-to-weight robots that are otherwise unattainable with traditional actuators. The proposed project, which will focus on the design and fabrication of a six degrees-of-freedom space manipulator using the Gear Bearing Drive joint technology, is divided into three main milestones. The first milestone involves conducting research and development on the GBD to improve Technology Readiness Level from proof-of-concept (TRL 1) into proven full-scale feasibility validation (TRL 6). The second milestone involves designing and developing the space manipulator according to a specific NASA application. The third milestone comprises testing and demonstrating the system capability in aerospace applications and publishing results. The proposed manipulator is expected to bring unprecedented strength and dexterity advantage and deployment modularity to space-based robotic manipulators. The research will shed light on new mechanisms of actuation and introduce a new class of compact powerful actuators with the potential to impact a number of space technology disciplines from space humanoids to powered exoskeletons and planetary rovers drive systems.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The research activities of this NSTRF project have revolved around developing advanced compact actuators for modern robotic and manipulation systems, specifically for planetary rovers and space manipulators. Today’s actuator requirements in both space and terrestrial applications is well known to have become more stringent in terms of torque density and efficiency, positioning resolution, torque capacity, torsional stiffness, weight and cost constraints. My research seeks to develop a new actuator hardware that reduces the size, complexity and cost of existing actuation systems down to one compact device that is numerically optimized for maximum torque density. The Gear Bearing Drive (GBD), see Fig. 1, is a recently developed robotic actuator that utilize a unique arrangement of brushless external-rotor motor technology and patented bearing-less transmission technology that integrates the gearing and bearing functions together and enable unlimited gear reduction from two fixed stages. As a result, this actuator can combine the motor and transmission and control elements into a space that is volumetrically smaller than a human elbow joint and capable of operating as an actuator providing high torques and as a joint providing bearing support in all directions. The technology enables the development of powerful low-cost drive systems with strong commercial prospects in robotics, manufacturing, aerospace, and toys, with a raw market potential of more than $50B.
