Development of High-Energy and Low-Cost Semi-Solid Sodium Batteries Operating at Extreme Cold Temperatures

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95620
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The extreme environments of many NASA planetary missions are far more demanding than those on Earth or in near-Earth applications. A typical case is the extreme cold environments on most of the outer planets, which poses a huge kinetic challenge to the electrochemical reactions at such low temperatures, making it rather difficult for the batteries to deliver the stored energy. The proposed research aims to develop groundbreaking high-energy and low-cost semi-solid sodium primary and rechargeable batteries that will operate at extreme cold temperatures (below -40 degrees Celsius) and exhibit high specific energy, excellent rate capability, and many-year lifetimes. These batteries will employ liquid with low freezing point or gas as cathode materials, which can tackle and alleviate the problem of slow kinetics in conventional solid-state cathodes when working at ultra-low temperatures. Such batteries will serve as affordable and sustainable power sources to meet broad NASA planetary science mission requirements for a variety of destinations.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This research can lead to dramatic low temperature performance improvements, serve as new affordable and sustainable power sources for NASA’s future planetary science missions, and enable lower cost and longer duration missions without the need for ancillary thermal systems.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This research aims to take a great leap beyond the current lithium (Li) battery technology by developing groundbreaking high-energy and low-cost semi-solid sodium (Na) primary and rechargeable batteries. Such batteries will operate at extreme cold temperatures and exhibit high specific energy, excellent rate capability, and long lifetime. The proposed batteries consist of low-cost Na metal as anode, high-capacity liquid or gas as cathode, and organic solvent with low freezing point as electrolyte, thus forming unique semi-solid Na−liquid (catholyte) or Na−gas (CO2) batteries. The employment of liquid or gas as cathode materials will alleviate the constraints of slow kinetics in conventional solid-state cathodes. Specifically, we have developed the following two types of semi-solid Na batteries: (1) A rechargeable Na−liquid battery composed of high-capacity and low-cost Na-phosphorus-sulfur complex (dissolved in organic solvent) as catholyte and Na metal as anode. (2) A primary/rechargeable Na−carbon dioxide (CO2) (gas) battery composed of CO2 gas as cathode and Na metal as anode. (Our proposed semi-solid Na batteries have demonstrated dramatic low-temperature performance improvements and extend the lower operating temperature range. Such batteries have the potential for a profound impact on various NASA’s future planetary science missions for a variety of destinations that require reliable, long-life energy storage in extreme low-temperature environments, such as outer planets, Mars, and small bodies. These batteries can serve as new affordable and sustainable alternative power sources for diverse NASA utilities to meet broad mission requirements, such as orbital, aerial, surface, and sample return missions. The Na-CO2 battery could be particularly attractive for Mars mission since ~96% of Mars atmosphere is CO2, which can be directly used as active materials for Na-CO2 batteries.
