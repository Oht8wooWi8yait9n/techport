Superconducting Parametric Amplifier for Ultra-low Noise DSN Ka-band Receiver, Year 1

Project Description

Use superconducting paramps to increase the DSN millimeter wave (Ka-band) receiver signal-to-noise, yielding a factor of two improvement in DSN data rates.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Traveling-wave parametric amplifiers based on superconducting kinetic inductance are a new technology which allows for wide-band operation + quantum limited noise. The key to making this type of device work is phase matching pump, signal and idler tones along the length of the device. The challenge of extending the technology to higher frequencies is to compensate for dispersion along the structure. The DSN sensitivity improvement / Ka-band data rate increase will benefit future planetary missions, prior to widespread use of optical com.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This task is extending the frequency range of a new type of amplifier, the superconducting Traveling-Wave Parametric Amplifier (TWPA), to the Ka-band where it could serve to improve receiver systems for the Deep Space Network (DSN). By lowering the receiver noise temperature relative to currently available technology, the TWPA will enable an increase in the data rate for downlinking data from a planetary mission to Earth. The key to making traveling-wave amplifiers, such as the TWPA, work is phase matching pump, signal and idler tones along the length of the device. The challenge of extending the technology to higher frequency is to compensate for dispersion on the TRL structure. Our work builds on TWPAs that have been developed for lower frequencies (< 10 GHz) that have demonstrated sensitivity consistent with the quantum limit for the noise of a phase preserving amplifier. In the first year of this effort, it was found that these devices could be fabricated with high yield, which is a huge improvement over the low-frequency CPW design. Up to 14 dB gain was measured at 30 GHz using 4-wave mixing.
