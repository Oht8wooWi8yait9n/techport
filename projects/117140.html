A Next-Generation Metasurface Polarimeter for Earth Science

NASA TechPort Project ID: 117140
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This IRAD seeks to supplement a project with external colleagues at Harvard University to develop a next-generation polarimeter based on metasurface technology. The GSFC team is tasked with performing exploratory modeling of the information content of circular polarization, to date ignored in Earth Science remote sensing applications, as well as developing observational concepts and strategies for space-based, airborne, and ground-based missions and deployments. This IRAD will enable expanded forward radiative transfer efforts and GSFC civil servant participation in the instrument concept formulation.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Maintaining a strong partnership with our external partners at Harvard on this metasurface technology is of strategic interest to GSFC. It positions the center with a small, low cost, low risk, next-generation polarimeter technology that is deployable in a wide range of observational settings, including spaceborne (cubesats/smallsats, etc.), airborne (traditional aircraft, UAVs), and ground-based (add-on to existing capabilities, e.g., AERONET, etc.). Furthermore, continuing forward model support also positions the center with state-of-the-art atmospheric radiative transfer capabilities that can be leveraged by other projects/missions including PACE and AOS.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Key accomplishments include significant enhancements to an efficient vector radiative transfer code and associated capabilities developed by Ping Yang at Texas A&amp;M University. Enhancements include computations of scattering properties for oriented particles along with updates to enable a more rigorous handling of the scattering properties within the RT code to enable characterization of the full polarization state (linear &#43; circular). Work began on polarimeter mounting designs to enable integration in an existing housing designed for the ER-2 and on an existing nadir port window for the G-III/G-V, both of which enable engineering flights of opportunity for polarimeter prototypes. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
