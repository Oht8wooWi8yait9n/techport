Micro-grid Definition and Interface Converter for Planetary Surface (MIPS)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 116399
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The overall goal of the Micro-grid definition and Interface converter for Planetary Surface (MIPS) project is to establish an evolvable, reconfigurable, reliable, and sustainable micro-grid design and power interface that supports the phased development and evolution of lunar surface activities. The first goal is to establish the overall need for electrical power to meet lunar surface operations as defined by the Agency and through the Lunar Surface Innovation Initiative (LSII). With the electrical power needs identified, a micro-grid can be designed to meet these needs and take into account the phased development and evolution of lunar surface activities. A micro-grid is a small-scale power grid that delivers electrical power to a network of users (loads) from power sources and/or energy storage devices via a power distribution system. For the purposes of this project, the lunar surface micro-grid is envisioned to include distributed generation and energy storage devices to provide electrical power to the loads. To be able to repurpose and reuse electrical power equipment designed specifically for early lunar science activities at later phases, a Universal Micro-grid Interface Converter (UMIC) will be designed, developed, tested, and demonstrated that can interface power sources loads or to the micro-grid. The second activity involves seeking inout from Exploration Systems Development Mission Directorates (ESDMD) and joint NASA-industry on modular power architectures. The final MIPS activity will design and deminstrate a Universal Modular Interface Converter (UMIC) which serves as a reference implementation of this power interface. The MIPS project will enable long-distance power sharing on the lunar surface.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Implementation of the Artemis lunar exploration campaign will require a strategy to achieve highly reliable sustainable power, enabling continuous power throughout the lunar day and night. An overall strategy for integrating, maintaining, and configuring multiple power assets with various loads has yet to be developed. The MIPS project looks to fill that gap by:
Focusing on an evolvable power micro-grid approach.
Developing a strategy for integrating and connecting dissimilar power sources and loads that arrive at the lunar surface over a period of years, can be robotically connected, and can function as a “synergistic-whole”.
Supporting a more focused and targeted investment strategy for technologies and advancements relevant to lunar surface operations.
Implementing a validated and verified standardized interface for lunar power/load technologies to reduce the overall integration effort and ensure power quality requirements are met.
There is currently no flight-proven electrical power management and distribution hardware capable of converting between the two standard DC space power voltages.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The MIPS project is a multi-part project intended to enable long-distance power transmission and universal access to electrical power on the lunar surface. To accomplish this, the MIPS project involved four major activities. The first activity involves documenting a timeline of power and energy storage needs on the lunar surface based upon NASA information available from Artemis and the Strategy and Architectures Office (SAO). The second activity involves Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) and Exploration Systems Development Mission Directorate (ESDMD) agreement to implement applicable standards and architectures, and coordinate with industry to adopt, adapt, or author appropriate standards and architectures that are universally acceptable to industry. The third activity involves identification of power needs on the lunar surface, modeling various grid attributes, and selecting a notional lunar grid layout that meets the identified lunar surface power and energy storage needs. The fourth activity is to design and demonstrate a Universal Modular Interface Converter (UMIC) which serves as a reference implementation of this power interface. The MIPS project has been successful in all the four (4) activities as indicated above and helped to create a government reference lunar surface power architecture that requires long-distance power transmission and includes a power grid. This government reference architecture has been used by other projects and referenced by the larger lunar community, including both industry and academia. The reference architecture assumes a total power demand of up to 150 kW and a total wire-distance up to 10 km. The project also performed an extensive trade study that considered DC, single-phase AC, and three-phase AC with frequencies from 60 Hz to 1000 Hz. The trade study found that increasing voltage had the largest impact on overall mass, regardless of power type. The study recommended AC power for the lunar power system, based on two factors— (1) AC power systems are more extensible than DC power systems, and (2) there are significant challenges involved in producing high voltage DC grid enabling interface converters. Based on the results of the trade study the MIPS project successfully designed and built the Universal Modular Interface Converter (UMIC). The UMIC is a bi-directional converter that converts between 120 VDC and 3000 VAC, three-phase, 1000 Hz and enables long-distance power transmission. The UMIC is designed to operate to the International Space Power System Interoperability Standard (ISPSIS), which means all loads and sources that are designed to the ISPSIS standard can interface with the UMIC. The MIPS project successfully demonstrated long-distance transmission between two UMICs in a lab. The demonstration consisted of a UMIC converting from 120 VDC to the transmission voltage, a cable of 100 m, and a secondary UMIC converting from the transmission voltage to 120 VDC. The MIPS team has also worked on tech transfer and tech infusion of this technology with industry partners and have successfully supplied licenses to one company and Space Act Agreements (SAA) with others.
