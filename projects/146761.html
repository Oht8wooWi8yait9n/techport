Project Description

We propose to demonstrate the new laser capabilities required for spectroscopic infrared reflectance measurement for high sensitivity mapping of water ice as well as for high-resolution topographic mapping.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Upgrade an existing laser that has been developed for the purpose of spectroscopic infrared reflectance measurement to allow for the additional requirement of high-resolution topographic mapping. This effort is primarily a risk reduction of the laser's critical component to achieve the needed performance.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This project was successful at completed the validation testing necessary to achieve TRL 6 for the laser optical design. Two brass-board level laser were completed and put through vibrational and TVAC tested with no signs of degradation in laser performance. A limited life test was also completed that achieved over 2 billion laser shots. The life test also incorporated periods of performance in which the laser repetition rate was increased by a factor 2.5X. These was also discrete increases in laser output energy throughout the life test to 2X required energy. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies. Goddard Space Flight Center’s IRAD program is managed under its Office of Chief Technologist. Activities develop concepts, reduce technology risk, and advance human capital and technological capabilities. Goddard’s IRAD program is highly competitive, opportunity-driven, and 100% strategically aligned with NASA's and Goddard's strategic priorities. A significant portion of the program is focused on Early Stage Innovations for high-risk, strategically aligned, potential high-payoff technologies that are longer-term or lower TRL.
