Project Description

This project aims to reduce cost and improve the performance of space systems development by leveraging modern computational technologies, such as cloud-based Computer Aided Engineering (CAE) tools and AI-enabled automation. Focusing on mechanical and opto-mechanical systems, the proposal will conduct market research, develop and test new tools, and address cultural and technical barriers to adoption. By integrating Large Language Models (LLMs) like ChatGPT, engineers will efficiently automate workflows and access critical knowledge. Building on the success of the Evolved Structures project, this 3-year effort targets incremental infusion of mature technologies while supporting the development of emerging ones, ultimately enabling human-AI collaboration for space systems development.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The strategic rationale behind this project lies in its overarching mission: advancing the digital engineering capabilities at NASA GSFC, enabling it to maintain a competitive edge in mission development. The proposed initiative addresses this mandate through an interconnected series of objectives: toolchain modernization, integrating LLMs with CAE tools, efficient knowledge transfer through LLMs, and adherence to Information Technology policies. The projected outcomes have substantial potential for creating transformative benefits for NASA in terms of performance, cost, and knowledge management.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This project successfully advanced Digital Engineering (DE) at Goddard and led to major advancements at the center, especially in Artificial Intelligence (AI). We compiled, evaluated, and documented a large database of DE and AI tools. Partnerships with industry led to impressive proof-of-concept demonstrations of the use of AI technology to radically accelerate space system development. This project also led to the development of in-house AI tools such as ChatGSFC that keep Goddard on the cutting edge of secure and responsible AI use. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
