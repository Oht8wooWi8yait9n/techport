Vlasov Simulation Methods for Air Breathing Electric Propulsion

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186804

Project Description

Atmosphere-breathing electric propulsion (ABEP) has gathered attention due to its potential to avoid onboard propellant storage. Maturation of ABEP would allow orbital maneuvering, stationkeeping, deorbiting, and active debris removal without the usual high mass and energy budgets that would be required otherwise. At lower orbits, some applications include surveillance and Earth observation, which are currently of high interest to government and commercial space communities. Current ABEP involves major architectural modifications, and is limited to very low Earth orbit (VLEO) due to propellant density requirements, and so are not well suited to broader applications. In this context, an external magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) propulsion system is proposed. It is a low-footprint alternative that avoids major spacecraft redesign by adopting an external patch configuration, with an exterior magnet and electrodes providing the required electromagnetic fields. This technology operates by generating orthogonal electric and magnetic fields in ambient plasma, generating a Lorentz force acting on the plasma. The counterforce then generates thrust in a non-invasive, external manner. Preliminary work has found that this technology is competitive against existing ABEP technologies, and can be used at much higher altitudes. To accomplish this, a fully-coupled kinetic plasma solver, OpenVlasov6, has been developed, and has been used for MHD propulsion simulation. Three main objectives will be pursued. Objective 1 is to optimize and publish current trade study results and applications for low Earth orbit (LEO) MHD propulsion. Objective 2 focuses on using the OpenVlasov6 simulation architecture for simulation and optimization of other ABEP systems, with comparisons to published ABEP architectures. Objective 3 will then apply the results of Objectives 1 and 2 to motivate the development and performance optimization of additional plasma propulsion simulation techniques for use in the LEO environment and in collisionless or weakly collisional plasmas. These simulations will enable further investigation into conventional ABEP and MHD propulsion, with additional applications in fusion and other electric propulsion techniques.
