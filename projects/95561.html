Non-Contact Eddy Current Manipulation in Microgravity Environments

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95561
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Interactions between space objects during proximity operations introduce damage and contamination risks. Non-contact interactions add robustness to changes in the relative positioning of space objects. Eddy currents are a promising non-contact method for relative positioning in microgravity. A spacecraft containing a time-varying magnetic field source induces eddy currents in and corresponding forces and torques on a conducting space object. These field sources may use in situ power resources and interact with resident objects, which reduce propellant and interfacing requirements, respectively. This project will develop eddy current actuator technology for manipulation (TA 4.3.1) and mobility (TA 4.2.4) in microgravity environments. My objectives are to (1) define magnetic field source requirements (e.g., magnetic field strength per unit mass and current) to identify actuation opportunities at multiple spheres of influence, (2) identify magnetic field source configurations for representative actuation tasks, and (3) develop and evaluate closed-loop control systems for the selected actuation tasks. The objectives will expand analysis of microgravity eddy current manipulation to include abstraction of analytical and numerical models to inform both electromagnet and permanent magnet sources. The models and verifying experiments will inform simulations for novel applications such as mobility of spacecraft inspection vehicles. Researchers have not yet demonstrated eddy current manipulation or mobility in microgravity environments. By developing eddy current actuation capabilities, I will inform opportunities to test the technology beyond TRL 3.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Propellant-free technologies support NASA objectives for sustained space presence. Free-flyers and end effectors with propellant-free actuators may support the routine inspection and servicing required for microgravity infrastructure such as the International Space Station, proposed Lunar Gateway, and subsequent Mars-orbiting infrastructure. Propellant-free solutions facilitate sustained operation during tasks with long durations and eliminate risk of plume impingement. Many clients have conductive, non-ferromagnetic aluminum surfaces. These surfaces facilitate use of non-contact, propellant-free eddy-current actuators. These actuators use time-varying magnetic fields to induce opposing currents in the aluminum surfaces. The interaction between the magnetic field source and opposing currents provide force components normal and tangential to the surfaces. This grant developed eddy-current technologies for single and multi-degree-of-freedom (DOF) applications. AC coils provide repulsion, and rotating permanent magnets provide a force component tangential to a conductive surface. Finite element and analytical actuator models described the sphere of influence (i.e., millimeters to a couple centimeters) for these actuators at small-satellite size, mass, and power. Emerging space technologies such as superconducting coils may facilitate actuation at larger separations, lower power, and lower mass; the contributed models can facilitate this extension. The actuator models also informed design of, actuator allocation for, and simulation of a 6-degree-of-freedom (DOF) actuator array. This array can provide relative mobility when attached to a free-flyer and relative manipulation of a client when attached to an end effector. The grant supported a hardware demonstration of the actuator array in 3-DOF subsets to raise the technology readiness level (TRL) to late TRL 3/early TRL 4. This grant also contributed a model and demonstration of a translation-based eddy-current actuator. The actuator provides one-time attraction and repulsion as well as repulsion with restoration. This work contributes a model for a finite-thickness client and finite trajectory length, which refine assumptions from earlier work. The combined contributions advance propellant-free relative mobility and manipulation technologies to 6 DOFs for planar clients. By including an electrostatic actuator, the 6-DOF array relaxes earlier requirements for target curvature for attraction. The array design and models support extensions to more complex geometries provided that the clients have sufficiently high conductivity and allow for small separations. This fellowship supports the transition of results to NASA and the public through contributions to a technical memorandum [4]; three conference papers [1]–[3]; and a poster [5] with journal publications under review and in preparation.
