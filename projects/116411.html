Project Description

​The Lunar Surface Innovation Initiative (LSII) Dust Mitigation effort provides groundwork for a long-term dust mitigation strategy. The effort also seeks to collect information on the state-of-the-art solutions that currently exist, develop low Technology Readiness Level (TRL) technologies, and integrate mature technologies into systems that require dust mitigation solution. In addition, Mars is covered with a layer of dust that has been homogenized by global dust storms. Dust,levi​ated by these storms aswell as by the frequent dust devils, is the dominant weather phenomenon on Mars. NASA’s Mars exploration rovers have shown that atmospheric dust falling on solar panels can decrease their efficiency to the point of rendering the rover unusable. Dust covering the surface of the moon is expected to be electrostatically charged due to the solar wind, cosmic rays, and the solar radiation itself through the photoelectriceffect. Electrostatically charged dust has a large tendency to adhere to surfaces. The Apollo missions to the moon showed that lunar dust adhesion can hinder manned and unmanned exploration activities. The Electrodynamic Dust Shield (EDS) which can lift, transport and remove particles from surfaces with no moving parts, will be demonstrated for the first time on the lunar surface. The EDS is an active dust mitigation technology that uses electric fields to move dust from surfaces and to prevent dust accumulation on surfaces. This technology, developed in the Electrostatics and Surface PhysicsLaboratory at NASA Kennedy Space Center, will show the feasibility of self-cleaning glass and thermal radiator surfaces. In addition to dust removal, the EDS will apply lunar dust to these surfaces using the new reduster technology which will lift and transport dust from the lunar surface and transport it to the desired location without moving parts or gasses. The EDS will be released from a fifth leg of the lander and positioned directly onto the lunar surface to maximize dust contact. High resolution images will determine the dust removal efficiency of the EDS on the lunar surface.​

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

​Technology is needed to move dust and prevent dust accumulation on surfaces in space.  The Electrodynamic Dust Shield (EDS) sensor provides the necessary dust removal capability for this environment for the commercial Lunar Payload Services and upcoming Artemis missions. Utilizing this technology will also benefit the ISS program as well as any Lunar, Martian, or Gateway platforms as well.  ​Enable technology developers to raise the TRL of several existing dust mitigation solutions through payload testing on near term Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) missions.Provide technology developers with opportunities for direct infusion into human-rated designs and long-term architecture plans.Provide proof of concept of technology for later infusion to larger scale devices such as solararrays, optical systems, spectrometers, viewports, thermal radiators, batteries, power systems, rovers, as well as on fabrics for human rated spacesuits.​Incorporate any lessons learned from the demonstration back into the technology for further refinement into extraterrestrial applications.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The need for active dust mitigation has never been greater for NASA as it is today with the Artemis Program returning astronauts to the lunar surface for the first time in over 50 years. Previous efforts to remove dust on the moon was performed by Apollo astronauts using brushes which proved to be ineffective. The Electrodynamic Dust Shield (EDS) is NASA’s leading active dust removal technology. The Electrostatics and Surface Physics Laboratory at Kennedy Space Center has been developing and testing EDS technology for Moon and Mars applications since 2002 in collaboration with several universities to apply this technology for space applications, specifically to remove dust from solar panels. The EDS technology has expanded past the need for self-cleaning solar panels; the current state of the EDS has transformed into self-cleaning camera lenses, thermal radiators, fabrics and textiles, flooring, gaskets/seals, and more. However, with all the laboratory and flight testing performed using the EDS technology over the years, it was unproven in the lunar environment with lunar soil. The Commercial Lunar Payload System (CLPS) Program selected EDS as one of the 10 payloads for the CLPS-19D flight program. The overall goal of the EDS payload was to show this technology was effective for dust removal in situ. In particular, the goals were to: (1) Demonstrate effective lunar dust removal from the surface of a thermal radiator and a glass surface while on the lunar surface. These two EDS demonstrators are called the Footpad EDS. (2) Collect images of dust on the Footpad EDS before activation, dust movement during activation, and after dust removal. Secondary goals and objectives included: (1) Successful operations of the Camera EDS used to keep the camera clean. (2) Successful demonstrations of the Reduster EDSs, which added dust to the Footpad EDS should they not be coated upon lunar landing. (3) Successful operation of the high-voltage power supply (HVPS) to drive each EDS. The demonstration of the EDS on the lunar surface addressed STMD Active Dust Solutions Gaps 1047 and 521. The Key Performance Parameter for this project was the EDS cleaning efficiency of a glass surface. The experiment also included a simulated radiator surface. This was accomplished by monitoring the performance of a glass or lens EDS during lunar operations. These were prototypes that were successfully demonstrated and raised the Technology Readiness Level (TRL) from a 5 to a 7. A second EDS comprised of thermal radiator materials was also tested. The TRL of this EDS was also raised to a 7. The glass EDS was heavily soiled during the landing as a result of the plume impingement from the engines. The overall clearing efficiency of the lens was estimated to be 96.6% during this lunar mission. For the Camera EDS, the actual system was flight qualified and tested, but never demonstrated on the lunar surface until now raising the TRL to an 7. The Reduster system was successfully operated on the lunar surface, with the exit TRL a 7. For the HVPS system, it was successfully demonstrated on the lunar surface with the exit TRL also a 7.
