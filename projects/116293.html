Project Description

Maxar developed and demonstrated a bang-bang pressure regulator system (BBPRS) with high propellant flow rate capability for high power Hall thruster propulsion systems. The developed BBPRS was tested with NASA GRC’s 12.5 kW HERMeS Hall thruster at NASA GRC vacuum facility 6. MAXAR developed the BBPRS and performed benchtop tests at MAXAR facilities. The BBPRS was then integrated with a NASA GRC HERMeS thruster to demonstrate its capability in supplying regulated flow to the thruster.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

- Increase system robustness by removing the mechanical pressure regulator in the propellant management system

- Lower cost than existing SOA

- Has space flight heritage (@ lower flow rates)

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: On-going development of next generation electric propulsion (EP) systems drives a need for low cost, scalable, and flexible propellant pressure regulators. EP systems need low pressure at the thrusters, requiring an upstream pressure regulation system. Geostationary Earth Orbit (GEO) satellites typically use a mechanical regulator to perform this role; however, an active bang-bang pressure regulation system (BBPRS) is a single, lower-cost solution for a wide range of flow rates and thruster power levels. Partnered with NASA, Maxar developed and built a BBPRS with high propellant flow rate capability for high power Hall thruster propulsion systems to meet this need. This configurable BBPRS prototype was tested in NASA GRC’s Vacuum Facility-6 (VF-6) over the full range of power levels of a Test Demonstration Unit (TDU) Hall thruster up to 12.5 kW. Tests were successfully performed with both xenon and krypton. Propellant, flow restriction, plenum volume, pressure setpoints, heater power, and thruster power were varied to collect data for Maxar to develop a model to simulate future designs. Maxar intends to continue development of the BBPRS for use across future EP product lines.
