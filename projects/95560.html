Photolithographically Textured Silicon Lenses for Millimeter and Submillimeter Wavelengths

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95560
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

My proposed work will focus on developing a gradient-index silicon lens with broad-band antireflection (AR) achieved with deep reactive ion etching (DRIE) for use in future millimeter and submillimeter astronomy missions. The primary science case for such lenses is the observation of primordial gravitational waves from inflation, as imprinted on the B-mode polarization pattern of the cosmic microwave background (CMB). Since the observed B-mode pattern is contaminated by foregrounds from dust and synchrotron emission, this measurement will require broad spectral coverage to separate out the foregrounds. As outlined in NASA's 2017 Physics of the Cosmos (PCOS) Annual Technology report, there is a need for optics with antireflection over a broad spectral band; these will allow a wide frequency range to be imaged in a single focal plane, enabling compact designs for future space-based missions. To realize such optics, the proposed work will design structures in silicon to lower its effective index of refraction using a novel DRIE technique that combines multi-layer etching and wafer bonding. This technique allows for varying the index both radially, to produce focusing, and axially, to implement broad-band antireflection. Our group has already produced and tested optical flats with two-layer coatings; four-layer coatings are currently being developed. The main emphasis of my work will be to incorporate a radial index gradient in a 15-cm diameter optic, but I will also need to expand to a 5-layer AR coating to provide sufficient bandwidth (75-300 GHz) for dust foreground subtraction to address the PCOS technology gap. This approach can scale to even broader bandwidths, making the proposed work a significant step towards extending to lower frequencies to enable synchrotron foreground removal.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The objective of this work has been to develop broadband antireflective (AR) coatings for silicon optics, including vacuum windows and lenses. Silicon optics, which are an important part of optical designs for both space-based and ground-based millimeter and submillimeter observatories, are often limited by the bandwidth and performance of AR coatings. Thus, developing improved AR coatings can significantly broaden the space of possible focal plane designs, allowing a reduction in the size and weight of payloads for, e.g., space-based cosmic microwave background (CMB) observatories. This work has made progress towards this goal by designing a six-layer AR coating based on etching microscopic sub-wavelength structures into the surface of the optics using deep reactive ion etching (DRIE). The coating’s performance depends on the exact shape and dimensions of these microstructures. To maximize the performance, we have tuned the structures’ dimensions by iteratively modeling and simulating using the high-frequency electromagnetic simulation software HFSS. The optics can be fabricated using a combination of multilayer etching and bonding of silicon wafers. In addition, to mitigate potential fabrication challenges due to the limitations of the material and of DRIE, we have produced multiple variations of the design to trade off performance for physical robustness and ease of fabrication. We optimized these coatings for a silicon vacuum window, and the design can be adapted to a gradient-index (GRIN) lens. The final designs are tuned to limit reflections to around -20 dB (1%) for the frequency range of 80-420 GHz, which is ideal for galaxy cluster observations. The designs can also be rescaled to the 75-300 GHz range for next-generation CMB-based cosmic inflation probes.
