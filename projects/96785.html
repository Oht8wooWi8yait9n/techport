HyperMapping with Controlled Optical Pointing for Decadal Survey Mission Pathfinder Activities

NASA TechPort Project ID: 96785
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This IRAD project will: i) integrate a suite of optical instruments into NASA’s Cessna 206 aircraft; ii) install and test a pointing system for optical instruments to allow for multiple control options; and, iii) test fly the instruments to understand the operational environment of the aircraft and obtain observations to support 610 science Pathfinder tasks. The effort will demonstrate (three 2-hour flights) a remote sensing instrument system for obtaining high spatial (< 1 m) visible and infrared imagery co-incident with hyperspectral remote sensing reflectance (Rrs) and Structure from Motion (SfM) topography to map the physical and ecosystem features of Wallops Island and adjoining coastal ocean, bays and marshes. The Rrs maps will be input data for testing and validating several product algorithms, for developing dynamic control software for the pointing systems, and for Pathfinder activities of the Decadal Survey Surface Biology and Geology (SBG) and Surface Topography and Vegetation (SVG) Mission.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Development of an active optical pointing instrument to obtain finer resolution hyper spectral observations of remotely sensed observations that would benefit from these higher resolution spectra.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The HYPPOS project's FY22 effort focused on three aspects of the instrumentation's capabilities to move from a passive system to one that is actively driven with an Artificial Intelligence (AI)-powered instrument package. A software package is being developed to post-process the full HYPPOS observations and output calibrated observations in Google Earth accessible xml formats. An additional software element was developed to ingest the various instrument observations without the need to post-process them using standard commercial software supplied by the instrument vendors. A centralized IT system is under development that will use multiple embedded AI components (Google TPU and NVDIA Coral GPU boards) to process the observations and inform the decision-control software for guiding the optical pointing component of the sub-nanometer spectrometer. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
