Project Description

This project supports the wider Intelligent Extensible Mission Architecture (IEMA) by providing the software and hardware platforms required to demonstrate artificial intelligence across heterogeneous, extensible constellations. There are three critical elements: a simulation platform in which to prove algorithms, an Unmanned Aerial System (UAS) platform to accomplish a snow hydrology science mission, and a credible path to spaceflight through a hybrid constellation demonstration. We will leverage the NASA Operational Simulator for Small Satellites (NOS3) and the On-board Artificial Intelligence Research (OnAIR) Platform to achieve these goals. Demonstrations will progress from entirely simulated, a field UAS campaign, to a hybrid constellation that incorporates distributed simulated assets and physical UASes.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The development of autonomous, ad-hoc, heterogenous constellations has the potential to enable new Science missions, increase the useful life of assets, and improve the return on investment compared to traditional, monolithic, one-off missions. Autonomy is required for assets to react to transient events, overcome bandwidth limitations, and deal with novel situations. Extensibility and heterogeneity is necessary to increase the longevity of assets so that they continue to be of use beyond their initial goals and adapt to new conditions and resources.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The team supported an accelerated drone campaign timeline (moved from February of 2026 to August of 2025) and a new flight opportunity on a CubeSat experiment; these changes necessitated removing the originally planned simulation activities. The drone campaign successfully demonstrated science data collection, autonomous anomaly detection, and task auctioning in the field. The team delivered a CubeSat experiment for coordinated image anomaly detection in under four months; the flight is still pending. Detailed results are reported in "OnAIR: Applications of The NASA On-Board Artificial Intelligence Research Platform" in AAAI and "Standards and Schematics for Intelligent Extensible Mission Architectures in Space" presented at SmallSat 2025. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies. Goddard Space Flight Center’s IRAD program is managed under its Office of Chief Technologist. Activities develop concepts, reduce technology risk, and advance human capital and technological capabilities. Goddard’s IRAD program is highly competitive, opportunity-driven, and 100% strategically aligned with NASA's and Goddard's strategic priorities. A significant portion of the program is focused on Early Stage Innovations for high-risk, strategically aligned, potential high-payoff technologies that are longer-term or lower TRL.
