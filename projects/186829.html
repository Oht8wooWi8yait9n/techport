Adaptive Autonomy for Lunar Regolith Construction through Graph-Based Planning and Physics-Informed Surrogate Modeling

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186829
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

NASA's Artemis program requires autonomous construction of lunar infrastructure including landing pads, protective berms, and roads using in-situ regolith. The primary objective of this research proposal is to enable robotic rovers to independently plan and execute complex earthmoving operations on the Moon, adapting in real-time to the unpredictable behavior of regolith materials. While work has been done on the development of autonomous systems for grading and construction, there is a gap in complex compute-efficient and adaptive regolith construction algorithms. To date, a total of ten kilograms of lunar regolith has been excavated by NASA. The estimated thousands of kilograms of lunar regolith required to be moved for full-scale In-Situ Resource Utilization highlights the urgency of developing autonomous systems that can handle moving these quantities of regolith, where alternative methods such as human teleoperation may be cost-prohibitive or infeasible due to delay in communication. The proposed framework combines two primary contributions: a graph-based construction planning algorithm that determines optimal deposition sequences while maintaining geotechnical stability and rover mobility constraints, and a physics-informed neural network surrogate model that predicts regolith deposition behavior during construction planning. The graph-based planner adapts NASA's ARMADAS modular assembly logic to the stochastic domain of regolith construction. A directed acyclic graph composed of voxels represents the desired regolith feature to be constructed, where the voxel structure is developed from the difference between the desired and initial topography. A multi-objective optimization function in the planning algorithm minimizes topographic error, energy expense, and operation time while enforcing "no-blocking" constraints to ensure rover accessibility. A signed distance function-based graph neural network surrogate is used for physics-informed planning by predicting regolith spillover, settling patterns, and compaction. This approach offers a potential 100x computational runtime advantage over discrete element methods, while maintaining high accuracy. Secondary contributions include developing a high-fidelity simulation environment using IsaacSim with Genesis physics backend, and implementing a ROS2-based autonomy stack with perception, localization, path planning, and control interfaces. The research will be validated using the ISRU Pilot Excavator (IPEx) rover architecture in simulation and will leverage the Colorado School of Mines' 120-square-meter lunar surface simulator. This research will build upon existing work in IsaacSim at the Robotics Space Exploration Lab at the Colorado School of Mines. The Graph-Based Construction Planning Algorithm enables an autonomous planning and action framework for lunar regolith construction, addressing Shortfall 1532 (Priority #35: Autonomous Planning and Decision Support), Shortfall 662 (Priority #102: Robotic Regolith Manipulation and Site Preparation), Shortfall 1400 (Priority #159: On-Surface Robotic Assembly of Horizontal Structures), and Shortfall 666 (Priority #160: On-Surface ISRU-Based Construction of Horizontal Structures). This algorithm, coupled with the Physics-Informed Regolith Deposition Surrogate Model, allows the rover to adapt to unexpected and dynamic changes in regolith conditions, addressing Shortfall 680 (Priority #85: Robust Robotic Intelligence for High-Tempo Autonomous Operations in Dynamic Mission Conditions). With the autonomous construction of regolith features, this work supports NASA's strategic objectives for sustained lunar presence while also developing technologies applicable to terrestrial construction.
