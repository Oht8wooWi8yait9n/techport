Lithium Niobate Based Photonic Integrated Circuits for Reconfigurable Sensing and Signal Processing

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95551
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

Microwave radiometry has been an essential instrument in characterizing atmospheric absorption and thermal emission for a wide range of Earth and Planetary applications. A state of the art microwave radiometry system typically covers only a few bands in a limited frequency range. Thus, it remains challenging to observe the radiometric characteristics with a single integrated solution over 2~200 GHz where both gaseous absorption and thermal emission feature multiple peaks. The proposed research seeks to develop photonic integrated circuits (PIC) for widely tunable and highly sensitive microwave and millimeter-wave radiometry. Capabilities surpassing state of the art will be demonstrated by exploiting the pronounced photonic, electro-optic and piezoelectric properties of the ion-sliced lithium niobate thin film platform for signal modulation, tunable filtering, and detection. The outcome of this research will enable a new chip-scale solution for passive microwave radiometry over a wide frequency range of 200 GHz and with fine spectral selectivity.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This research seeks to develop photonic integrated circuits (PIC) for widely tunable and high-sensitivity microwave and millimeter-wave radiometry. Capabilities surpassing state of the art are demonstrated by exploiting the pronounced photonic, electro-optic, and piezoelectric properties of the ion-sliced lithium niobate thin film platform for modulation and tunable filtering. The proposed platform is formed by three main subsystems: (i) a wideband electro-optic modulator that up-converts the micro and millimeter-wave signals of interest by modulating an optical carrier; (ii) a voltage-tunable photonic filter that adaptively selects the millimeter-wave sideband of the carrier; and (iii) a resonant optomechanical detector that performs the power measurement of the spectrally chosen signals. The proposed research enables a full solution for passive microwave radiometry over a wide frequency range (2-200 GHz) while maintaining the spectral selectivity (BW=0.1 GHz). The subsystems will see applications in Earth and Planetary Science as well as limb sounders and auroral imagers in Heliophysics. A fundamental building block of PICs is the optical modulator, which operates based on the electro-optical properties of a particular material. Lithium niobate stands out as the material possessing the strongest linear electro-optical effect. If utilized properly, this strong effect can lead to Mach-Zehnder modulators that provide superior modulation performance, though at the penalty of a substantial footprint on the photonic die. Alternatively, resonant microring modulators can occupy a minimal footprint. X-cut and Y-cut microring modulators are easy to fabricate using side electrode because the necessary electric field is in-plane but suffers from orientation limitations because of this need to align the field to the in-plane extraordinary axis of the crystal. Z-cut implementations of microring modulators have suffered from a weakly induced DC field inside the lithium niobate waveguide because of sub-optimal geometries such as a metal electrode beneath a thick buried oxide layer for realizing a vertical electric field. Though it might appear impossible to bring together the best of both X-cut and Z-cut worlds (i.e., ease of fabrication, strong modulation effects, no constraint on in-plane orientation, and high modulation speed), we believe we have cracked the problem. We have pushed the boundaries of state of the art for the integrated lithium niobate microring modulators. In addition, we also think that we can extend our work to X-cut and Y-cut lithium niobate to investigate the fundamental limitations.
