Low mass, low power, non-mechanical excavation of gypsum and other evaporites for water production on Mars

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95513
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Several potential water sources have been identified on Mars below 50 degree latitude, including buried glacial ice and hydrated minerals such as smectite clays and gypsum. Of the minerals, gypsum has been identified as the most efficient source of water due to the low water release temperature at 150 C and the high water content; 20% water by mass. The objective is to demonstrate an innovative process for extraction of water from hard extraterrestrial soils and gypsum in particular. The process involves ‘dissaggregating’ material using a water jet to form a slurry, and pumping the slurry to a processing system for separation and water extraction. This innovative process eliminates the hardest problem in mining: comminution, which involves heavy equipment, significant energies, forces, and tooling impractical for sustained extraterrestrial ISRU. The proposed process combines the steps of excavation and extraction within a simple, self-contained system of minimal mechanical complexity; resulting in a durable system with low mass and low energy requirements. The process takes advantage of mineralogy with benign processing temperatures that are generally less than 200 degrees C, and it is adaptable to a wide range of geomechanical properties, deposit sources and contaminants. The project includes modeling fluid and heat transfer in the gypsum rock, experimental studies of water-gypsum disaggregation, and development of select subsystems for in-situ disaggregation, slurry transportation, and sealing. Subsystems will be tested in a simulated Mars environment followed by system-level testing also in relevant Martian environment conditions. This promising approach could potentially be used for other hydrated minerals and even ice-cemented soils in other locations such as the Moon.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The objective of this Early Stage Innovation (ESI) research project was to demonstrate an innovative process for extraction of water from hard extraterrestrial soils. The process involves ‘disaggregating’ material with a water jet to form slurry, and pumping the slurry into water extraction system where the liquid water and the crystal bound water in the rock is extracted and collected for storage. This innovation eliminates the hardest problem in mining: comminution, which involves heavy equipment, significant energies, forces, and tooling impractical for sustained extraterrestrial ISRU. At the start of the research technology was TRL 1-2 for the processes for excavation of minerals and extraction of water at 0.8 kg/hr. We have progressed all subsystems consisting of bootstrapping, sealing, disaggregation, slurry suction, separation, calcination to TRL-4 during this ESI project. Based on the research, modeling, experimentation and subsytem prototype testing we conclude that the method of extracting water from hard rock gypsum using a water jet system and calcination has no principal showstoppers. If an accessible site consisting of rock gypsum can be found on Mars, this could be a contender for water production from poly-hydrated suphates. It is recommended that a more detailed trade study should be performed comparing the alternative methods for mining/extracting water. In addition, further fine-tuning of the developed prototypes and techniques would allow for better power estimates and better production performance. At the conclusion of this project, we have advanced the key stages of this innovative process, namely hydraulic disaggregation, slurry transport, separation, and sealing to TRL 3-4.
