Monolithic Compliant Space Mechanisms Design Strategies

NASA TechPort Project ID: 91551
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

New additive manufacturing technologies such as Direct Metal Laser Sintering and Electron Beam Melting now allow 3D printing of complex geometries. These processes build up a part layer by layer, instead of removing material from a large block as in traditional machining. Such processes are ideal for low quantity production. Using these technologies, assemblies can be simplified to one part in a monolithic (made from a single solid piece) design. Advantages of monolithic designs include simplicity, robustness, and lower cost. Compliant mechanisms derive their motion from the flexing of constituent members. This eliminates sliding contact in bearings and joints, which can greatly increase reliability, especially in space. Because compliant mechanisms do not rely on assembled joints, they are ideally suited to monolithic designs. Combining additive manufacturing techniques with compliant mechanism technology has the potential to greatly increase performance and reduce costs in space technology applications. Compliant mechanisms are often designed to be monolithic, and using 3D additive machining processes is a natural step in the development of compliant mechanism technology. Adapting compliant mechanism design strategies for monolithic space technology carries some unique challenges that must be addressed in order to take full advantage of the technology. In the context of additive manufacturing techniques, I will address two major challenges: vibration characteristics and large strain energy. Because compliant mechanisms are flexible, they often have low natural frequencies, making them subject to undesirable vibrations. By employing the pseudo-rigid-body model of complaint mechanisms in conjunction with finite element analysis, I believe we can find ways to optimize compliant space mechanisms for high natural frequencies without compromising the needed flexibility. As compliant mechanisms are actuated they act as springs, storing energy as the flexible members deflect. This results in a need for larger input forces to overcome the mechanism's internal stiffness. Large actuators add mass, complexity, and increased power consumption. Static balancing has been demonstrated to be effective in reducing the force required to actuate compliant mechanisms. Usually static balancing strategies require some assembly step to introduce a pre-load that compensates for energy added during actuation. Think of an architect's lamp and how the springs compensate for the weight of the lamp. Now realize that all those springs and links add complexity and expense. Finding strategies for pre-load introduction without departing from monolithic fabrication will be a major part of ensuring the feasibility of this technology. By exploiting compliant mechanisms in monolithic designs, we can eliminate much of that complexity. By developing design guidelines that adapt compliant mechanisms to the needs of space technology I hope to take advantage of new advances in additive metal manufacturing to achieve unprecedented performance and longer life. This research will directly impact Technology Area 12.3 (Mechanical Systems), but TA13.3 (Increased Reliability) and TA04.3 (Robot Manipulation) will also benefit.

  • Closed Out: The goal of this research is to enable designers to fully exploit the advantages of combining 3Dprinting and compliant mechanisms while providing new flexure types with superior performance. Additive metal manufacturing allows for unprecedented geometric complexity in mechanical design. Compliant mechanisms enable monolithic (one-piece) devices to provide complex motion. Combining the advantages of each could result in the design and economic construction of monolithic mechanisms that are friction-free, wear-free, lubrication-free, and high-precision. However, the stiffness of compliant mechanisms is governed by material properties, geometry, and boundary conditions. This research sought to develop new strategies for designing low stiffness 3D-printed compliant mechanisms. Three strategies were explored: static balancing, lattice flexures, and compound joints. Static balancing is well established in the field, but has remained complex. In this research I demonstrated a simplified method for static balancing of rotation flexures. I established a new family of flexure geometries, called lattice flexures, and published data on their stiffness reduction and off-axis-stiffness behavior. I also explored the concept of compound joints, which improve flexibility by arraying flexures in series and parallel. All of these methods are detailed in journal papers. Finally, I demonstrated how these three methods can be used in concert to achieve ultra-low stiffness in a monolithic, 3D-printed compliant mechanism. •Created and demonstrated a simplified method for static balancing of rotational flexures ▪ Current methods focus on linear systems and are complex o This method uses four parameters and requires no optimization ▪ Current methods use many parameters and rely on optimization o Validated method numerically and experimentally •Formalized a method for determining the load-dependent stiffness of rotational flexures ▪ Load-dependent stiffness is often overlooked or neglected in flexure design o Published load-dependent stiffness behavior of four distinct flexure geometries •Introduced the concept of lattice flexures ▪ Formerly this topology has not been published in the literature o Established two distinct lattice topologies o Analytically, numerically, and experimentally validated stiffness reduction o Found surface-fitted equations describing off-axis stiffness •Formalized the concept of compound joints ▪ Compound flexures appear in the literature but are not explicitly referred to o Explored two configurations; series and series-and-parallel o Investigated and described stiffness behaviour in six degrees of freedom ▪ Understanding the behaviour of these flexure configurations allows them to be applied in new areas o Analytically, numerically, and experimentally demonstrated reduction in center shift
