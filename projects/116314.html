Diamond-like Coating Assessment for Reduction of Thruster Erosion (DCARTE-ACO)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 116314
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Validation of a new, lightweight, coating material for solar array interconnects that can reduce erosion from electric propulsion thruster plumes that will increase the reliability and decrease the cost of solar arrays for long duration, high-power solar electric propulsion missions.



Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Electric propulsion thruster plume erosion rate lower than that of the current standard Room Temperature Vulcanized (RTV) CV-1142 at Xenon ion energies between 250 and 600 eV and angles between 0 and 60 degrees.A coating that adheres to Kovar (solar cell interconnect material) and remains adherent during exposure to electric propulsion thruster plume at Xenon ion energies between 250 and 600 eV and angles between 0 and 60 degrees.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The Diamond-like Coating Assessment for Reduction of Thruster Erosion (DCARTE) project completed testing under the Announcement of Collaborative Opportunity (ACO) with Maxar Technologies. The purpose of DCARTE was to measure the xenon (Xe) ion sputter erosion yield at selected energies and angles for diamond-like coated (DLC) Kovar and room temperature vulcanized (RTV) silicone, NuSilTM CV-1142. The CV-1142 was cast in disks and cured at Maxar Technologies prior to shipping to NASA GRC. The DLC was applied to Kovar sheets by IBC Coatings Technologies. The DLC is a mixture of diamondlike carbon and silicon. The silicon was added by the manufacturer to provide low friction and release properties as well as lower coating stress. Sputter testing was conducted on these materials in order to assess the benefit of using DLC to protect solar cell interconnects instead of using the current state of the art protective material CV-1142. Currently, Maxar paints the thin Kovar interconnect surfaces with CV-1142 to act as a sacrificial coating to prevent thinning and breakage of the interconnects due to Xe ion thruster plume erosion on solar powered electric propulsion systems. The process is labor intensive, increases production time, and results in greater mass of the solar arrays due to the thick CV-1142 coating. Maxar Technologies estimates that $500,000 in production costs annually could be saved by changing from a thick painted coating to a more lightweight, low sputter yield material like DLC, that could be applied to the Kovar in a batch process prior to array assembly. Xenon ion sputter yields for the control, molybdenum, were for the most part consistent between the two ion sources used for testing and with literature values. The only exception was one data point at 600 eV, 60 degree angle of incidence which was significantly lower than expected. Xenon sputter yields for CV-1142 followed typical trends with energy and angle except for the 250 eV data which was higher than the 350 eV sputter yields. The 250 eV sputter yields were measured using the 15 cm DC ion source while the 350 and 600 eV data was measured with the RF ion source. A difference between results of sputter yields with different ion sources was not observed for either molybdenum or DLC so it is more likely that the difference in sputter yields could have been caused by the difference in the CV-1142 material. The 250 eV data was measured on CV-1142 from an earlier batch which was visibly yellow compared to the most recent batch which was more clear and colorless. DLC Kovar sputter yields were very consistent and followed typical trends versus energy and angle of incidence. The sputter yield for DLC was low in terms of magnitude, and lower than carbon/graphite which has one of the lowest known sputter yields. X-ray Photoelectron Spectroscopy (XPS) results indicated that DLC changed very little as a result of Xe ion sputtering. Solar absorptance for DLC Kovar is ~0.72 with thermal emittance ranging from 0.82 to 0.57 for temperatures ranging from 150 to 400 K. Solar absorptance is high, but it will depend on the application whether this is acceptable. Results look promising for using DLC as a lightweight protective coating against Xe ion sputtering for the ion energies and angles tested.
