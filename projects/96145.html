Project Description

Space exploration of the present and the future will increasingly involve humans and autonomous systems working together as distributed and co-located teams. My research examines the behavioral, technical, and design implications of re-embodied intelligence: artificial agents that migrate across multiple robotic bodies to achieve smoothness across multiple interactions in different contexts. This proposal describes a research plan for establishing human-system communication-related needs on human-robot space exploration teams, evaluating re-embodiment as a tool for recovery from failure in the context of space exploration, and designing for appropriate calibration of expectations for re-embodyable systems. The work pertains to Technology Area (TA) 4.4: Human-System Interaction.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The goal of this work was to study interactions between humans and complex systems (systems that have multiple humans, robots, software agents, interfaces, interaction modalities, etc.) that are relevant to space exploration. Specifically, our work focuses on how interactions among and across the components of an integrated system influence the way humans perceive, understand, communicate, and work with such a system. This grant supported research that resulted in findings about (a) how public-facing service robots can use agents that have different kinds of relationships to their users to provide personalized experiences during multi-person interactions, (b) how humans use language to mourn robots, notably the Mars Opportunity Rover, in social media posts, (c) how people perceive trust and competence of a multi-robot system after a failure is experienced, based on the strategy the system uses to perform its recovery from such a failure, and (d) current use patterns and envisioned future use of smart home technologies and human-agent interactions in smart Page 2 – Revised 02/2018 environments. It has also supported (e) the development of a conceptual framework for understanding human-smart environment interactions, (f) the development of a conceptual model of AI agent as mediators in relationships among individuals, embodiments, and services, and (g) studies that used design research methods to understand people’s perceptions of robots and IoT devices that interact with one another in present-day and future smart environments. These projects have resulted in several conference and journal publications (ACM/IEEE Human-Robot Interaction Conference (HRI); ACM Human-Agent Interaction Conference (HAI); ACM Conference on Designing Interactive Systems (DIS); IEEE Transactions on Human-Machine Systems Journal (THMS)) and actionable design guidelines for natural and conversational human-agent interaction in complex, integrated, multi-device systems.
