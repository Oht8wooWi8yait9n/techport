Oxygen Recovery via Carbon Dioxide Electrolysis with Microtubular Solid Oxide Cells

NASA TechPort Project ID: 91780
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Carbon dioxide reduction is considered a major shortcoming for the current Atmosphere Revitalization System. Novel technologies are desired so that the oxygen recovery rate can be significantly increased, thereby approaching full closure of the atmosphere revitalization loop. Microtubular solid oxide cells are being developed to carry out direct carbon dioxide electrolysis and achieve high oxygen recovery rates. The major research objectives include material development and innovation, cell fabrication and component assembly, so that the electrolysis performance and stability can be significantly improved. The research activity also includes electrochemical performance characterization and durability evaluation. The proposed project will provide a highly efficient oxygen recovery technology from direct carbon dioxide electrolysis, which can fulfill future space exploration needs. The proposed research will potentially obtain sustainability for future space exploration by operating solid oxide cell in electrolysis mode for oxygen recovery and fuel cell model for power generation alternatively. This project will bring innovative advancements of solid oxide cell technology beyond the NASA applications.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The goal of the proposal is to study a highly efficient solid oxide electrolysis technology to recover oxygen from carbon dioxide, which can fulfill future space exploration needs beyond Low Earth Orbit. The proposed research includes the synthesis and characterizations of electrode materials, the fabrications and characterizations of microtubular solid oxide electrolysis cells. •Developed new approaches to reliably determine electrochemical kinetic properties (surface exchange coefficient and bulk diffusivity) of electrode materials; •Developed new fabrication process to fabricate micro-channel array structured fuel electrode substrate; •Developed a new thin film technique to fabricate the dense and thin SDC electrolyte with the thickness of ~ 10 μm; •Comprehensively characterized the fabricated microtubular cell Ni-SDC/SDC/PrBaCo2O5+δ and obtained high power density of ~1484 mW cm-2 at 600 °C; •Comprehensively characterized the fabricated microtubular cells with different material systems for CO2 electrolysis; Identified the critical role of electrolyte for high efficiency of oxygen recovery from CO2.
