Let It Go (After Latching): Synchronized Antenna Deployment System

NASA TechPort Project ID: 182454
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

NASA is seeking innovative solutions for a synchronized antenna deployment system. The primary objective is to develop a mechanism that ensures sequential deployment of antenna panels, addressing a critical aspect of space-based communication technology.


In this challenge, participants were tasked with designing a mechanism that will release hexagonal panels in a predetermined sequence. Specifically, the mechanism should trigger the release of the next hexagon in a stack only after the previous one has successfully latched into place. This sequential deployment is crucial for maintaining the antenna's structural integrity and operational efficiency.


The proposed designs needed to be compatible with one of the winning latch designs from the previous "Let's Connect" challenge. Additionally, they must integrate seamlessly with the provided backing structure model without compromising the parabolic surface of the antenna. Participants were directed to focus on creating a solution that is both effective and adaptable to existing NASA technologies.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Under "Starburst: A Revolutionary Under-Constrained Adaptable Deployable Structure Architecture" we have a unique approach that can deploy antennas to much higher accuracies than ever before. It consists of deploying a series of hexagons to form a parabola.

This challenge could result in concepts for a system that will release the hexagons in the stack one at a time, after the previous hexagon is deployed and no longer moving.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This project is a continuation of Micro-004: Positive Connections where the project owners asked the GrabCAD community to build on previous winning solutions for a new ask. The study "Starburst: A Revolutionary Under-Constrained Adaptable Deployable Structure Architecture" demonstrated a unique approach to deploying large antennas to much higher accuracies than ever before. The first system had all the segments release at once, but this created problems with the segments interfering with each other during deployment, except for very simple “Z-fold” stowing patterns. We want to sequentially deploy the segment after the prior segment latches to enable more complex deployments. The winner for Micro-008 was the third place winner for Micro-006. Marcelo Valderrey's from Argentina design impressed the judges with its "over-center" latch mechanism, which eliminated sliding friction by utilizing rollers. The system allowed for quick actuation when the latch was engaged, moving significant distances efficiently. Additionally, the design package was exceptionally detailed, further demonstrating the thoughtfulness and precision of this submission. (note only the latch design was judged for placement, extra material submitted did not impact the judging). The results for both GrabCAD challenge will be modified for prototyping and subsequently used to advocate for additional funding to fully integrate designs.
