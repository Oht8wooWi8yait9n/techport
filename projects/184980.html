Project Description

NASA sought innovative solutions for recycling food packaging and polymer waste for space and lunar missions. Managing waste was critical for establishing a long-term presence on the Moon, as current methods of discarding or burning waste upon re-entry were unsustainable.

Insights were needed to develop recycling technologies that:

  • Processed various types of polymer waste, including food packaging.
  • Operated efficiently in space or lunar environments.
  • Produced high-quality recycled materials for manufacturing or other applications.
  • Achieved high recycling efficiency (>90%).

Trash from food packaging was discarded during missions by release during re-entry, which incinerated the material. This was not useful for inhabiting the lunar surface where discarded materials would accumulate. The development of this technology enabled long-term sustainability by identifying packaging that could be processed into useful goods with high efficiency, minimizing waste.

Recycling food waste packaging from lunar or in-space human-operated missions was a necessity to reduce stored trash, prevent contamination of the terrain, and enable long-term human sustainability outside of terrestrial resources. A steady stream of packaging was disposed of after consuming the contents. The challenge sought answers to several key questions regarding packaging materials, coatings, cleaning, potential reuse for human-facing applications, required processes, and ideal supply chain management.

The project team was interested in understanding current and future food packaging, and how it could be converted into additive manufacturing (AM) feedstock.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Better understanding of:

  • What coatings are typically found on packaging, and how can they be removed? For example, consider Capri-Sun packages with composite layers of aluminum and polymer.
  • How can packaging be cleaned for processing and subsequent reuse?
  • Can materials be repurposed for human-facing applications such as eating utensils, personal care items, or clothing?
  • What existing processes are available for reusing these materials, or do we need to develop specialized methods?

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Project was fully successful; deliverables planned for future implementation. The project started with no baseline knowledge, so having a comprehensive landscape review is a 100% improvement. While there was some word-of-mouth input from the Spark campaign, this effort delivered an actual product—documentation our team didn’t have to create, saving significant time and energy. Next steps include repeating this kind of review for clothing and crew waste, and reaching out to some of the companies identified in the tech survey to explore collaboration opportunities while continuing to build out the broader space waste picture.
