Development of an Intelligent Coupling Approach for Modeling and Prediction of Cryogenic Propellant Behavior in Microgravity during Long-term Storage

NASA TechPort Project ID: 118283
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The ultimate goal of the proposed project is to provide an integrated computational toolkit for NASA scientists to efficiently and accurately predict the long-term cryogenic propellant behavior in storage tanks for space mission planning. The toolkit will leverage capabilities of existing Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) and nodal codes used by NASA for cryogenic propellant analysis with new efficient coupling approaches and accurate fluid dynamics and heat transfer correlations. To realize this goal, three key and central objectives are proposed: (1) enhance the accuracy of nodal models for fast simulation of complex two-phase cryogen systems by implementing physics-informed correlations or reduced thermal models based on high-fidelity reference simulations, (2) facilitate efficient use of the CFD and nodal codes for prediction of the long-term cryogenic propellant storage, and (3) deliver a fully validated analysis tool accounting for accuracy and computational speed while satisfying NASAs need for mission operations.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This project will enable integrated analysis of complex two-phase cryogenic propellant behavior in microgravity during long-term space missions as the first-of-its-kind tool. It will provide enhanced modeling capability to analyze complex physics by improving prediction accuracy and computation efficiency. The project will lead to dramatic improvements at the system level to storage tank designs with better performance and minimize time, human resources, and capitale cost for space mission planning.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: NASA’s present and future space exploration missions require the capability to store cryogenic propellants for long periods without substantial propellant losses in the space environment. However, cryogenic fluid management (CFM) in microgravity presents several fundamental physical challenges, especially for the storage of liquid hydrogen due to its low critical temperature. These physical challenges generally arise from control of tank pressure and temperature, ullage space size and location, boil-off venting, and work of liquid acquisition devices. To achieve an effective CFM during the long-term storage of cryogenic propellants in space, a thorough understanding of cryogenic fluid dynamics in microgravity becomes very important. Modeling techniques have been demonstrated to be a powerful means to facilitate such an understanding. Currently, lumped parameter (nodal) analysis and computational fluid analysis (CFD) are the two main tools used to model and predict cryogenic thermo-fluid behavior. Given the fact that nodal tool runs quickly and cheaply while CFD achieves high accuracy, an integrated numerical tool combining both codes’ merits would be particularly attractive to cryogenic propellant storage designers in providing highly reliable solutions for long-term space missions. The objective of this research is to provide an integrated computational toolkit for NASA scientists to predict the long-term cryogenic propellant behavior efficiently and accurately in storage tanks for space mission planning. The toolkit leverages capabilities of existing CFD and nodal codes used by NASA for cryogenic propellant analysis with new efficient coupling approaches and accurate fluid dynamics and heat transfer correlations. To demonstrate the newly developed coupling techniques, the team simulated the self-pressurization process in a small cryogenic propellant tank, the sizes of which are similar to those in the Tank Pressure Control Experiments and the Zero Boil-Off Tank (ZBOT) experiment, with an initial liquid fill level of 50%. The coupling scheme was developed based on a domain decomposition approach, based on which the tank is divided into liquid and vapor regions at the liquid-vapor interface. Due to a relative lack of models for resolving the internal flow in the vapor region, the CFD code is used to simulate the vapor region and the nodal code is used to simulate the liquid region. In addition, an in-house code was developed to realize the data exchange between two commercial codes, namely SINDA/FLUINT and ANSYS Fluent. Results showed that the coupling scheme successfully integrated the two codes to predict the fluid dynamics and heat transfer in the tank. In addition, the results were compared to those of a CFD simulation only. Great agreement was achieved in both fluid temperature and tank pressure predictions with a largely reduced computational cost. In addition, an equation-free based coupling approach has been developed and implemented for multiscale fluid dynamics and heat transfer analyses. In this coupling scheme, CFD and nodal codes run simultaneously at the beginning of a problem for a short period to find the equation-free corrections. Then the nodal code continues to run alone for the rest of the simulation period using the just-calculated corrections to generate ‘CFD-like’ solutions. This coupling approach was applied to multiple numerical testing cases consisting of a pipe flow problem and cryogenic tank pressurization-depressurization transients. This coupling approach has been demonstrated to effectively improve the accuracy of the nodal code simulation. The computational cost is greatly reduced and is comparable to that of traditional nodal modeling techniques.
