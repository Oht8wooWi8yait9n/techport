Project Description

The Mars Campaign Office (MCO) Life Support Systems project Environmental Monitoring (EM) systems task objectives are to develop and demonstrate onboard monitoring, detection, and analysis capabilities that will replace the state-of-the-art (SOA) need to frequently return air samples to Earth for ground-based laboratory analysis. This effort is addressing these challenges by adopting new analytical technologies and techniques that will allow for a modular EM system architecture that integrates multiple sensing modalities to address the monitoring needs of future deep-space exploration missions. 

Exploration Aerosol Monitors (ExAM): Provides a flight demonstration of multiple aerosol monitors to measure and characterize particles in the crew respiratory hazard range. The suite of aerosol sensors will collect passive samples, as well as detect, measure, and distinguish aerosols of varied sizes and types.

 

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The Aerosol Monitors suite of instruments are candidates for exploration missions and the ISS deployments will raise the technology readiness levels (TRL) and provide insight and lessons learned for the next generation of spacecraft dust monitors.  OPERA has the unique ability to distinguish between different types of particles, which can assist smoke detection and prevent false alarms from dust in a coordinated monitoring architecture.  The MARS samplers provide gravimetric verification for any dust monitoring activity, with archival samples that can be analyzed with a variety of techniques offline.  Respirable lunar dust particles are a threat to human health and dust monitoring technologies provide the following benefits: (1) track exposure levels for human health protection and requirements verification, (2) Validate dust mitigation technologies used in lunar missions, (3) Alert to failures in HEPA filtration or other ECLSS equipment.

Publications, References & Library Items

  • Aerosol Monitors Instruments — The Aerosol Monitors payload includes four devices: an instrument to measure particles from 5 nm to 3 µm (Modified Aerosol Growth by Internal water Cycling [MAGIC]), a novel instrument to measure particles from 100 nm to 10 µm while distinguishing between cabin dust, lunar dust, and smoke using AI (OPto-Electrical Real-time Aerosol classifier [OPERA]), a sampler collecting particles in a filter for gravimetric analysis enabling instrument calibration (Mobile Aerosol Reference Sampler [MARS]), and the Passive Aerosol Samplers deployed on ISS, collecting particles in 2016 and 2018.
  • Typical Particles Sampled on ISS — Particles sampled on ISS are predominantly from humans, such as skin flakes and antiperspirant (very prevalent due to required hours of daily exercise and the absence of laundry facilities). Stainless steel wear particles come from the exercise equipment and fiberglass is known to shed from flexible portable ducting or harness overwrap.
  • Aerosol Monitors Deployed on ISS — The four devices were deployed in seven different locations near air intakes with the Passive Aerosol Samplers intercepting the dirty air flowing into ISS HEPA filters, the MARS units deployed as a pair to sample PM10 and PM2.5, and the MAGIC and OPERA nearby to measure different size ranges of particles in the vicinity.
