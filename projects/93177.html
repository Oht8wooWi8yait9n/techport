Carbon Dioxide Removal (CDRILS, CDep)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 93177
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The Life Support Systems project Carbon Dioxide Removal and Management project includes development of systems that remove CO2 from a crewed cabin and manage the flow rate of removed CO2 to a downstream CO2 reduction system. In general, the goal of these efforts is to develop technology demonstrations of systems and components for future exploration vehicles. A long duration platform for testing in a relevant environment would enable the evaluation and certification of the technology candidates for future missions. The project is developing a liquid amine technology to remove CO2 from a spacesuit and cabin, as well as regenerate liquid amines in a space suit after Extra Vehicular Activity (EVA). In addition, the project is using cold surfaces to collect volatile organic compounds and remove them from a CO2 stream to minimize fouling of downstream CO2 reduction systems.

Efforts include:

Carbon Dioxide Removal by Ionic Liquids System (CDRILS): Development of a 2-crew scale CO2 removal system that uses ionic liquids to be demonstrated in a spacecraft cabin, surface rover, and a space suit.

CO2 Cold Surface Deposition (CDep) for Volatile Organic Compounds (VOC) removal from a CO2 stream to be demonstrated in a spacecraft cabin and surface rover.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Maintaining low CO2 levels is critical for astronaut health. These technology developments may provide the function of removing CO2 with a system different from the state-of-the art that could potentially be more reliable and require less resources to operate.
