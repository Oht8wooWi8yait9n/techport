Synthetic Biology for Recycling Human Waste into Nutraceuticals, and Materials: Closing the Loop for Long-Term Space Travel

NASA TechPort Project ID: 91777
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

It is impractical for astronauts to travel with all necessary supplies in future long-term space exploration missions. Therefore, it is imperative that technologies enabling the production of food, nutraceuticals, medicine, and materials from extraterrestrial resources are developed. These resources found in space include minerals and feedstocks found on distant bodies, waste generated by humans and space shuttle operation, and solar energy. Together with synthetic biology-driven technology for engineering microorganisms that can reliably, efficiently, and flexibly utilize such “in situ resources”, astronauts may be able to generate the food, nutraceuticals, medicine, and materials they need to carry out their mission. Due to potential uncertainty in resource availability, this proposal focuses on engineering yeast to convert respiration CO2 algae biomass and human urine into nutritional omega-3 fats and 3D printable plastics.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Astronauts will be able to generate the food, nutraceuticals, medicine, and materials they need to carry out their mission.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: It is impractical for astronauts to travel with all necessary supplies in future long-term space exploration missions. Therefore, it is imperative that technologies enabling the production of food, nutraceuticals, medicine, and materials from extraterrestrial resources are developed. The overall objective of this proposal is to use synthetic biology and metabolic engineering to improve food and material production from human waste. This will be accomplished through three specific objectives: Objective 1. Engineer Utilization of Space Shuttle and Human Wastes. Objective 2. High Efficiency Yarrowia lipolytica Production of Nutraceutical, Omega-3 Fats Objective 3. High Efficiency Yarrowia lipolytica Production of PHA for 3D Printing To those ends, we demonstrated successful source of nitrogen from waste sources. These sources include pure urea, a mock urine, and real human urine. Waste carbon was considered in the form of cyanobacterial biomass. We also demonstrated the use of acetate as a fixed form of carbon dioxide. Pathways for producing omega-3 fatty acids were established. We chose a noncanonical pathway that used CoA carrier instead of phosphatidyl choline, which reduced the bottleneck caused by the so-called substrate dichotomy. The omega-3 pathway involves desaturation and elongation of oleic acid and linoleic acid. In the canonical pathway, the desaturation typically occurs on fatty acids activated by phosphatidyl choline, occurring in the endoplasmic reticulum. The elongation occurs in the cytoplasm using CoA. The advantage of using the CoA-dependent desaturases is that it avoids the back-and-forth shuttling of fatty acids between different cellular compartments. We demonstrated the production of medium chain PHAs through a variety of metabolic engineering strategies, including media optimization, pathway compartmentalization, and push-pull-block. We also developed high titer strains for the production of fatty alcohols.
