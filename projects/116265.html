Project Description

In this collaboration, Maxar Technologies will deliver to the NASA test centers a mechanical Inverted Meta-Morphic-Alpha (IMM-α) Standard Power Module (SPM), an electrically active IMM-α SPM and associated ground test equipment. The IMM-α SPM technology will enable future commercial and NASA missions with higher power at a lower mass. Using the technical expertise and unique test capabilities at NASA GRC and NASA MSFC, these two centers will perform a comprehensive combined environmental test series. This array technology can be further advanced from TRL4 to TRL6.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Higher power solar arrays; less massive solar arrays for same power

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Flexible Solar Array Protocols was a Game Changing Development (GCD) Program task through the Announcement of Collaborative Opportunities (ACO) effort that used a Space Act Agreement (SAA) between Maxar Technologies and NASA (GRC and MSFC) to test 5-junction Inverted Metamorphic Multijunction (IMM-α) solar cells on roll out compatible blankets through qualification protocols similar to AIAA Standards S-111/112 to raise TRL to ≥ 5. Maxar, with SolAero, provided IMM-α coupons in flight-like configurations to GRC and MSFC for performance and environmental testing including electrical performance using LED-based pulsed solar simulators, sine vibe testing, thermal balance testing, electrostatic discharge testing, UV radiation exposure, electron radiation exposure, proton radiation exposure, and thermal cycling. NASA used the unique test facilities and technical expertise it has to accomplish a comprehensive suite of space combined environmental tests including Solar cell electrical performance testing capabilities at GRC, Space environment testing facilities at MSFC. Early delays in coupon delivery were compounded by mandatory telework requirements implemented by NASA as part of the COVID-19 Response Framework. The original test plan was followed with an exception for ESD testing. ESD testing was shown to be a problem in separate tests Maxar conducted in 2018 and 2019. It was not worth the risk to damage the one and only test coupon before the space environmental tests could be applied. The electrical coupon performed very well throughout the application of space environments. The changes, particularly due to radiation, were inline with expectations and models. NASA’s current emphasis on the Moon to Mars campaign has revealed many applications for this new solar array technology. This helped motivate the ACO Project team to finish this project, and in doing so, advance this technology. This advancement will enable future commercial and NASA missions with higher specific power (W/m2) at a lower mass (W/kg).
