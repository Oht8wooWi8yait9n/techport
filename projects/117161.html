Rapid Prototyping Module for Advancement of In House, Low Cost, Low Noise, Ultrafast High Voltage Pulse Drivers for Planetary Remote Sensing and Spectrometry

Project Description

The Module for Rapid Prototyping High-Voltage Electronics with Ultrafast Switching (MoRPHEUS) is a modular plug-and-play unit for the rapid integration, test and characterization of several state-of-the-art HV power supply and pulse driver designs to advance center competency in HV development while improving in-house assembly and screening techniques to reduce lead-time and risk for flight HV drivers. The novel designs proposed are promising to significantly improve performance while reducing footprint to enable new instrumentation capabilities with applications including electro-optic beam steering, raster scan imaging, fiber optics, and feedback polarization control for mass spectrometer systems.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

1. Advance center competency in HV development including design, assembly and screening.
2. Reduce lead-time and project risk with rapid prototyping, integration and test.
3. Standardize in-house HV design, fabrication, assembly and test procedure.
4. Enable new instrumentation capability with in-house advancement of state-of-the-art HV technology.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Four unique high voltage (HV) power supply topologies were built, tested and characterized. Two novel high voltage pulse driver topologies were also designed that will see continued development through partnership with several flight projects as well as future R&amp;D endeavors. Furthermore, this IRAD established a foundation of formal, peer reviewed procedures for in-house HV assembly, insulation and screening to improve quality and reduce risk to flight HV instruments. These efforts have expanded Goddard&#39;s scope of competency in flight HV development. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
