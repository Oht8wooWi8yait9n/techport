Development of RF Hydrogen Plasma for Ion-Assisted Metal Processing of Lunar Regolith

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186797
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

NASA's Artemis program aims to establish a sustained human presence on the Moon. Achieving this goal will require access to energy resources and construction materials to support habitation and exploration. Transporting these materials from Earth is costly and increases mission risks, underscoring the importance of technologies that can make use of local lunar materials, commonly referred to as in-situ resource utilization (ISRU). Lunar soil, known as regolith, beneficially contains abundant metal-bearing minerals that can support the development of surface infrastructure, radiation shielding, and power systems. However, most existing metal extraction technologies require extremely high temperatures (800--1700 °C), leading to high power demands, thermal management challenges, and limited practicality for long-duration surface operations. These limitations highlight the need for more energy-efficient ISRU technologies. This proposal aims to develop a low-technology-readiness approach for lunar regolith metal separation using hydrogen plasma. In this method, hydrogen gas is energized using radio-frequency (RF) power, which applies oscillating electrical energy to create a cloud of highly reactive hydrogen particles, including charged hydrogen ions (H+, H2+, H3+). These species can deliver energy directly to the surface, enabling oxygen removal from metal-oxygen bonds, a process known as reduction, without reliance on extreme bulk heating like conventional techniques that rely on heating the entire material to very high temperatures. Hydrogen plasma has demonstrated potential for efficient metal reduction; however, the plasma conditions that govern reactive hydrogen ion populations have not been systematically investigated, leaving an important gap in defining the parameters that maximize metal reduction. The objective of this research is to identify and optimize the plasma operating conditions that enhance lunar regolith metal reduction rates using a tunable RF plasma system. This study will vary gas pressure, gas composition, RF power, and RF frequency to determine which plasma parameters favor the most reactive hydrogen ion species (H+ and H2+) while minimizing less effective reaction pathways (H3+). This technology will also incorporate controlled electrical biasing of the regolith sample surface to attract hydrogen ions and evaluate the impact of increased ion energy on reduction performance. Gentle mechanical agitation will also be applied to enhance plasma surface area exposure and support continuous surface processing. Using the optimized plasma parameters, experiments will be conducted with iron-rich mineral simulants, particularly ilmenite (FeTiO3), and will be extended to regolith metal oxides such as silicon dioxide (SiO2), which is common across lunar regions relevant for future Artemis missions. Metal formation and changes in regolith composition will be measured using post-processing analysis tools, while the abundances of reactive hydrogen species will be monitored using in situ diagnostic instruments. By examining how changes in plasma settings influence metal production, this research will identify practical operating conditions for low-temperature regolith processing that are suitable for use in space. Overall, this research advances an early-stage hydrogen plasma technology toward practical application for lunar surface operations. By reducing power and thermal demands while supporting scalable processing through increased plasma-dust interaction volumes, the proposed work aligns with NASA's goals in surface sustainability and Moon-to-Mars exploration by enabling more efficient use of local lunar resources.
