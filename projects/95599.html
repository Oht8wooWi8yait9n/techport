An ultracompact opto-electro-fluidic system for preconcentration and separation of chiral molecules in in-situ life detection

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95599
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The objective of this proposal is to design and demonstrate an ultracompact opto-electro-fluidic system that can efficiently preconcentrate and separate low-abundance chiral molecules for in situ life detection. To achieve the objective, I plan to accomplish three specific research aims: (1) demonstrate an electro-fluidic preconcentrator for low-abundance molecules, (2) demonstrate an opto-fluidic separator for enantioselective separation of chiral molecules, and (3) integrate the preconcentrator and separator into a single ultracompact system and test its performances. With its superior performances, the proposed system will become an integral component for the future end-to-end in situ instrument for NASA space science missions. It will also benefit drug screening, drug purification and point-of-care biomedical applications in human space exploration.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Enhance in situ life detection for determining whether other habitable environments and even life itself can be found outside of Earth. Benefit drug screening, drug purification, and point-of-care biomedical applications in human space exploration. Enable the miniature end-to-end in situ instruments in flight missions.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To: Received partner funding.
  • Closed Out: Our long-term goal is to develop miniature end-to-end in situ instruments with liquid sample handling, acquisition and detection for future NASA science and human exploration missions. The objective of the research is to design and demonstrate an ultracompact opto-electro-fluidic system that can efficiently preconcentrate and separate low-abundance chiral molecules for in situ life detection. Chiral molecules are the building blocks of life. Many of biologically active molecules, such as amino acids, drugs, sugars, and enzymes, exhibit chirality. Dominance of one enantiomer over its mirror image is a sign of life. Study of chirality of amino acids in cometary and interstellar ice enables a better understanding of life origin on Earth. Detecting interstellar chiral molecules also plays a significant role in searching for life in space. For in situ life detection, miniature devices with reduced mass, volume and power for acquisition, handling and detection of low-abundance chiral molecules are highly desired, which, however, has remained unrealized. The proposed opto-electro-fluidic platform will exploit thermophoresis and optical force for preconcentration and separation of chiral molecules under the variable gravity fields.
