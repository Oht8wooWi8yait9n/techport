Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Over the years, the field of additive manufacturing has been brought into use by numerous industries. Additive manufacturing is a process that creates three dimensional objects in layers based on a Computer Aided Design (CAD) model to create a finished structure. The field of construction is an area that could see major improvements by utilizing this technology. Utilizing additive construction technology will revolutionize infrastructure and building construction activities by enabling rapid, cost effective, smart, sustainable, and structurally superior facilities. Additive construction is not only fit for Earth, but also has applications in off-Earth infrastructure. This technology is also under development by NASA for the Artemis Program for lunar infrastructure construction. The purpose of this project was to utilize additive construction technology to demonstrate the construction of spaceport infrastructure. Two large fluid storage tanks were to be built and used to capture potable water that is currently being purged and released into a dry well at a rate of approximately 2,000,000 gallons per month. Water lines must be flushed daily at KSC to maintain potable water status. The water was to be collected in tanks and used for KSC pesticide and herbicide applications. Pesticide and herbicide control currently use hydrants in the LC-39 area to supply water. This number increases as the usage goes up. Implementing the tanks would decrease both the waste and cost of water. In addition, the tanks would provide the pesticide and herbicide application contractor an operational savings by reducing the distance that must be traveled to fill up. In addition to the water tanks, this project began to explore the use of additively constructed metallic reinforcement printed within the structure, like rebar. To date, no one in the world has additively constructed a large-scale structure with metallic reinforcement printed concurrently. This aspect of the project is related to space based in-situ construction technologies because it is thought that metals, such as ferrosilicon, created as a waste product from processes like Molten Regolith Electrolysis (MRE) could be used as a lunar in-situ feedstock for construction of metallic structures. Though this project was not able to complete all the objectives due to COVID-19 impacts and resource limitations, lessons learned lead to improved lab capabilities that put the Granular Mechanics and Regolith Operations (GMRO) lab in a better position to support in-situ construction activities. The work completed and lessons learned during this IR&TD significantly contributed to the award of the In-Situ Construction Game Changing Development (GCD) and Relevant Environment Additive Construction Technology (REACT) Announcement of Collaboration Opportunity (ACO) projects. Significant progress was made and can serve as a starting point for future work. This progress includes the establishment of tank requirements, operations and maintenance schedule, and plan for tank construction and final assembly.
