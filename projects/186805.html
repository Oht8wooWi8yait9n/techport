Lifelong Plan Adaptation for Robot Manipulation Starting from One Example

Project Description

As NASA develops a sustained presence on the Moon and Mars, autonomous robots can be used to save crew time, maintain uncrewed vehicles, and support scientific discovery. For robots to perform tasks such as cargo sorting, equipment maintenance, and consumables handling, they must reason about interdependent steps over long task horizons, adapt to a changing environment, and interact with new objects. Neither the task-specific expert specification required by classical planning approaches nor the large scale of demonstration data required for learning-based approaches can scale to the varied tasks and dynamic environment of spaceflight. This proposal outlines a course of research that leverages the long-horizon reasoning afforded by task and motion planning (TAMP) while exploiting prior experience to adapt to new tasks. The proposed work extracts individual actions and cues about high-level problem solving from a single long-horizon demonstration. The actions and high-level reasoning cues, called strategies, are adapted to new environments, objects, and robot embodiments to expand and accelerate planning over a lifetime of operations. To accomplish this, actions are represented using a robot-agnostic and collision-aware framework such that these actions can be applied to new scenes and objects. Strategies that provide high-level reasoning cues are used to speed up task planning when approaching problems similar to ones a robot has solved before. The feasibility of adapting these actions and strategies is evaluated to mitigate the catastrophic effects of failure in a spaceflight environment. Finally, these developments are combined into a single framework and evaluated through integrated physical testing. In the long term, this framework can become a closed-loop process in which successful executions arising from adaptation serve as additional experiences to exploit for future operations, laying the foundation for fully autonomous operations as we explore far from Earth.
