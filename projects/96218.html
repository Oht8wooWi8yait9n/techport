Distributed Algorithms for Exploration with Robotic Sensor Networks

NASA TechPort Project ID: 96218
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Building on our understanding of the lunar surface and subsurface will be integral to a sustainable, long-term lunar presence. To explore this harsh, unknown environment, multi-agent systems of small-scale robots have been proposed as system architecture due to the low volume, mass, and development costs of small robots, and the robustness and coordination capabilities of multi-agent systems. This research investigates the challenges associated with deploying and controlling a network of robots in unknown environments with a specific focus on collaboration and exploration. These challenges include localization, multi-hop routing, motion control for exploration, consensus and task allocation, and multi-robot mapping. In particular, this research focuses algorithms which are low-complexity (suitable for resource-constrained robots), distributed (to minimize reliance on a central agent), and perform well under uncertainty. This research will further the state of the art through the design, evaluation, and implementation of algorithms which use inter-robot signals to perform localization and can efficiently and reliably explore unknown environments under constraints on connectivity.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

By demonstrating the feasibility of this algorithm and evaluating its performance relative to a centralized communication architecture, this research will result in novel distributed autonomy for robotic sensor networks. This research directly addresses the NASA-identified technology need for Automated Intelligent Networked Systems (TA 5.5.3.1).

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Lunar and planetary exploration puts stringent requirements on a robotic system including accurate localization and the ability to communicate findings with a remote base station despite the lack of existing infrastructure. Robotic networks are well-suited for harsh, remote environments because they are robust to the failure of a single robot and can communicate and collaborate. We identify four key problems and provide the necessary solutions to enable networked robotic exploration in harsh, GPS-denied, communication-restricted environments. First, we consider that while connectivity is necessary for communication, strictly maintaining connectivity limits exploration. We propose a distributed controller which more flexibly constrains connectivity and improves exploration coverage. Second, we note that predicting connectivity is challenging in unknown environments with obstacles that prevent line-of-sight. Thus, we propose a data-driven approach to signal strength prediction which leverages well-known models of signal propagation phenomena (e.g. shadowing) and machine learning. Third, we observe that accurate signal strength models can also enable collaborative localization: pairwise measurements can determine the positions of all robots. Thus, we propose an algorithm for network localization which is robust to obstacles which prevent line-of-sight. Finally, we demonstrate that collaborative localization can reduce the computational complexity of exploration and mapping through a distributed approach to localization for a team of robots with coordinated mobility. Together, these contributions enable a team of mobile robots to efficiently (in terms of time and complexity) explore and map remote environments, e.g. the lunar subsurface, while allowing timely data transfer. We validate the proposed algorithms and models in simulation and on a multi-robot team in large-scale subterranean environments in collaboration with NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory.
