Revolutionary Vertical Lift Technology Project: Two-Speed Drive System Demo

NASA TechPort Project ID: 93073
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Demonstrate two-speed drive system capable of 50% rpm reduction with less than 2% power loss while maintaing current power-to-weight ratios. This is accomplished through design and testing of unique transmission concepts capable of transmitting high torque through a wide speed range. A unique facility is developed to test scaled transmission configurations. New technologies to reduce drive system weight, losses and cost such as hybrid metal/composite gears, health monitoring, operation under loss of lubrication and reduce windage losses are explored.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The benefits are improved vehicle performance, empty weight fraction and safety through the demonstration of advanced multi-speed drive system designs and advanced component technologies. The benefits include an improvement in maintenance and operational costs.

Technology is most targeted at vertical lift drive systems, but has application to other geared systems, including automotive.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Brief Summary of Completion / Accomplishment:Technical Challenge: Two-speed Drive System Demonstration Tech Challenge Exit Criteria: Develop and demonstrate required technologies and designs for achieving a 50% reduction in rotor rpm via a two-speed drive system that incurs less than 2% power loss and maintains current power-to-weight ratios. Reduction of rotor speed by 50%:The research program execution demonstrated three mechanical assembles that achieved the required 50% reduction of rotor speed. A gearing concept termed the dual star idler was successfully tested with a dry clutch. Also, a gearing concept termed the offset compound gear was successfully tested with two different clutch concepts (a dry clutch and a wet clutch). All three mechanical assemblies were experimentally demonstrated to TRL-4 with successful transitions from high-speed (vertical take-off)mode to low-speed (forward flight) mode and then transitioning back to high-speed (vertical landing) mode. Two-speed drive incurs less than 2% power loss:The offset compound gear with a dry clutch was assessed by experiment and analysis to determine the power loss. The results of the power loss evaluation revealed, as anticipated, that the largest contributor to total power loss for the two-speed drive system was the windage loss created when rotating surfaces interact with the oil-air environment. The total power loss for the offset compound gear and dry clutch configuration operating at 1000 horsepower were determined to be 1.3% in high-speed mode and 1.7% in low-speed mode, exceeding the technical challenge objective of less than 2.0% power loss. Weight reduction technology that offsets two-speed transmission weight increase: The most promising weight reduction technology considered was the replacement of metal with composite material for gear bodies and shafts. A full-scale 16.5 inchdiameter hybrid composite gear was successfully demonstrated to operate at 5,000 horsepower, showing viability of the approach. Replacing steel with composite materials at selected locations was determined through analysis to result in a weight benefit of 61lbs for the two-speed drive system.
