Mechanoluminescent Materials for Structural Health Monitoring

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95503
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Various advanced sensor technologies (e.g. fiber Bragg grating, piezoelectric sensor) can detect damage, but require constant power, have large form factor, high cost in sensor interrogation, and data processing.\nUtilize mechanoluminescent (ML) materials for self-powered structural health monitoring of fiber reinforced polymer composites. \nGround test of ML-composite material on aerospace structure to determine feasibility and characterize properties. Complementary to existing EPSCoR research effort.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Autonomously detect internal delamination of composites and harvest energy from dynamic motion. Design of multifunctional materials and composites. Mechanoluminscent optoelectronic sensors is contributing technology in the development of multifunctional materials and composites for structural diagnosis, prognosis, and energy harvesting. Advanced manufacturing of multifunctional structural composites can decrease cost and increase knowledge for future NASA vehicles and any industry requiring health monitoring of composites.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: New Mexico Tech is continuing to work on improving the MLO sensors based on the lessons learned from testing at AFRC. While the CIF was met with limited success in fully characterizing the sensors, deficiencies identified through the CIF will steer the remaining time in an EPSCoR contract towards improvements and operability of the sensors for future applications. The sensors are currently at TRL 4 and require further development to increase robustness and consistency in order to be sufficiently reliable for integration into composite test articles.
