Sensor-based Prognostics to Avoid Runaway Reactions and Catastrophic Ignition (SPARRCI)

Project Description

Electrified aviation has been growing and will continue to grow as industry, academia, and government invest in hybrid and all-electric aircraft concepts to reduce the carbon footprint and reach electrification goals by 2050. Batteries are a logical choice for powering these hybrid and all-electric aircraft, but battery technology is not yet at the required performance and safety levels. To enable a higher level of safety for existing and future batteries, the Sensor-based Prognostics to Avoid Runaway Reactions & Catastrophic Ignition project, SPARRCI, aims to improve safety of batteries through a multi-pronged approach that includes embedded sensors, nondestructive evaluation, and advanced modeling and prognostics.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Ultimately, this effort will give a higher level of insight into the operations and safety of cells within battery modules and allow highly informed decisions while these batteries are deployed in any application.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This effort demonstrated that sensors could be embedded into a pouch cell and provide useful, actionable data while the cell was cycling, including failure detection during off-nominal events. Several nondestructive evaluation techniques were demonstrated as very promising options for performing off-line monitoring of cell health via morphological changes in the materials&#39; properties. Modeling &amp; prognostics were advanced to include off-nominal thermal conditions and confirmed to detect an unanticipated anomaly during test operations. The SPARRCI project showed positive progress towards feasibility of a more finely-tuned, actionable, “smart” battery management system to help prevent catastrophic failures for next-generation batteries.
  • Transitioned To (Transformational Tools and Technologies): Some sensor work was transitioned to Transformational Tools and Technologies

The photo below those two photographs shows a sensor placed between separator layers. The sensor pads on the right side of the separator connect to barely perceptible thin gold leads extending beyond the edge of the cell. The photograph at the bottom right in the image shows the completed pouch cell with the thin gold sensor leads exiting the cell.
  • Resistive Sensors — Two sheets of thin white film are shown; these are the battery separators that go inside the battery to prevent electrical short circuiting. Printed directly on each separator sheet are four Titanium resistive traces. The traces have specific resistance signature depending on several factors and also include longer leads towards the bottom for taking measurements.
  • Analyzing the embedded sensor — This photo depicts the inside of a cycled pouch cell. Destructive physical analysis is a tool often used to analyze the components within a battery cell after cycling profiles. In this case, you can see a thick layer of gray, mossy lithium growth onto the separator and the area where the embedded sensor was is clearly visible. This was one of many experiments performed to determine the survivability of the embedded sensor in the battery’s electrochemical environment.
