Project Description

Whether a young Mars harbored microbial life remains unknown. The answer to this question would have ramifications for our understanding of the development of life on both Earth and elsewhere. Searching for life on Mars has been hindered by the difficulty of finding pristine samples that could contain organic compounds. The absence of a substantial atmosphere and global magnetic field has left the top 1-2 m of the planet's surface thoroughly irradiated, necessitating the capture of deeper samples for astrobiological research. Martian lava tube caves present an opportunity to access protected underground samples without the need for heavy and complex drilling equipment. Robust robots capable of traversing lava tube terrain could travel from tube entrances (skylights) and obtain protected samples deeper within the tubes. Technology capable of exploring Martian lava tubes could also be used to map them, as lava tubes are considered to be a possible location for human settlement on Mars. I propose to develop a novel robot design capable of exploring Martian lava tubes. While robots that may be able to navigate this environment exist, none are both small enough that multiple can be sent, but capable of complex maneuvers to traverse obstacles. I propose to design and prototype a 1-2 kg robot to meet these constraints. The starting point will be my research with the Stockton Group at Georgia Tech, during which I have designed a robot that implements positionable arms that pull and climb with continuous tracks. While this design will likely evolve with time, the end result will be capable of reaching pristine samples to perform in-situ analysis within lava tubes. The NSTRF fellowship would allow me to develop this early research into a robust and tested robot design.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Many of our extraterrestrial exploratory goals are not within the capabilities of current rovers; even state-of-the-art field robots cannot demonstrate robust mobility over terrestrial environments involving complex, unstructured terrain. Current approaches tend to emphasize computationally expensive methodology such as extensive visual processing or online trajectory optimization. Furthermore, with these approaches robots are still frequently unable to sense disturbances quickly enough to reject them before they can be destabilized. In contrast, animals exhibit far more robust mobility than our best robots. Animals efficiently process data from sensory capabilities arrayed across their bodies so as to directly sense forces and moments acting on them (rather than rotational rates) before they are significantly destabilized. By taking inspiration from how animals capture and process such extensive information about their environment, I am developing a framework for perception and control in which the same principles can be applied to our robots. I aim to be able to synthesize high-bandwidth feedback control loops for any platform with distributed sensing capabilities to improve its robustness in the field. This approach involves taking the spatial inner product of the distributed sensor measurements (as a function of their spatial location on the robot) with specially designed weighting functions in order to provide a few salient signals for use on feedback control. My research is therefore largely focused on the synthesis of these weighting functions to achieve various open- and closed-loop control performance objectives. This involves exploring various optimization methods and objective functions. I have developed several novel methods and objectives, and compared the results to existing techniques for designing distributed sensing systems, as well as to new data from the sensory systems of flies provided by collaborators. I have several papers in progress that discuss this work While I have so far only shown the effectiveness of these methods in simulated systems, in conjunction with various collaborators I am currently exploring physical demonstrations on the following platforms: UAVs with distributed strain sensors and accelerometers, a soft robotic orthotic device that adapts to its user’s activity, and an exploratory snake robot.
