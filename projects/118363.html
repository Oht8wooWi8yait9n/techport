Characterizing Quantum Devices Using the Principles of Quantum Information

NASA TechPort Project ID: 118363

Project Description

This proposal outlines ongoing and future work pertaining to the development and characterization of quantum devices. The suggested methodologies borrow ideas and rely on techniques from quantum information theory. Specifically, four projects are discussed: developing single-site rotations in a Penning trap as a scalable mode of quantum computation, enabling large-scale quantum optimization in a neutral atom trap, benchmarking quantum computers with a quantum error detecting code, and bounding the integrated quantum Fisher information for metrological protocols. The first two projects are experimental collaborations working towards quantum computation with hundreds of qubits. The last two projects work towards developing theory to benchmark quantum computers and build quantum sensors. The first project is using an electromagnetic trap to confine ions, and perform manipulations on individual ions in the trap. These in turn will enable the use of the trap as a quantum computer. The second project involves developing algorithms for solving combinatorial optimization problems using a classical computer in tandem with a quantum computer. This research considers the particular case of hundreds of qubits, with noisy and slow measurements. The third project is continuing to develop an error detecting code that can be used to encode one logical qubit into four physical qubits. The proposed work provides a method for demonstrating genuine quantum error correction, as well as developing benchmarks for noisy quantum computers. Finally, the last project discusses developing theoretical bounds on a quantity in quantum information theory. By deriving these bounds, we will be able to build quantum sensors that are maximally sensitive over a range of frequencies.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The goal of this project is to develop and characterize quantum devices, building quantum sensors that are maximally sensitive over a range of frequencies.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The research project, titled "Characterizing Quantum Devices Using the Principles of Quantum Information," examines the development and characterization of quantum devices, incorporating techniques from quantum information theory. This thoughtful exploration aims not only to promote new experimental approaches but also to generate theories that could potentially enhance future advancements in quantum technology. The central question that this research project aims to address is: "Is this a quantum device?" This query is manifested in quantum characterization, verification, and validation (QCVV), thereby inspiring a sequence of significant experiments to decipher this critical aspect. Seven distinct projects are the core of this research, focusing on multiple aspects of quantum computation and metrology. The first two projects probe into experimental collaborations that aim to enable quantum computation with hundreds of qubits. This achievement would represent a significant milestone in engineering and reflect our advancing understanding of physics. The subsequent three projects examine the demonstration of genuine quantum information theoretic phenomena. They not only propose a straightforward Bacon-Shor code that can be executed on current hardware, but also delve into quantum metrology, an aspect that improves classical methods significantly in scientific experiments. The final two chapters explore the broader goal of characterizing quantum circuits, shedding light on the understanding of how errors affect the working of structured quantum circuits. The thesis, hence, contributes to our understanding of quantum technology and presents significant steps towards their development and optimization. These outputs could influence practical transitions to industry and provide constructive inputs for NASA's advanced research and operations.
