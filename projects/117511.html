Flow Testing of Corrugated Metal Flexhoses to Evaluate Flow-Induced Vibration and Stiffness

NASA TechPort Project ID: 117511
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Corrugated metal flexhoses are used to supply fluid routing where straight rigid pipes cannot meet the design requirements due to vibrations, thermal expansion, or motion. These types of hoses are found in a wide range of applications at KSC (Exploration Ground Systems Program in particular) with various fluid commodities such as fuel, oxidizers, coolant, and cryogenics. However, flexibility of design comes at a price: due to increased levels of turbulence generated by the hose geometry, the necessary supply pressure must increase to achieve the same flow rate. Furthermore, the convolutes of the flexhoses interact with the flow field to generate areas of flow separation which leads to a phenomenon known as vortex shedding. When the frequency of vortex shedding couples with the natural frequency of the hose, this can be detrimental and cause premature failure. There are a limited amount of software that can correctly model the coupled fluid structure interactions (FSI) between the solid and fluid physics. This project aims to develop a computer model that can predict Flow-Induced Vibration (FIV) on flexhoses in one of the atypical configurations found in the State-of-the-Art (SOTA) standard for hoses in an angulated state.
Our research seeks to extend the literature and expand NASA’s FIV standard. The computational rigor and resources available in the Apollo Era did not allow for flow coupling between fluids and structure interfaces to evaluate FIV of the flexhose. Our team believes that modern techniques and methods of evaluation should be used to reevaluate and extend the database of FIV and stiffness properties of flexhoses to assess the risk of failure. The core performance criteria is a computer model that is able to predict the FIV frequency within 10%.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Our team sought to develop a proof-of-concept numerical tool using commercial software Simcenter STAR-CCM+ to evaluate Flow-Induced Vibration in a series of corrugated metal flexhoses. The target was to develop a computer tool that can predict Flow-Induced Vibration frequency within 10%; however, the tool was unable to consistently do so with 90-degree bend cases (atypical case), with a worst-case error of 32%. The team was successful in meeting core performance criteria in simple cases, but not in the complex case. There is future work planned to refine the numerical model to meet this criteria. Computational Fluid Dynamics models are highly sensitive to given conditions, so it is necessary to examine a wide range of geometries, perform turbulence modeling, and develop higher fidelity computer models. A higher fidelity model such as Large Eddy Simulation, Detached Eddy Simulation, or Direct Numerical Simulation should be used to further investigate for a more detailed understanding of the unsteady flow phenomenon. A high-performance computing cluster will be required to perform the analysis of these techniques.
