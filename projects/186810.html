RESIST: Radiation-Enabled Structural Improvement in Space Materials

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186810
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Sustained human and robotic operations on the Moon and Mars require reliable surface power systems capable of operating for years in extreme environments without maintenance or replacement. Nuclear-based surface power is a leading option, but its viability is limited by the long-term durability of structural and heat-transport materials exposed to high temperatures, radiation, and off-nominal thermal transients. I hypothesize that certain advanced dispersion-strengthened alloys, such as NASA's recently developed GRX-810, can maintain their exceptional high-temperature creep strength under irradiation through a mechanism termed Radiation-Assisted Dynamic Alloy Recovery (RADAR). At the elevated temperatures characteristic of surface power systems, irradiation is expected to drive continual dissolution and re-precipitation of nano-oxides into smaller, denser defect sinks, retaining sink efficiency, suppressing void swelling, and improving ductility. This dynamic recovery mechanism is anticipated to expand the temperature--dose regime over which these materials remain mechanically reliable, thereby improving surface power system lifetimes. To demonstrate RADAR, I will use a novel high-temperature (<1400 °C) irradiation stage developed during my first year of graduate study, in combination with the accelerated lifetime testing capabilities of the Michigan Ion Beam Laboratory, to irradiate GRX-810 under representative surface nuclear power operating conditions. Post-irradiation microstructural characterization will elucidate nano-oxide evolution, defect behavior, and swelling, and these results will be integrated with predictive modeling to define temperature-dose operating regimes. This work directly supports the development of low-TRL space power technology through (i) a validated model and operational map for GRX-810, (ii) a reproducible high-temperature irradiation protocol for accelerated material qualification, and (iii) microstructural datasets that support material down-selection and lifetime assessment while remaining independent of specific reactor architectures, supporting broader lunar and Martian exploration goals.
