Towards Dynamic Configuration for a Cloud Evolution Targeting Radar System (CETR)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 117116
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

We explore a novel scanning cloud radar concept for observing the evolution of low-level clouds and convective initiation in a targeted manner. Results from high resolution cloud model simulations will be used to perform a trade study to define measurement requirements and help inform design specifications of radar system with a metasurface antenna for electronic steerable beam scanning in two dimensions. A key goal of the study is to identify enabling technology to develop a new measurement capability involving a cloud radar, nominally operating at W-band (94 GHz), that scans in two dimensions while also providing near-surface radar profiles of boundary layer clouds.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

A future airborne campaign for the targeting cloud radar concept is an excellent candidate for a NASA EV-S proposal. With the acknowledgement that spaceborne sensors alone will not fully meet NASA’s Earth Science priorities outlined in the Decadal Survey, the targeting cloud radar could also be infused in AOS and PBL suborbital components.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This project significantly improved upon previous radar simulations targeting cloud and precipitation evolution through increased cloud model resolution and more frequent output, as well as considering antenna sidelobe impacts. A pattern with peak two-way sidelobe levels of -40 dB, the baseline for a scanning metasurface antenna currently under development, successfully minimized influence from off-boresight scatterers, although surface impacts remained. A Random Forest (RF) machine learning (ML) model was applied to the simulated radar scans to explore the ability to ‘detect’ evolution based on consecutive radar scans such that a future algorithm might flag a feature for autonomous tracking by the radar. Although the RF model showed some skill in identifying convective evolution over 10-30 s, the primary insights gained from ML task were the need for extended and diversified cloud lifecycle data along with employing a ML model better suited for spatiotemporal pattern evolution such as Convolutional Neural Networks or transformer-based architectures.
