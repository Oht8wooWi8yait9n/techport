Novel Aerogel-Based Catalysts for Spacecraft Life Support Application

NASA TechPort Project ID: 145941
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Identify 1-3 applications of aerogel-based catalysts (or sorbents) to ECLSS subsystems. Develop methods for synthesis of those catalysts. Test synthesized samples in the CO2 Reduction Catalyst Test Stand. Analyze performance, and recommend selected catalyst(s) for further development

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Researchers from NASA&#39;s Jet Propulsion Laboratory have developed a microgravity anchor and rock coring drill that uses a microspine anchor to grip the surface of a rock using 100s of sharp hooks. The forces and torques required to drill are redirected back into the rock through this anchor. The drill has successfully acquired rock cores in flat, vertical, and fully inverted configurations, and the microspine anchors have been used as feet on the world&#39;s first rock climbing robot. In a 2014 parabolic flight campaign, researchers demonstrated the drill aboard a parabolic aircraft to further validate the technology and characterize its performance in a microgravity environment. Specifically, the flight campaign allowed researchers to demonstrate the ability to: • Anchor to a high-inertia asteroid • Acquire a core sample from a high-inertia asteroid • Grapple a low-inertia rock sample The successful flight campaign resulted in data including anchor strength, misalignment angles, and drilling rates/loads. These data can inform the next design iteration of the tool. This technology could be used in a future asteroid redirect mission to grip large boulders or acquire core samples from rocks that are too large for the crew to transport back to Earth in their entirety. In the long term, the technology could enable the robotic exploration of lava tubes that have been observed on Mars and the Moon.
