Project Description

I am researching nano-magnetic logic (NML) because it has low power consumption, high density of computing and memory elements, CMOS integration capabilities, and reliability in the presence of radiation. The objectives of my research are to (i) to study and improve the reliability of NML with respect to radiation (ii) implement important algorithms in computer vision and signal processing with NML and (iii) look into different devices and their geometries to reduce the overall power consumption. The objectives are at least partially dependent on majority logic synthesis, which is the synthesis of Boolean functions using the majority operator. NML and many other nano-computing schemes depend on the Boolean majority operator rather than on the AND/OR perspective used to implement CMOS circuits. For fabricated devices, correct functionality can be determined by viewing them with SEM, MFM, or AFM microscopes. Objective (i) will be accomplished by studying the ability of redundant elements in a majority-logic-based Boolean network to reduce the probability that an input error results in an output error. Objective (i) will also be accomplished by simulation, fabrication, and testing of devices with varying geometries and materials. Variation of physical parameters may result in devices that are less prone to error in fabrication and less prone to suffering from radiation and thermal disturbances. The devices will first be tested for functionality, which depends on avoiding fabrication errors. The performance of the devices in high-radiation or high-temperature conditions will be tested subsequently. For objective (ii), the signal processing algorithms will be mapped to a majority-logic form that is suitable for NML. Some of the targeted algorithms are the Discrete Fourier transform and Viterbi encoding/decoding. The computer vision research will focus on using NML to perform non-Boolean computations of computer vision algorithms, and will be accomplished with simulation and fabrication of devices. Using NML in ways other than Boolean computation has been shown to greatly decrease the computation time of some computer vision algorithms, such as the recognition of parallel lines. Objective (iii) will be accomplished by studying the reduction of Boolean expressions that are based on the majority operator. Furthermore, because clocking is the main source of power consumption in NML, (iii) will also be accomplished by simulating the effects of different clocking cycles on NML. A good clocking cycle will require minimal power while avoiding the bi-directional information propagation that can occur with a poorly designed cycle. This research corresponds to the Nanotechnology roadmap, research area number 10, with significant overlap in research area number 11, the Information Technology and Processing roadmap. This research is crucial to space technologies because NML is known to be radiation-hard and low-power, two of the critical objectives of NASA’s Nanotechnology roadmap. Aside from these broad objectives, I believe that my research will particularly impact the development of micro-satellites, because these devices depend upon our ability to reduce the size of hardware. NML has a two-fold potential for reducing the size of devices. Firstly, NML has the potential for high-density fabrication. Secondly, the low power consumption of NML will allow us to reduce the size of components dedicated to powering devices. A final reason that this research will be beneficial to micro-satellites is that, for the first time, signal processing algorithms and computer vision algorithms will be implemented with NML. Since all satellites need to communicate with other devices, and since many satellites are designed for the purpose of imaging, such as in surveillance satellites and weather satellites, signal processing and computer vision algorithms are of special significance to micro-satellites.

  • Closed Out: This research was originally intended to focus on the development of reconfigurable magnetic logic using ferromagnetic shape memory alloys (FSMAs). FSMAs have strong coupling between thermal, magnetic, and mechanical properties. For instance, they can undergo a cubic (austenitic) to tetragonal (martensitic) phase transition when cooled, compressed, or when a magnetic field is applied. Furthermore, a purely tetragonal can undergo field- or stress- induced reorientation. This crystalline reorientation can also result in a change of magnetic preference. For instance, in the NiMnGa system, the magnetic preference is along the shortest crystalline axis. Unfortunately, early experiments on films and microstructures did not yield magnetization that could be sensed via AFM, and the research instead turned to focus on thin film properties, in the hope that good fabrication processes would result in better magnetic properties. In the first year of study, films were sputter-deposited and annealed in a vacuum chamber with 10-3 Torr pressure with nitrogen backfill. However, post-annealing AFM revealed large precipitates literally hundreds of nanometers high. Such large topography changes are unsuitable to nanostructures. Furthermore, the obvious chemical reactions affected film properties. This study did reveal large coercivity changes between annealed and unannealed samples. The general magnetic results are shown below. Coercivities as high as 450 Oersteds were found after annealing at 500 Celsius. After the initial film studies displayed unsatisfactory results, attention shifted to higher vacuum annealing. After the installation of a heating element in a sputter deposition system capable of reaching 3*10-7 Torr, the films were equally smooth before and after annealing, indicating that no significant surface reactions occurred during the annealing process. Films were also deposited with in-situ heating, however, the deposition rates were too low to build up enough material for magnetic characterization. It was also found that significant oxidation was affecting the films. EDAX results had showed high oxygen content before, but it was originally believed that this oxygen was mostly confined to the silicon-dioxide substrates. However, films with 2 microns of thickness were produced, and EDAX results no longer showed any silicon readings, but still showed oxygen readings, meaning that oxidation was still significantly occurring in the films. A deposition process with a longer bakeout process was performed, and this cut the oxygen levels in half, from approximately 20 at.%, to approximately 10 at. %. The film magnetic properties also significantly changed. A summary of film magnetic properties is shown in the tables below, and some discussion follows. The four tables above summarize magnetic information about four different films with hysteresis loops run at 6 different temperatures (50 Kelvin, 100 Kelvin, …, 300 Kelvin). The four parameters that are summarized are saturation magnetization, remanence, remanence over saturation, and coercivity. One trend that seems clear is that the magnetic properties of annealed films are more stable across the entire temperature range than unannealed films. For example, the coercivity of the annealed sample is much higher at room temperature than that of the unannealed sample. However, at low temperature, they are relatively similar. This suggests that the films have different coercivity mechanisms. Perhaps the unannealed film undergoes a phase transition that leads to much higher coercivity at low temperatures, whereas the annealed film gradually becomes more coercive as thermal disturbances lessen. XRD work on these films revealed relatively expected results, namely, the presence of the beta (austenitic) phase at the (110) reflection, as well as the gamma phase (a cobalt-nickel alloy) at the (200) reflection. The XRD results are shown in the figure below, for an annealed and unannealed film. The implications of these results are still being analyzed. There are fundamental questions about the coercivity and magnetization mechanisms in different films. Additionally, the integration of these materials into magnetic microstructures still needs to be investigated. This will be discussed in future plans.
