New Lithographic Techniques for X-Ray Spectroscopy

Project Description

The goal of my proposed research is to develop a procedure for fabricating high-resolution X-ray diffraction gratings using new techniques in electron-beam lithography. Gratings are the instrument of choice to obtain high-resolution spectra at soft X-ray energies, where a large population of spectral lines exist in astrophysical plasmas. Improving spectral resolution and throughput of spectrometers for next generation X-ray observatories is a direct technological need of NASA as outlined in the Space Technology Roadmap with Technology Breakdown Structure 8.1.3. This fabrication method will produce reflection gratings that push the state of the art for advanced spectrometers by realizing an idealized groove profile.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This research investigated advanced techniques in electron-beam lithography (EBL) to fabricate specialized reflection gratings for astronomical x-ray spectroscopy. The objective was to establish a new fabrication recipe for generating blazed groove facets over a custom layout to enable high spectral resolving power and high sensitivity in a telescopic x-ray spectrograph. This was accomplished by first establishing methodology for dose-modulated grayscale lithography in 125 nm-thick poly(methyl methacrylate) (PMMA) resist using a Raith EBPG5200 EBL tool and supporting software developed by GenISys GmbH. Using a resist contrast curve approach for dose modulation, repeating staircase structures of 400 nm periodicity were patterned in PMMA on a silicon wafer. By heating the wafer by hotplate with optimized temperature and duration, these structures were smoothed into a blazed grating topography through selective polymer thermal reflow. Known as thermally activated selective topography equilibration (TASTE) in the literature, this process is capable of producing a grating mold that functions as a reflection grating when coated in metal and also serves as a template for replication. As part of this research, TASTE results for sub-micron blazed gratings were reported for the first time. Additionally, substrate conformal imprint lithography (SCIL) was identified as a means for grating replication, which is especially beneficial for high-volume imprinting over large areas. Grating prototypes fabricated using TASTE and SCIL were tested for diffraction efficiency at extreme ultraviolet and soft x-ray wavelengths at the Advanced Light Source (ALS) of Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. Results demonstrate efficient blaze response but improvement is needed to maximize performance capability.
