Optics and Optical Coupling for Future CMB Missions

Project Description

Study of the Cosmic Microwave Background at large angular scales reveals information about the inflationary epoch of the universe and the formation of early structure during reionization. CMB experiments aim to produce precise measurements of the CMB at both large and small angular scales. These experiments must cover a frequency range spanning 30-400 GHz to separate foregrounds from signals to produce unambiguous measurements of the cosmological signals. I propose to develop optical and optical coupling technologies for future satellite based CMB missions including antireflection coatings for >300 GHz, antireflection coatings for sapphire, filters embedded in silicon optical elements, and feedhorns with high performance and for high frequencies. These technologies are all low TRL and can be fabricated using laser machining. The development of these technologies will improve the quality of the next generation of ground based CMB experiments, as well as raise the Technical Readiness Level of silicon optical elements for future balloon and space-based CMB missions.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Millimeter and sub-millimeter observations of the cosmic microwave background (CMB) and other astronomical objects provides a wealth of information about the history of the universe and the growth of large-scale structure. Developing optics that operate effectively at those wavelengths are essential to improve our understanding of aspects of the current cosmological paradigm like dark matter and inflation. The work supported by this award focused on the development of metamaterial anti-reflection (AR) coatings for silicon and alumina optical elements that permit sub-percent reflections at millimeter and sub-millimeter wavelengths. These coatings were fabricated using a custom-made multi-axis dicing saw with micron accurate stages. Using that machine, silicon optics were fabricated for the AdvACTPol, CLASS, Simons Observatory, TolTEC, and EXCLAIM experiments. The variety of bandpasses of those instruments demonstrate the versatility of metamaterial AR coatings, and the field-leading nature of those experiments show that metamaterial AR coatings are among the cutting edge. Alumina IR filters were also AR coated for the Simons Observatory using this approach and the resulting coatings have the lowest reflection across the largest bandpass of any metamaterial coating for alumina optics to date. All the AR coating efforts were at a production rate that can satisfy the largest demand of optical elements of any deployed experiment. The fabrication and testing of the metamaterial AR coatings done by this work has raised that technologies Technical Readiness Level to 4. Additionally, this award supported maximum-likelihood mapmaking efforts for the TolTEC experiment. That relied on the Minkasi maximum likelihood mapmaker that was developed by the MUSTANG2 collaboration. The results of that were maps of the Crab Nebula and MonR2 molecular cloud at field-leading angular resolution at 150, 220, and 280 GHz. That software has been tested with those commissioning targets and is now ready for science observations with TolTEC.
