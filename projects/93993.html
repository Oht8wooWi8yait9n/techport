Project Description

Augmented Reality (AR) & Deep Machine Learning (DML) are part of solution to support crew autonomy and surface operations at destinations with > 6 second time delay to ground. For an effective and sustainable operations, an integrated development for augmented reality must be available for the ops team to create and manage AR content quickly and easily. To achieve crew independence, DML has the potential to analyze a large number of problems such as medical image analysis, fault detection, or object identification. The approach is to embed DML based knowledge within eProc to provide autonomous guidance instruction cues to the crew. To integrate these two technologies (AR-eProc and DML) in order to provide the crew with both AR cues and expert guidance instruction during procedure execution.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This research is directly targeted to support Mission Operation community of Human Spaceflight programs. The technology is aimed to increase operation efficiency and to enable the operational team to create and manage augmented reality operational products including planning, execution and training on their own. The technology has potential to be adopted by organizations across many industries with complex and expensive maintenance tasks, requiring safety and accuracy. The proposed system can support the need to improve the development of advanced maintenance operating procedures. Industries include the aerospace maintenance services, Oil and Gas, military, and the automobile and heavy machinery industry.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The goal of this project is to explore the use of Augmented Reality (AR) and Deep Machine Learning (DML) technologies to support human exploration missions. Using the IRAD funding, the team has extended the current eProc (electronic procedures) development environment to integrate the AR specific authoring elements. These include adding the new representation language elements to identify an AR step, the engine communication layer with the AR device, the 3D polygon/CAD model of the target object to be recognized as well as the associated marker, and animations/cues to layer on top of the actual object during the procedure execution. Also, to integrate DML technology, the team developed tools that automate the DML training dataset development process by generating large sets of labeled images using computer graphics techniques. So, the DML based AR cues can be incorporated and improved the robustness of object recognition and tracking in a AR applications and ultimately improve AR user experience. The current TRL is at 4-5, where a system design has been completed, prototypes are being developed to assess feasiblity of the overall system performance.
