SuperSpec: A Revolutionary New Spectrometer for Submillimeter Astronomy

NASA TechPort Project ID: 91773
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

SuperSpec is a new spectrometer for submm astronomy. SuperSpec stands out from other submm spectrometers in that the detectors are coupled to a series of resonant filters on a single feedline instead of optics. SuperSpec uses kinetic inductance detectors (KIDs) to detect radiation in this filter bank. The natural multiplexibility, simplified manufacturing process, and small physical size of KIDs allow for the creation of a wide band 500 channel spectrometer that can be lithographically patterned on only a few square cm of silicon. This technology has the potential to transform the field of submm spectroscopy, allowing 100s to 1000s of spectrometers to be packed into a single 2D focal plane without the need for large cryogenic optical systems. The goal of our collaboration is to produce two R~600 science grade prototypes, one for the 1 mm (195-305 GHz) atmospheric window, and another covering the 850 Âµm and 650 Âµm (315-510 GHz) windows, and test these on a ground-based telescope (the Caltech Submillimeter Observatory or Submillimeter Telescope). The end result will be an increase in the Technology Readiness Level (TRL) from 4 to 5 through demonstration in a relevant environment. My personal involvement at the University of Colorado will be to lead the measuring of both the optical noise equivalent powers (NEPs) with our FTS and the dark NEPs with a cryogenic blackbody. In addition to this, I will also simulate and model the detectors and work to improve device designs. Finally, I will work to define the scaling requirements, for a future NASA mission, needed to meet the TRL 5 exit criteria. This would include defining requirements such as: how to decrease the NEP to the level of the background limited performance of a balloon-borne or cryogenically cooled telescope and how to move from our single device prototypes to a multi-object spectrometer.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: SuperSpec is an on-chip spectrometer developed for multi-object, moderate resolution (R = 300), large-bandwidth survey spectroscopy of high-redshift galaxies for the 1.0 to 1.5 mm atmospheric transmission window. SuperSpec targets the CO ladder in the redshift range of z = 0 to 4, the [C II] 158 um line from z = 5 to 9, and the [N II] 205 um line from z = 4 to 7. SuperSpec employs a novel architecture in which detectors are coupled to a series of resonant filters along a microwave feedline instead of using dispersive optics. This construction allows for the creation of a spectrometer occupying only a few cm2 of silicon, a reduction in volume of several orders of magnitude when compared to state-of-the-art grating spectrometers. This small profile will enable the production of future multi-object spectrometers and integral field mapping spectrometers. SuperSpec has demonstrated the feasibility and incredible potential of on-chip spectrometers via demonstration of fully functional on-chip spectrometers in the lab. Not only has filterbank spectrometer technology been shown to work but SuperSpec has also built and demonstrated some of the most sensitive kinetic inductance detectors to date. SuperSpec will further demonstrate the feasibility of this technology by fielding a science grade 3-pixel instrument on the Large Millimeter Telescope.
