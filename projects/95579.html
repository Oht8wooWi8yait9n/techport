Ultralight Lattice-based Materials for Multifunctional Space Structures

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95579
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The study is focused upon applications where large bending loads must be supported at the lowest possible mass. It proposes an investigation of the design, fabrication and performance of two ultralight cellular lattice topologies with combined high stiffness/strength and toughness. One topology is an octet truss lattice, the second is a cubic+octet foam. While the simpler to fabricate octet truss can meet NASA specific stiffness, specific strength and fracture toughness goals, recent theoretical studies have demonstrated that a cubic+octet foam topology can reach the upper bound elastic stiffness bound (an approximately 2 times lighter solution). This study will develop advanced manufacturing techniques which enable the scaled-up fabrication of lattices with the proposed topologies. By using high ductility, corrosion resistant materials, the program will also reduce their susceptibility to failure by time dependent degradation modes. They will reduce the mass of existing (1-m scale) 3D secondary space structures by more than 20%. The cubic+octet foam topology also offers space debris shielding, impact energy absorption and thermal (conduction, convection and radiation) protection functionalities.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To: Dr. Panzer's team brings a unique knowledge of the ins and outs of helmet testing and a deep understanding of the biomechanics behind concussion to NFL's Helmet Challenge. He is partnering with Dr. Haydn Wadley from UVA and Dr. Jonathan Berger from Nama Development— experts in the design and manufacturing of advanced metamaterials—to create a new energy absorbing layer in a football helmet that seeks to minimize the risk of concussion.
  • Closed Out: This grant addressed NRA Topic 7 on “Lightweight Multifunctional Lattice Materials”. It investigated of the design, fabrication and performance assessment of two ultralight metallic lattice topologies offering different combinations of multifunctionalities. One is an open cell, octet lattice while the second is a closed cell, octet plus cubic foam. Each of their mechanical properties are highly “tunable” and are to be designed to exceed NASA specific stiffness, strength and fracture toughness goals, and provided NASA with new options for ultralight space structures. Due to the exceptional mechanical performance displayed by lattices made from the octet plus cubic topology, the project goals focused upon implement strategies for the manufacture of metallic octet plus cubic lattice, and investigated the performance of sandwich panels with octet plus cubic lattice cores to current state-of-the-art honeycomb core concepts.
