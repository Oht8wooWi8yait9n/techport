Master-Equation-Informed Reduced-Order Model for Non-Equilibrium Thermochemistry of Air in Planetary Entry

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186791
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

Atmospheric entry remains one of the most challenging aspects of planetary mission design. Rigorous mission planning necessitates physics-based, predictive thermochemical models that can be integrated into multidisciplinary Entry, Descent, Landing (EDL) simulations. The proposed work advances such capabilities by addressing the key limitations in present state-of-the-art entry modeling. During atmospheric entry into Earth, shock-induced compression raises the temperature of the gas surrounding the entry vehicle and induces a state of thermochemical non-equilibrium. The excitation of the internal energy modes triggers chemical reactions such as dissociation, recombination, and nitric oxide (NO) formation. Understanding these phenomena is critical for designing mass-efficient thermal protection systems (TPS) that ensure the survival of the entry vehicle. The present highest-fidelity frameworks can resolve these effects, but their implementation in multidimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) simulations is intractable. Consequently, CFD calculations must rely on reduced-order models (ROM). Current ROMs are built on Boltzmann distributions (realized only at thermochemical equilibrium) and inherently do not capture the non-equilibrium kinetics. This introduces uncertainties that can only be mitigated by conservative design margins. It is proposed that an accurate and efficient ROM can instead be built based on the physically-realized non-equilibrium distribution, i.e., the quasi-steady-state distribution. A thermochemical model will be developed that captures the thermal non-equilibrium dissociation of nitrogen and oxygen, NO formation, and radical recombination under NASA-relevant Earth-entry conditions. This ROM will capture the accuracy of the highest-fidelity ab initio approaches at the cost of a one-temperature model. The key deliverables include explicit, analytical expressions for the reaction rate constants and rovibrational energy distributions derived from first principles. The model will be validated against high-fidelity simulations and experiments.
