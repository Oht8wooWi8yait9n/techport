hMSCs Cultured on Plant-Derived Tissue Engineering Extracellular Matrix in a Microgravity Environment

NASA TechPort Project ID: 91774
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

The objective of this proposal is to fabricate an all plant-derived renewable, biodegradable complete mimic of the bone extracellular matrix (ECM). For the first time in space research technology, plant-derived materials will be used to fabricate fully plant-derived mimics of the ECM as constructs to study the underlying cellular mechanisms responsible for the alteration of bone cells as a result of microgravity during space missions. Net bone mass is dictated by the relative activities of bone cells, osteoclasts and osteoblasts, both of which have been found to be affected by microgravity environment. In order to understand the underlying cellular mechanisms, in vitro cell studies are conducted in simulated microgravity environments, but appropriate constructs are needed. By supplying the bone cells with a biomaterial that closely resembles their native ECM, the variables affecting cell behavior may be reduced to microgravity alone. Plant-derived materials have natural properties and molecular structures resembling the constituent fiber proteins and the glycosaminoglycans in the native ECM; thus, the use of these materials to completely mimic the bone ECM appears appropriate. Preliminary data revealed that the materials and methods utilized to fabricate the constructs provided a suitable environment for human mesenchymal stem cells lineage (bone cell precursors). By creating tissue engineering complete mimics of bone ECM for in vitro cell studies in simulated microgravity environment, a better understanding of the effects that microgravity has on bone can be achieved. This is a step closer toward potential treatment for microgravity-induced bone loss.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Advanced From
  • Closed Out: We proposed to fabricate an extracellular matrix (ECM) mimetic utilizing plant-derived materials to enhance bone formation and mineralization by bone cells. Plant-derived protein with glycosaminoglycan (GAG) mimetics will be fabricated into a fibrous structure to support bone cell growth and function. Additionally, we sought to evaluate the effects of the fabricated scaffolds on bone cells under simulated hypergravity mechanical stimuli. Simulated hypergravity by centrifugation has been shown to increase osteoblast proliferation, enhance collagen matrix deposition and maturation, and promote stem cell osteogenic differentiation and mineralization. Thus, hypergravity has been previously proposed as a potential countermeasure for spaceflight-induced osteoporosis and/or for the stimulation of in vitro tissue constructs prior to implantation. We hypothesized that the GAG-containing scaffolds would enhance osteogenic differentiation of stem cells and bone cell progenitors in vitro. We also hypothesized that hypergravity stimulation by centrifugation would further affect cell behavior on the scaffolds. After successful fabrication of fibrous zein scaffolds containing plant-derived glycosaminoglycans (GAGs) mimetics, we found that these scaffolds significantly enhanced mineral deposition, particularly calcium, by bone progenitor cells onto the surrounding cellular microenvironment in comparison to the standard two-dimension tissue culture plate (TCP) controls. Overall, our data agrees with our selection of specific carrageenan types, which are the GAG mimetics, and in combination with zein fibers carrageenan also supported bone formation. The zein scaffolds with kappa-carrageenan enhanced bone differentiation markers in pre-osteoblasts, while lambda-carrageenan enhanced the bone differentiation markers in human mesenchymal stem cells. We sought out to find whether kappa- and lambda-carrageenans would have an effect on various bone cell progenitors based on their similarities to the sulfation of native glycosaminoglycans (GAGs). Based on the sulfation levels and position of the sulfate groups within its chemical structures, kappa-carrageenan could be a mimetic for chondroitin-4-sulfate found in mature bone, while lambda-carrageenan could be a mimetic for heparan sulfate found within the stem cell niche. We found that the bone marrow derived stem cells (hMSCs) performed significantly better on the scaffolds containing lambda-carrageenan (heparan sulfate mimetic), while pre-osteoblast cells performed better on zein scaffolds containing kappa-carrageenan (chondroitin-4-sulfate mimetic). Both observations agree with our initial hypothesis. Further, we intended to evaluate the effect of simulated hypergravity on our in vitro cell culture system for bone application. We found that simulated hypergravity can enhance the cell growth and osteogenic differentiation of cells cultured on fibrous scaffolds. Simulated hypergravity can also counteract detrimental effects that the surrounding environment, specifically, the extracellular matrix exerts onto the cells. The latter was observed on zein scaffolds containing 10% lambda, as 20-g conditions significantly improved the average cell growth and the expression of osteogenic differentiation markers on these scaffolds in comparison to the 1-g conditions.
