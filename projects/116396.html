Lunar Safe Haven Seedling Study (LSH)

Project Description

The Lunar Safe Haven Seedling Study has performed a trade study to determine what system(s) should be manifested in the Artemis campaign to protect crew and systems on the lunar surface from the harsh lunar environment (e.g., radiation). Also evaluated 15 concept options using decision analysis techniques. The study evaluated 15 concept options using decision analysis techniques. Additionally, Level Zero requirements as well as derived, system-level requirements have been defined.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This study has identified which technologies must be matured and what challenges to overcome (e.g., constructability) in order to build a Lunar Safe Haven. A new mission for the Lunar Safe Haven could be proposed to be added to HEOMD’s Artemis manifest, in time to protect the crew and exploration systems during the “Sustained Lunar Presence” phase.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: NASA’s Artemis program will send astronauts to the lunar surface for extended mission durations throughout the 2020s and into the 2030s, with a focus on sustainability and extensibility for Mars exploration. However, NASA must place more emphasis on protecting both the crew and the exploration surface systems if they hope to achieve long-duration sustainability on the lunar surface. It is now reasonable with excavation, construction, and autonomy technologies to achieve a significant level of protection that was not viable with architectures to date. The Lunar Safe Haven (LSH) was proposed as the first step to understanding how to protect astronauts, electronics, and other surface exploration systems from the hazards of the lunar environment, including radiation, micrometeoroid strikes, lunar dust, thermal extremes, and vacuum. HEOMD has declared development gaps in TX6 06-38 Radiation shielding, and TX12 12-07 Construction of Unpressurized Structure for Protection; both of these gaps are the focus of our Study. The goal of the LSH Seedling Study was to perform a trade study to identify best approaches to construct and maintain a safe haven shelter for sustained lunar missions within the 2020s. A multi-center team (LaRC, MSFC, and KSC) identified requirements for an LSH and performed a comprehensive trade study. The approach employed standard systems engineering practices with several technical interchange meetings (TIMs) for stakeholder and peer review along the way. The LSH study included firstly identification of Level Zero Requirements, which were focused on promoting synergy with other NASA Artemis exploration programs. Next, a decision analysis framework was baselined to evaluate concepts. The decision analysis framework included two pieces: stakeholder objectives and decision attributes. The objectives reflected what the stakeholders hope to achieve with an LSH, and the decision attributes reflected a quantifiable measure of how well the objectives were achieved. LSH concept alternatives could then be evaluated and compared in a quantifiable way using the attributes. Next, numerous alternatives for establishing and maintaining a safe haven shelter on the lunar surface were identified. The trade space included: 1. site preparation options and approach; 2. environmental protection concept options and subsequent technology needs; 3. implications of concept designs on radiation, thermal control and ballistic (MM) protection of the structure put under the Lunar Safe Haven; 4. structural verification; 5. Implications for autonomy for preparation, construction, and maintenance operations; and 5. lander payload capabilities and required transportation for the concepts. Fifteen concept alternatives were then down-selected for further development and evaluation using the decision attributes. The concepts were down-selected based on an initial assessment of their ability to respond to stakeholder objectives within the specified timeframe of the 2020s. Sub-teams of the LSH Study delved deeper into the design of the concepts, addressing the aspects of site preparation, surface construction, environmental protection, materials selection, autonomy, in situ resource utilization (ISRU), and long-duration maintenance. Finally, a final report was written to provide a thorough understanding of the trade space, requirements for sustained lunar operations, contributions from the community internal and external to NASA, as well as how to leverage existing and proposed NASA investments. Project Objectives: 1) Define technical objectives and perform functional decomposition for a lunar safe haven shelter 2) Perform literature review, collect ideas, and review current and proposed NASA investments with input from subject matter experts across the agency as well as from an industry, academia, and OGA forum 3) Develop a thorough trade tree by generating several concepts for a safe haven shelter 4) Evaluate the concepts for feasibility and merit using decision analysis techniques 5) Develop Level Zero requirements to promote synergy with future Artemis missions 6) Identify and recommend high-value lunar safe haven concepts that meet the Level Zero requirements 7) Develop derived system-level requirements that further guide the development of the recommended lunar safe haven concept 8) Write a trade study report which presents the trade study results, 1-2 concept recommendations, the derived requirements for the concepts, and how existing and proposed NASA investments can be leveraged to support development of the lunar safe haven prior to a 2028 Artemis mission
