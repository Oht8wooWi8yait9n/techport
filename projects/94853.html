Textural Pattern Recognition for Life Detection (TPR)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 94853

View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Identification of cells, colonies, and biofabrics at any scale, whether in hand sample (mm scale) or in a microscope (e.g, sub-micron), is a skill learned through years of extensive practice and generally a significant amount mentorship. Such finely honed skills allow the expert to differentiate organisms of interest from other inorganic materials that may have similar shapes and textures, but which are inorganic. The creation of a well-trained algorithm as a pattern analysis package to a flight-capable imaging instrument would allow quick identification and assessment of rocks and other surfaces that warrant further chemical analysis – an ability that could speed the cadence of investigation for flight instruments and will be necessary on life detection missions where close human supervision for every analysis is impractical.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The proposed pilot study expands on current efforts to catalog biologic structures, textures, and fabrics on surfaces and in geologic substrates by training machine learning algorithms to identify these unique biosignatures from nano-scale rock topologies without the aid of a human microscopist/biologist.

This is a non-traditional optical method of biosignature detection that compliments existing techniques for discovering life evidence on other worlds. This method can be used to dramatically reduce the time and resources required when determining high-value specimens for analytical interrogation and/or sample return.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: We successfully developed a research plan to train an automated machine learning system to detect biosignatures in fossils and rocks. We assembled and successfully imaged a number of specimens to be used in system training. We designed software for image sampling and began work on the machine learning system. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
