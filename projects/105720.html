High Data Rate C-band Telemetry System for Suborbital platforms

NASA TechPort Project ID: 105720
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This IRAD will develop a high rate (300 Mbps+) telemetry test system using the 4400 to 4940 MHz Aeronautical Mobile Telemetry band. The system will be used to evaluate link performance on high dynamic, suborbital applications such as sounding rockets. To date, maximum sounding rocket downlink rates have been limited to less than 50 Mbps using commercially available transmitters and range telemetry receivers. Significantly higher rate downlinks are needed to support the higher data volume generated by sounding rocket science instruments. The results from this IRAD will help identify and configure flight and ground systems that could be utilized on a rocket flight to evaluate impacts of vehicle dynamics and high roll rates on the link performance.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The development of the High rate telemetry system in the C-band RF frequency allocation will allow for a much greater bandwidth (>300MHz). These game changing data rates that have not been achievable before using existing sounding rocket technologies. With these high data rates, high bandwidth data such as high definition video or other high bandwidth data transfers will be possible.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: We successfully developed a high rate (300 Mbps+) telemetry test system using the 4400 to 4940 MHz Aeronautical Mobile Telemetry band. The communication test system has been assembled, configured, tested, and verified. This included the airborne Block downconverter (translating from X-band to C-band) and Power amplifier (PA) bench checks being completed and has been integrated into the communication system. The ground receive setup has been updated with inline attenuators and the ground receive downconverter for feeding the test rack. There is a plan to use the high data rate telemetry system on a sounding rocket which will allow for evaluation of a high data rate link under those flight conditions. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
