Adaptive Wire-Bent Compliant Attachment Aids for Small Satellite Docking and ISAM Applications

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186800
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

In-space servicing, assembly, and manufacturing (ISAM) missions increasingly depend on reliable rendezvous, capture, and docking operations conducted under uncertainty in alignment, dynamics, and target condition. These challenges are amplified for small satellites, where size, weight, power, and cost constraints limit the use of large, rigid, or highly redundant docking systems. Existing attachment mechanisms are typically pre-integrated and mission-specific, offering limited adaptability once deployed and requiring conservative soft-capture approaches to mitigate impact loads and risk. This proposal investigates in-space fabricated, wire-bent compliant attachment aids as a low-SWAP-C approach to augment small satellite docking interfaces. Fabricated on demand using a compact programmable wire-bending system, these structures provide geometrically programmable compliance, energy absorption, and alignment guidance to reduce impact loads and sensitivity to uncertainty without replacing flight-qualified hardware. The research addresses the NSTGRO26 technical area Advanced Attachment Methods for Small Spacecraft, with secondary relevance to modular ISAM construction. The proposed work develops predictive structural and dynamic models for wire-bent attachment aids, experimentally validates their load-mitigation performance through laboratory impact testing, and establishes design rules linking geometry, material properties, and fabrication parameters to docking performance. Collectively, these efforts advance the technology from conceptual feasibility (TRL 2) to experimentally validated performance in a relevant laboratory environment (TRL 4). The results provide foundational data and models to enable adaptive, system-aware attachment strategies for future small satellite docking and ISAM missions.
