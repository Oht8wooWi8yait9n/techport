Advanced Langmuir Probe for Measurements in high atomic oxygen environments

NASA TechPort Project ID: 157682
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This IRAD develops an Advanced Langmuir Probe (ALP) for flight on low perigee satellites carried out within high atomic oxygen environments such as Goddard’s proposed Atmosphere-Space Transition Region Explorer (ASTRE). This task consists of advancing Goddard’s existing Langmuir probe instrumentation to enable accurate swept I-V curves with minimum hysteresis in a highly atomic oxygen environment by optimizing the surface properties of the probe and by developing high voltage circuitry to enable electron “bombardment” to clean the probe surface in flight.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This work will advance our ability to take Langmuir Probe measurements in upper atmospheres dominated by atomic oxygen, including Earth and Venus.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This IRAD developed an Advanced Langmuir Probe (ALP) to gather accurate measurements of plasma density and temperature on low perigee satellites carried out within high atomic oxygen environments. High Atomic Oxygen environments in the earth’s lower ionosphere cause hysteresis in swept Langmuir probe measurements, degrading the plasma density and temperature accuracy. Based on previous laboratory studies in oxygen environments in 2014 by our group led by Rowland, we pursued AlTiN for the Langmuir probe surface and confirmed this can be manufactured in Langmuir probes in our lab. We identified a source of this material that we are procuring for testing and flight on two sounding rockets this summer. We designed a means to heat the probes using a thermal sleeve. The majority of our IRAD time was spent on this, including discussions with Wallops engineers regarding the optimum way to include the power for the thermal sleeves on our sounding rocket payloads. We explored in-flight cleaning with high voltages (+200V) as carried out in 1981 on DE-2 by GSFC. We worked with electrical engineers at GSFC to develop the electronics to include a HV “cleaning” mode in our instrument.
