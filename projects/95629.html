Project Description

The objective of this project is to investigate the use of electrospray thrusters for guidance and control of microsatellites such as CubeSats. A propulsion system can be created with electrospray thrusters that can provide both primary propulsion and attitude control for a CubeSat in less than 200cc of volume and 100g of mass. With such a system, CubeSats will have the capability for high precision (sub-arcsecond) pointing control and independent low thrust manoeuvres for rendezvous or missions to the Moon with volume for scientific payload. The research done as part of this project can also be extended to applications outside of the CubeSat form factor such as high precision point for the next generation of space telescopes. Two specific applications of electrospray thrusters are considered in the proposal: low-thrust trajectories to lunar orbit and high precision pointing. Both applications extend on previous work done in the Space Propulsion Laboratory at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology. The proposed solution for low-thrust trajectories is to match geometries of an orbit integrated forwards in time from Earth and an orbit integrated backwards in time from the Moon. High precision pointing will be investigated through improving a magnetically levitating balance to allow for more accurate state estimation and therefore more accurate attitude control. Completion of this work will provide impact not only for in-space propulsion technology but also for low-cost scientific research and technology development through providing a system that significantly increase the capabilities of the CubeSat form factor.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The primary goal of this project was to explore the application of electrospray thrusters for guidance and control of CubeSats and other small spacecraft during the exploration of planetary bodies. Aspects ranging from the propulsion architecture through on-orbit thrust characterization and trajectory design during proximity operations were explored. Specifically, this project analyzed a novel stage-based architecture for electrospray thrusters in order to bypass the lifetime limitations of individual thrusters and improve the overall lifetime of electrospray-thruster-based propulsion systems. In addition, methods for inferring the thrust output of the propulsion system based on measurements of the spacecraft’s position over time were developed and an analytical maneuver library for maneuvering of an underactuated spacecraft during proximity operations was derived. Finally, the use of electrospray technology for space systems concepts outside of propulsion, such as anchoring or levitating above an atmosphere-less planetary body, was proposed and analyzed.
