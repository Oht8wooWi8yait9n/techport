Revolutionary Vertical Lift Technology Project: Variable-Speed Power Turbine Technology Demonstration

NASA TechPort Project ID: 93071
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Demonstrate 50% improvement in efficient operational capability using a Variable-Speed Power Turbine concept. To enable this improvement, the rotordynamics mechanical challenges from operating across variable shaft speeds must be accomodated. Also, aerodynamics research for the engine high-efficiency turbine component design must be conducted over a wide range of incidence angle variation. The engine must be able to operate efficiently at low Reynolds number and the wake-induced unsteadiness within the component must be reduced. These technologies will be demonstrated in a component test conducted jointly with several partners.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Efficient variable engine rpm capability enables higher cruise and takeoff efficiency levels and reduces fuel burn.

Using the engine to efficiently vary main rotor rpm will dramatically improve speed capability and efficiency of conventional, compound and tiltrotor vertical lift configurations.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Brief Summary of Completion / Accomplishment: Technical Challenge: Variable Speed Power Turbine (VSPT) Demonstration Tech Challenge Exit Criteria: Demonstrate variable speed power turbine with 50% improvement in efficient operational capability Cascade testing defines the loss buckets for incident tolerant blade designs required for VSPT. Completed VSPT cascade testing at high and low inlet turbulence levels over a large range of Mach number, Reynolds number, and incidence to provide a benchmark data base to validate the ability of CFD analysis codes to predict the VSPT losses over the entire flight operating envelope. VSPT Design to meet the TC objectives and full scale VSPT test Rig Design completed. Joint US Army AATD / NASA VSPT component contracts awarded to Pratt & Whitney (P&W) and General Electric Aviation (GE). The P&W 4 stage VSPT design met the VSPT efficiency goals at 100% speed or maximum rated power (MRP) for vertical lift and a 55% speed or maximum continuous power (MCP) for cruise. The VSPT design efficiency exceeded 90% over the entire operating range. Successful completion of VSPT rig test achieves goals and demonstrated the potential of the VSPT concept as a viable approach to improve engine efficiency over a wide range of operating conditions. The full-scale VSPT 3 stage component rig was installed in the P&W Canada Continuous Flow Turbine Rig facility and tested over a wide range of operation. The facility emulated the engine inlet flow conditions to the VSPT compete with swirl and turbine main frame struts. Design and computational analysis tools validated experimental results. CFD was integrally used in the VSPT design process. Post-test analysis of the detailed rig data indicated the CFD had accurately predicted (pre-test predictions) radial distribution of the key flow field parameters, the vane pressure distributions, and the overall performance over the wide operating range.
