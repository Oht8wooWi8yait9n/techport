Micro-Concentrator Photovoltaics for Extreme Space Environments

NASA TechPort Project ID: 118395
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Modern space solar power systems use multi-junction cells that deliver high efficiency (~30%) at one-sun irradiance but suffer efficiency losses in extreme radiation and low solar irradiance and low temperature (LILT) environments. These radiation and LILT effects limit both the end of life power and distance of solar powered outer planet missions. To operate in extreme environments, conventional coverglass interconnected cells require thick and heavy coverglass. Furthermore, LILT missions necessitate time and cost intensive individual cell screening. We propose a new microcell solar concentrator to bypass these limitations, dramatically improving radiation tolerance and efficiency in LILT without comprising specific power, efficiency, or simplicity. The system uses microscale multi-junction solar cells bonded to a reflective micro-concentrator optic. To validate the design, we built a 1.7 mm thick proof-of-concept system which achieved a terrestrial power conversion efficiency of 25.8 ± 0.2% over a ±9.5° angular range, equivalent to ~111 W/kg specific power under AM0. Building off these results, we propose a space-optimized system with a geometric concentration ratio of 30x that is ultra-compact (<600 µm thick) and capable of >350 W/kg at >33% power conversion efficiency under AM0. The reliability and performance of this system will be demonstrated in simulated and real space and LILT environments. Success will position this technology for scale-up and enable solar powered discoveries beyond the range of conventional space solar power.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Success of this project's microcell solar concentrator will position this technology for scale-up and enable solar powered discoveries beyond the range of conventional space solar power.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This program set out to develop and demonstrate a new concentrating photovoltaic (CPV) paradigm for space photovoltaics. The designs explored within this program differentiate themselves from past space concentrators by using ultra-small solar cells with active areas less than 1 mm2. Scaling the cell to microscale dimensions allows for the associated optics to also be scaled down, therefore the entire microcell concentrator can be built as a thin, monolithic piece. Here, we explored the design space for this new class of space concentrators. We developed two systems and demonstrated both experimentally. Both are built out of glass, and, where possible, we used the same radiation resistant glass used in conventional space PV coverglass. Details on the derivation, fundamental limits, practical limits, optical, electrical, and thermal behavior were explored in detail by simulations and experimental testing. This program succeeded in building and demonstrating two different ultra-compact microcell concentrators for space. The first prototype was built with a 1 mm-thick glass mirror array paired with 650 µm square triple junction cells. The system provides 18x concentration and achieves 26% power conversion efficiency in outdoor testing with a wide 9.5° acceptance half-angle and a specific power of ~110 W/kg. The second prototype introduces a new concentrator tailored specifically for the demands of space. The system pushes the limits of what is possible for space CPV with a high 140x concentration ratio. In outdoor testing, the new concentrator recorded 30% power conversion efficiency with a 4.5° acceptance half-angle. Based off these results a pathway to > 36% efficiency microcell CPV with >400 W/kg specific power is identified that would provide an evolutionary step to space PV efficiency and a revolutionary reduction in cost.
