Project Description

This is the lead NIAC Phase II proposal for “10 meter Sub-Orbital Large Balloon Reflector (LBR)” with Christopher K. Walker as PI. We propose to develop and demonstrate the technology required to realize a suborbital, 10 meter class telescope suitable for operation from radio to THz frequencies. The telescope consists of an inflatable, half-aluminized spherical reflector deployed within a much larger carrier stratospheric balloon. Besides serving as a launch vehicle, the carrier balloon provides a stable mount for the enclosed telescope. Looking up, the LBR will serve as a telescope. Looking down, the LBR can be used for remote sensing or telecommunication activities. By combining successful suborbital balloon and ground-based telescope technologies, the dream of a 10 meter class telescope free of ~99% of the Earth’s atmospheric absorption in the far-infrared can be realized. The same telescope can also be used to perform sensitive, high spectral and spatial resolution limb sounding studies of the Earth’s atmosphere in greenhouse gases and serve as a high flying hub for any number of telecommunications and surveillance activities. LBR is a multi-institution effort between the University of Arizona (the PI institution), SWRI, JPL, and APL. LBR was selected in 2013 by the NASA Innovative Advanced Concepts (NIAC) program to proceed into Step B of the NIAC Phase I program. This makes LBR eligible to propose for a 2014 Phase II award. The goal of our NIAC Phase II effort is to bring LBR concepts to a Technology Readiness Level of at least 2 in maturity, by addressing key unknowns, assumptions, risks, and paths forward remaining after the completion of our Phase I study.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To: Follow on funding: NASA for Artic Naval Research Lab to develop orbital version of inflatable antenna. Developed Orbiting Astronomical Satellite for Investigating Stellar Systems (OASIS) and Nautilus mission concepts.
  • Transitioned To (Department of Defense): This project has received multiple follow-on grants and contracts from NASA and the DoD.
  • Infused To: Professor Walker used the NIAC-influenced technology to create an inflatable antenna concept for telecommunications, creating a new company called FreeFall Aerospace. In 2018, Freefall Aerospace successfully demonstrated their spherical antenna at a record-breaking altitude of 159,000 feet using NASA’s 60 million cubic foot stratospheric balloon .
  • Infused To: Students working in PI's lab created “CATSat” which is an inflatable space antenna, selected in 2019 for NASA's CubeSat Launch Initiative. To be launched late 2022 on Firefly!
  • Infused To (Goddard Space Flight Center): Goddard Space Flight Center (GSFC) is developing a Terahertz Spectrometer for in-situ Resource Utilization (THSiRU), a handheld technology, for water detection in lunar regolith potentially for Artemis missions. Several students from Walker’s lab at the University of Arizona are using their experience from developing CatSat to create electronics that will collect and transmit data from THSiRU to the lander.
  • Infused To (Goddard Space Flight Center): Collaborating with GFSC, FreeFall Aerospace, and Rincon Research Corporation, University of Arizona students are simultaneously developing a lunar-orbiting version of CatSat, called LunaCat, that will use a larger inflatable antenna to capture radio signatures of water vapor percolating off the surface of the Moon.
