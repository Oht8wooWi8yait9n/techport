Computational design of additively manufactured functionally graded materials for structural applications

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95611
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Additive manufacturing (AM) has an enormous opportunity to become a leading technique for manufacturing high-value, mechanically optimized parts. Functionally graded materials (FMGs) prepared by additive manufacturing enable the use of composition as a spatial design parameter that can be used to control the stable phases in a part as well as transition between dissimilar materials to tailor the properties of materials as a function of position. However, a major challenge in the development of FGMs is the formation of brittle intermetallic and TCP phases and phase transformations that cause strain and cracking during the build process. Due to the number of degrees of freedom in the composition path between two dissimilar multicomponent alloys, it is not feasible to design viable FGM gradient paths that avoid forming detrimental phases using experiments alone. A thermodynamic database that covers the entire composition range in the Al-Cr-Fe-Mo-Ni-Ti-V can be used to develop FGMs between common materials used in AM such as commercially pure Ti, Ti-6Al-4V, stainless steels, Invar, and Inconel alloys. The database can be used to design multicomponent gradient paths between these alloys including any interlayers to facilitate the transition. Large, multicomponent thermodynamic databases across different materials systems have not traditionally been explored using CALPHAD modeling due to the challenges with evaluating the energies of experimentally inaccessible phases and with the maintenance and improvement of large databases. First-principles calculations based on density functional theory can be used to predict the thermodynamic properties of stable and metastable phases including phases with large amounts of non-stoichiometry that are present in structural materials, such as the sigma, mu, or Laves phase. The open-source Python software ESPEI has been developed and used to automate the modeling of binary thermodynamic systems and will be extended to generate CALPHAD parameterizations for higher order systems in this work, which will allow for the rapid optimization and reassessments of large, multicomponent thermodynamic databases. The Al-Cr-Fe-Mo-Ni-Ti-V database that will be created in this work will be able to be used for designing FGMs and other advanced structural materials relevant to NASA. The further development of ESPEI will allow this database to be maintained and continually improved so that it can be used in simulations requiring thermodynamic properties or phase relations, such as phase field modeling.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Additive manufacturing (AM) of functionally graded materials (FGMs), where the layer-by-layer deposition process of laser-based AM can be used to design parts that have spatial variance in composition, phases and properties, has been of recent interest. FGMs could be used to transition between dissimilar metals, but a major challenge in developing FGMs has been the formation of deleterious phases that are often brittle and lead to cracking during or after the AM build process. Due to the combinatorically large number of possible composition paths to join two materials, it is not feasible to design FGMs using traditional alloy development techniques and the use Integrated Computational Materials Engineering (ICME) approach is necessary to design these materials. The CALPHAD (CALculation of PHAse Diagrams) method is a core component of the ICME approach and enables phase-based computational thermodynamic and kinetic calculations to be performed by semi-empirical modeling of the Gibbs energy, diffusivities, and other thermochemical and thermophysical properties. This project developed tools and methods to advance the state-of-the-art in thermodynamic modeling by incorporating Bayesian uncertainty quantification and propagation methods into the CALPHAD modeling process through the development of the open-source ESPEI software package, based on pycalphad. Through the critical analysis of FGMs using the CALPHAD method, we were able to validate the use of Scheil solidification as a design tool for FGMs, which led to a computational design criteria for creating viable FGMs. This phase-based design criteria driven by CALPHAD modeling enabled the prediction of a pathway to join Fe alloys to Ti alloys through an FGM joining SS316 to Ti-6Al-4V by grading though interlayers of Ni-20Cr, pure Cr, and pure V that was produced and shown to be free of detrimental phases.
