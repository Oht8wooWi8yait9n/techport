RNCP: A Resilient Networking and Computing Paradigm for NASA Space Exploration

Project Description

Efficient communications systems and effective computing techniques are crucial to ensure the success of each NASA mission. Considering the disrupted and wireless nature, efficient space communications require the improved automation, environment-awareness, and intelligence. To meet this requirement, this project intends to develop a resilient networking and computing paradigm (RNCP) that consists of two essential parts: (1) a secure and decentralized computing infrastructure and (2) a data-driven cognitive networking management architecture. Furthermore, we will evaluate the performance of our proposed RNCP in various space communication environments.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Contribute to the next-generation computing infrastructure. Improve the environment awareness of the space networking via data-driven method

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Efficient communications systems and effective computing techniques are crucial to ensure the success of each NASA mission. Considering the disrupted and wireless nature, efficient communication systems require improved automation, environment-awareness, and intelligence. Additionally, since space explorations produce a massive amount of high-dimensional data with complex features, effective computing systems demand decentralized and secure mechanisms to reduce the computation cost, ensure the security, and preserve the privacy. To address these challenges, in this NASA Early Career Faculty (ECF) project, we aim to develop an innovative Resilient Networking and Computing Paradigm (RNCP) by exploiting deep learning (DL), blockchain, decentralized learning, software-defined networking (SDN), and software-defined radio (SDR) techniques. This proposed project mainly consists of three cohesive research objectives: 1) designing a decentralized and cooperative computing architecture with high security and resilience. To achieve this objective, we have designed two secure, privacy-preserving, and decentralized computing infrastructures for autonomously processing massive data for NASA deep space exploration. We have also developed a domain knowledge-enabled weakly supervised learning method for enabling effective data processing and accurate decision making even when the sensing data are sparsely annotated. Additionally, we have also worked on enhancing the security of the proposed computing infrastructures by investigating the practical threat models and defense strategies of adversarial attacks; 2) developing a cognitive and resilient networking management architecture. To achieve this objective, we have developed blockchain-enabled SDR/SDN-based cognitive and resilient networking management architectures that consist of an intelligent decision-making scheme and a decentralized cognitive control scheme. Additionally, we have also explored the application scenarios of our proposed blockchain-enabled SDR/SDN-based cognitive and resilient networking management architectures by developing a resilient and intelligent CubeSat Internet Constellation infrastructure; and 3) realizing a hardware-in-the-loop (HITL) blockchain-powered decentralized networking and computing prototype for simulating, testing, and validating the performance of the proposed RNCP in different space communication environments. Our research achievements have great potential to advance the autonomy, environment awareness, and intelligence of NASA’s deep space network and to improve the resilience and scalability of NASA space communication system. Furthermore, there is a possibility to integrate the developed technologies in the NASA’s CubeSat Launch initiative, Artemis missions, and other NASA missions to demonstrate the cooperative, decentralized, and secure cognitive networking and computing architecture in space.
