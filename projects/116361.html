Project Description

The RedWater project investigated and developed a concept to extract water from deep ice deposits on Mars. It combined two terrestrial technologies into one: Coiled Tubing (CT) for making a hole and RodWell for melting/pumping water. The objective of the RedWater project was to combine CT and RodWell technologies into a single product that could be used to extract in-situ ice deposits in the near-subsurface of Mars. The contract was broken into two parts: a base period that focused on advancing the technology readiness level (TRL) of critical components (the tube, the injector, and the BHA) to TRL5; and an option period that integrated all of these subsystems together (along with a drum) to be tested to a TRL6 level. The base period of this effort focused on advancing the TRL of critical components (the tube, the injector, and the bottom hole assembly) to TRL 5. Experiments conducted on the prototype developed in the base period of this project yielded 2 liters/minute of water extracted from a borehole. The project integrated all these subsystems (along with a drum) and tested to TRL 6, in relevant environment in a temperature and vacuum controlled chamber. The project also conducted a review of the development and the results achieved in their TRL 6 demonstration on October 13, 2023. The hardware and equipment developed in this project is stored at Honeybee Robotics until it is needed by NASA. Elements of the development have already been infused into other projects developed at Honeybee Robotics for application on lunar missions.



Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

In the past decade orbital measurements revealed that a third of the Martian surface contains shallow ground ice. SHARAD revealed the presence of debris-covered glaciers as well as buried ice sheets in the Arcadia Planitia (30°N to 45°N) that are up to 170 m thick and nearly pure ice. The limit of the radar used to detect shallow ice deposits is 20 m, and therefore, the top of the ice layer is within ~20 m from the surface. To reach and extract water, a water-production-from-ice system would need to penetrate through at most 20 m of regolith. RedWater is developing technology for in-situ water production from subsurface ice deposits for Mars and potentially on the moon. The deliverable from this effort will be technology demonstrated to TRL 6 (in relevant environment) for production of up to 2 liters/minute of water from ice. The deep subsurface access component of this technology is also applicable for lunar missions.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: A final close-out meeting was conducted on October 13, 2023, at Honeybee Robotics’ Altadena, California facility to review the RedWater project. In attendance in person at the review were Hari Nayar (Project Manager) and Dave Eisenman. QuynhGiao Nguyen, the GCD Program Element Manager (PEM) for the project, and several other NASA GCD Program Office members including Dave Moore (Program Manager), Elizabeth Taylor (Deputy Program Manager), Mark Thornblom (Deputy Program Manager for Integration), Chris Kuhl (Chief Engineer), and David Goggin (Chief Engineer Staff Member), attended the review remotely. The concept at the beginning of development was at TRL 4. The milestone achieved at the conclusion of the project was TRL 6 demonstration of the Redwater concept in relevant environment. The Honeybee Robotics RedWater Team successfully integrated the RedWater system components and set up the demonstration and evaluation of the TRL 6 version of the system. The RedWater concept combines coiled tubing technology (used in commercial drilling) with Rodriguez Well (used in Antarctica for water extraction) technology into a single product that can be used to extract water from in situ ice deposits in the near-subsurface of Mars. An ice block tower was built using pre-frozen approx. 20x20x9in sculpture ice blocks lifted and fused on top of one another. This process involved using a warm plate to flatten the side of each block that is being fused, also creating a small film of meltwater. Seven blocks were fused together creating a final tower height of roughly 60 inches. The large and relatively heavy Redwater system was positioned next to the fused ice block tower and methodically lifted onto the lower structure using the overhead chain hoist in the freezer tower and a 4-legged sling attached to each corner of the Redwater top structure. The end-to-end test was completed in October 2023. POCs: LaRC, QuynhGiao Nguyen (216) 433-6073, JPL, 347F/H. Nayar, (818) 393-2505
