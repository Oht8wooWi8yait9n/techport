Shape Memory Alloys for Regulating TCS in Space (SMARTS-TP)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 116383
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Paragon Space Development Corporation worked with JSC and GRC using a shape memory alloy driven morphing radiator to develop an environmental control and life support system as well as a thermal control system for lunar missions that maintain acceptable operating temperatures throughout the Moon’s day and night cycle. The design of these systems could be adapted for crewed missions to Mars. By providing a passively controlled high turndown ratio radiator capability, the SMARTS system significantly reduced the complexity and mass required in comparison to competing technologies.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

By providing a passively controlled high turndown ratio radiator capability, the SMARTS system significantly reduces the complexity and mass required compared to competing technologies. Overall mass reductions for a pumped loop thermal control system may be reduced by up to 25% by enabling single-loop thermal management for crewed vehicles.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The Shape Memory Alloys for Regulating TCS in Space (SMARTS) project employed shape memory alloys (SMAs) to provide heat exchanger regulation in response to cooling loop temperatures, requiring no external power for actuation or control. The SMARTS project was a multi-organizational team of researchers, led by Paragon Space Development Corporation (Paragon) and collaboratively developed by Texas A&M University (TAMU), Boeing, and NASA Glenn Research Center (NASA GRC) as a NASA Tipping Point Project, funded by the Game Changing Development Program. This work successfully demonstrated the SMARTS technology design, analysis, and functionality, operating inside a representative thermal environment, simulated by TVAC testing. The design and analysis demonstrate the progression of incorporating SMA into thermal control systems, tuning SMA parameters to meet mission needs. While the project stretch goals and idealized performance was not achieved, SMARTS met the ambitious turndown goals provided by NASA’s 2015 roadmap, achieving a turndown of 8.9:1, greater than the 6:1 requirement. SMARTS also exceeded the heat rejection requirement of 265 W/m2 , demonstrating >290 W/m2 in the 5-panel configuration and >300 W/m2 in the single-panel configuration. The project’s work demonstrates faithful progression of the SMA morphing radiator predecessors, achieving greater turndown and higher degrees of closure than previous iterations. Additionally, thermal switch technology using SMAs was demonstrated in an integrated radiator system that not only provided turndown heat rejection, but protections to adverse heating environments. As noted above, the SMA materials developed for this project demonstrate the SMA technology advancements to alloy, heat treat, and train SMA materials for tight temperature needs and minimal hysteresis. With tunable SMA properties, SMARTS technologies are adaptable and tunable to many mission needs and temperatures.
