Laser Guide Star for Large Aperture Segmented Space Telescopes

NASA TechPort Project ID: 92006
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Astronomers plan to use very large telescopes to take pictures of exoplanets, which are hard to see next to their bright parent stars. High contrast (<10^-10) coronagraph instruments onboard the telescope help by blocking the parent starlight so the exoplanets can be imaged. Exoplanet spectra, images taken over many wavelengths, provide information about the planet's composition and habitability. To achieve high contrast, wavefront sensing and control techniques need to be used. Natural guide stars nearby the exoplanet system are typically used to set up the wavefront sensing and control, but many natural guide stars do not provide enough signal to run the wavefront control loop fast enough to overcome instabilities and disturbances in the system. We propose to study using a space telescope architecture with external small satellite laser guide stars paired with the large telescopes to act as the reference sources. This would reduce the need design and build large telescopes with picometer stability, which would be needed if only natural guide stars were used. We will study high contrast imaging performance using the external small satellite laser guide star, as we relax the stability requirements on the large telescope.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Reduce cost and complexity of large aperture segmented space telescopes. Improve stability. Increase ability to do high contrast imaging. Also benefits photometric calibration. Results relevant to large space telescope study teams.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The objectives of this work were to: Assess how well laser guide stars can improve the wavefront stability of large aperture segmented space telescopes compared with natural guide stars by increasing the number of photons/second available to the wavefront sensing and control system, increasing its bandwidth. •Evaluate whether the performance improvement is sufficient to relax manufacturing and assembly tolerances on the space telescope, and if so, by how much. •Determine what laser guide star mission architectures work best for a large segmented aperture exoplanet direct imaging telescope and whether these are currently feasible. •Consider using the laser guide star beacons as photometric calibration sources and capture any additional requirements on the laser guide stars to enable their use as calibration sources. •Perform a design reference mission study both with and without the laser guide star capability and determine whether the laser guide star enables more targets to be reached at higher contrast or with shorter integration times. •Develop more detailed plans for simulation and prototyping based on initial analyses, advancing TRL for the concept from TRL entry at 1-2 to TRL exit at 3-4. Relevance: This work falls under Topic 5: Advanced Telescope Architecture Technologies and Optical Components. We address the need for improved wavefront stability for large aperture segmented space telescopes. Approach: This work involved both simulation and experimental efforts. The simulation effort consists of two parts: 1) modeling of segment disturbances, control, and radiometric link budget and 2) modeling of the laser guidestar system architecture using multiple small satellite laser guide stars. The experimental effort consisted of a hardware testbed built at the University of Arizona to test the use of Zernike Wavefront Sensors (ZWFS) and a conjugate segmented deformable mirror to the primary with a laser guidestar system. Results: Results from simulation part 1) were published in the Astronomical Journal, Douglas et al., “Laser Guide Star for Large Segmented-aperture Space Telescopes. I. Implications for Terrestrial Exoplanet Detection and Observatory Stability,” and a second paper is expected on the system architecture by Clark et al. with submission before June 2020, along with a dedicated Ph.D. thesis summarizing the work on this topic. Experimental results showed that it was possible to use a Zernike Wavefront Sensor and conjugate segmented mirror in the wavefront sensing and control relay. Efforts to evolve this work into a NASA SAT have not yet been successful, but continued search for support is ongoing. This work is not only relevant to large segmented aperture space telescopes, it is also applicable to large segmented aperture ground telescopes, and should be seriously considered for an Earth demonstration. TRL was increased from 1-2 to 2-3.
