Project Description

Space Policy Directive 1 directs NASA to return to the Moon. NASA is joined by an international community yearning to perform science and commercial activities in the developing lunar ecosystem. This forms a robust market as evidenced by Astrobotic's $3B sales pipeline for lunar payload delivery. The NASA Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) program has stimulated the expansion of the US economy towards the Moon with regular lunar delivery services, however, there remains a gap in surface mobility services. A substantial portion of this market demands rovers of their own or rovers as a platform to host small mobile payloads. There are currently no US small-scale commercial rover offerings. The goal of the CubeRover Tipping Point Project is to further develop Astrobotic's CubeRover as a small-scale platform to meet this world-wide demand for mobility as a service.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The CubeRover is a first of its kind ultra-light, modular, and scalable commercial rover. Combined with commercial payload services available in CLPS, it represents a low-cost onramp to the Moon for small space programs around the world. CubeRovers contain all the subsystems necessary to provide payloads with mobility, power, communications, and protection from the lunar thermal environment. They utilize high TRL components and are currently undergoing full system flight qualification. CubeRover is designed around the form factor standardized by the CubeSat market to take advantage of heritage components and standard interfaces.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The NASA Commercial Lunar Payload Services (CLPS) program has stimulated the expansion of economic opportunities for the Moon with planned regular lunar delivery services; however, there remains a gap in surface mobility services. A substantial portion of this market demands dedicated rovers or rovers as a platform to host small mobile payloads. There is currently limited US, small-scale, commercial rover offerings. As a result of the NASA 2020 Tipping Point selections, Astrobotic developed the CubeRover, a small-scale platform to meet this world-wide demand for mobility as a service. The CubeRover is a first of its kind ultra-light, modular, and scalable commercial rover. As part of this tipping point contract, Astrobotic enhanced and matured the CubeRover design, initiated under a 2017 Phase I SBIR contract, that included developing a CubeRover Engineering Model and a Proto-flight Model capable of interfacing generic payloads and satisfying vacuum and launch conditions per NASA GEVS. The new design included enhancements to the chassis, wheels, electronics, and software that successfully completed testing, which was targeted to satisfy payload needs for polar missions. An interface control document and payload user guide were created to facilitate the integration of payloads with the CubeRover. A universal deployment system has also been developed and is under patent approval. The integration of JPL developed image compression software was implemented for Astrobotic to optimize data transfer and retrieval of images. The CuebRover Tipping Point contract enabled key developments and maturity advancements making the CubeRover platform available for NASA and the global space community. The CubeRover was created to support diverse scientific and commercial missions. CubeRovers will serve as standardized platforms to democratize planetary surface mobility, analogous to the transformation that CubeSats brought to the domain and economics of Low Earth Orbit. The CubeRover product line leverages the internationally recognized CubeSat sizing method to define the payload volume and carrying capacity, where a 10 cm X 10 cm X 10 cm volume that supports 1 kg of payload is called a unit and is abbreviated as “U.” Accordingly, the 2U CubeRover has a payload volume of 20 cm X 10 cm X 10 cm and supports 2 kg of payload. The 4U and 6U CubeRovers grow in size and carrying capacity with their respective “U” designation and contain nearly all the same electrical, thermal, and mechanical components as their smaller 2U counterpart.
  • Infused To: Companies, governments, universities, non-profits, and individuals can send payloads to the Moon on a CubeRover.
