Design, Fabrication, and Control of a Robotic Gripper Powered by Compliant and Self-Sensing Twisted String Actuators

NASA TechPort Project ID: 118372
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Compliant and high-performance robotic grippers have strong potential to support NASA's missions -- they can be mounted on rovers to effectively collect samples in space or safely interact with astronauts on the International Space Station (ISS) due to their inherent compliance. However, existing grippers powered by soft and compliant actuation mechanisms are predominantly weak and slow, and rigid and hard actuator-driven grippers are often heavy and bulky. With its large translational force, power density, energy efficiency, and muscle-like compliant structure, the twisted string actuator (TSA) holds exciting promise for robotic grippers and manipulators. We recently created a compliant and self-sensing TSA that exhibits high-force, high-bandwidth output by incorporating stretchable and conductive super-coiled polymer (SCP) strings into TSA. In this project, key innovations will be created to further enable a compliant and high-performance TSA-driven gripper promising for space applications by (1) conducting the first characterization and comparison study to confirm the strong promise of the proposed compliant TSAs over conventional TSAs and electromagnetic motors,(2) designing a compliant and self-sensing TSA-powered high-performance gripper, (3) fabricating and testing the developed gripper, and finally, (4) proposing and experimentally validating position and velocity control of the TSA-driven gripper using self-sensing based feedback signals.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This scope of this research was within the fields of robotic actuation and robotic manipulation. Challenges have historically arisen in generating sufficient force using a robotic actuator while maintaining low mass and volume. One way to generate high forces within a compact form factor is by using a twisted string actuator. In this mechanism, a motor twists two strings. The twisted strings convert rotational motion to linear motion with high force amplification (albeit a high reduction in speed). In the academic literature, this mechanism is known as a twisted string actuator (TSA). During the academic year, three main research tasks were completed. TSAs were studied to (1) optimize their design and component selection, (2) improve their control performance, and (3) develop an anthropomorphic soft robotic gripper. During the visiting technologist experience (VTE) at NASA Johnson Space Center (JSC), two research tasks were completed. Firstly, TSAs were directly compared to the tendon-based actuators that drove the end-effectors in NASA’s Valkyrie and Robonaut 2 hands. Secondly, a novel “multi-mode” robotic gripper was fabricated. The multimode gripper was in collaboration with researchers at the Harvard School of Engineering and Applied Sciences, who designed and tested the gripper within their research labs.
