A Microbial Characterization System for Advancing Planetary Protection Bioburden Assessments

Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This project will implement a system for characterization and study of microorganisms detected on Planetary Protection-sensitive flight-hardware at Goddard Space Flight Center (GSFC).

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This project represents the start of a targeted approach to cleaning Planetary Protection (PP) sensitive hardware that evaluates the metabolic processes and the inherent nature of the microorganisms.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This IRAD prepared the GSFC Planetary Protection (PP) lab to implement metagenomic DNA sequencing capabilities. Metagenomic sequencing will support success of upcoming missions by advancing PP bioburden assessments and providing deeper data on cleanroom microbiomes, as not all microorganisms can be grown in the lab. In preparation for this effort, and in collaboration with JSC, DNA sequencing of a small fragment of DNA was performed for all microorganisms in the GSFC PP archive. This effort achieved at least genus-level identification for all archived organisms. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies. Goddard Space Flight Center’s IRAD program is managed under its Office of Chief Technologist. Activities develop concepts, reduce technology risk, and advance human capital and technological capabilities. Goddard’s IRAD program is highly competitive, opportunity-driven, and 100% strategically aligned with NASA's and Goddard's strategic priorities. A significant portion of the program is focused on Early Stage Innovations for high-risk, strategically aligned, potential high-payoff technologies that are longer-term or lower TRL.
