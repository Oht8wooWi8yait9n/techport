Quantum Computational Biology: A Quantum Computing Approach to Hard Computational Problems Related to Origin of Life and Synthetic Biology

NASA TechPort Project ID: 145951
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

The origin of life is one of the most fundamental problems in science and a key research topic within the NASA Astrobiology Institute at Ames. Also, the problem of learning gene interaction networks from gene expression data is appealing to NASA's and the Nation's Synthetic Biology Initiative. In both of these vast emerging fields, several open questions remain unanswered. Many of these unknowns require computational power that significantly exceeds the capabilities of even the most powerful supercomputers available today or expected in the next decade. Quantum Computation is a promising approach to over-come these limitations, given its radically different way of processing information by using quantum mechanical effects to more efficiently explore the solution space of these intractable problems also known as NP-hard.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Synthetic biology and genetic engineering are disciplines at the core of solutions needed towards a sustainable bioeconomy. By providing a novel quantum technology capable of going beyond the reach of the available tools from computational biology and bioinformatics, our research will hopefully catalyze the needed breakthroughs in biological research to mitigate the Nation's needs. This proposal will also leverage NASA's in-vestment toward our leadership in quantum technologies and place our Center in an advantageous position as a pioneer in this new field of Quantum Computational Biology.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To
  • Closed Out: There are currently no mass measurement devices available on the International Space Station (ISS) that can accurately measure objects with a relatively low mass, or objects sensitive to accelerations, such as live animal specimens. Ground-based biology (plant and physical science) researchers use mass measurements as a fundamental data parameter. In order to compare microgravity research results with experiments conducted on the ground, mass measurement capabilities are essential and must be provided in the space laboratory setting. Experimental procedures are restricted without an appropriate mass measuring device, one of the most basic instruments in any scientific laboratory. The Zero Gravity Mass Measurement Device (ZGMMD) has been developed to quantify the mass of objects, including live animal specimens, in the microgravity environment onboard the ISS. In parabolic flight demonstrations conducted in 2016, researchers collected mass measurement data to evaluate system performance in microgravity, and detailed notes to document operation of the system for subsequent evaluation of human factors in microgravity. Researchers expect the flight testing to aid in refinement of the system design to optimize performance for application in the microgravity environment. Future applications include biological research in orbital space, such as current rodent research being conducted on the ISS.
