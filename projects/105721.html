Sounding Rocket Flight Computer Flight Software Baseline

NASA TechPort Project ID: 105721
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The objective of this project is to develop a flight software (FSW) baseline for a sounding rocket flight computer in order to consolidate functionality, reduce sounding rocket sub-systems, and ultimately save mass – improving performance & science return.

We will investigate the use of the Galois Recorder (previous IRAD project) or SpaceCube Mini-Z as a flight computer to host the flight software to support the consolidation of functionality in an effort to reduce sub-systems and ultimately mass from existing heritage sounding rocket hardware. A trade study will be conducted on the two options. Additionally, a Field Programmable Gate Array (FPGA) from a commercial vendor can be utilized to host a heritage set of core Flight Executive (cFE) and core Flight System (cFS) applications. A preliminary sounding rocket FSW roadmap will be developed. Custom hardware applications could also be developed in the future with additional investment.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Currently, the sounding rocket architecture includes flight computers and software in each subsystem, resulting in multiple instances of each and requiring a lot of ground support equipment. This project opens the possibility and develops plans for sounding rockets to have a single flight computer to consolidate functionality via a common flight software (cFE/cFS) and simplify ground support equipment. The flight computer and FSW technology advances one of the Sounding Rocket Program Office’s (SRPO) development priorities to miniaturize and modernize existing sounding rocket systems by consolidating functionality (therefore reducing mass) in an effort to increase performance and capabilities of current sounding rocket vehicles. By reducing mass and gaining additional vehicle performance, more science data can be collected on each sounding rocket flight, benefiting the entire science community of sounding rocket users.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The goal of this IRAD was to demonstrate a viable flight computer option for sounding rockets. The team identified the commercially available Raspberry Pi (RPi) Compute Module 4 (CM4) as an affordable option that meets requirements. During this IRAD the team successfully demonstrated key capabilities of the RPi CM4. This IRAD has generated interest from other suborbital platforms like NASA scientific balloons. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
