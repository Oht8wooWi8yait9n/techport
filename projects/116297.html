Lunar Lander Advanced Nozzle (LAN-ACO)

Project Description

Blue Origin established a partnership with Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC) and Langley Research Center (LaRC) to evaluate and mature high-temperature liquid rocket engine nozzle material technologies under an Announcement of Collaboration Opportunity (ACO). This ACO, through a non-reimbursable Space Act Agreement, executed parallel paths for coupon level testing and sub-scale nozzle testing in a high-temperature, reduced oxygen environment to accelerate development and provide early risk reduction of rocket engines. This joint effort leveraged Blue Origin’s experience in propulsion and material development and NASA’s expertise with high-temperature materials and nozzle testing in a relative hot fire test environment. This technical effort took place from September 2019 through March 2022.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The activity reduced the risks for the first high-temperature mid- scale nozzle test on rocket engines by testing coupons and subscale hardware at elevated temperatures and in a reduced oxygen environment. This development also benefits both NASA and industry knowledge of high temperature composites for nozzles. Potential benefits include:

  • A 50% reduction in mass (weight) versus that of comparable metallic or ablative nozzle extensions.
  • Improvement in thermal margins versus that of comparable metallic nozzle extensions.
  • Reductions in overall costs are possible with composite nozzles
  • Futher increase in capabilities by using state-of-the-art coatings and mixed and/or inhibited matrices could lead to higher thermal performance.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Blue Origin established a partnership with Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC) and Langley Research Center (LaRC) that evaluated and matured high-temperature liquid rocket engine nozzle material technologies under the LAN ACO. This joint effort leveraged Blue Origin’s experience in propulsion and material development and NASA’s expertise with high-temperature materials and nozzle testing in a relative hot fire test environment. This technical effort took place from September 2019 through March 2022 and was focused on five primary tasks: 1) Definition of requirements and material/supplier recommendation(s); 2) Coupon level material testing and analysis; 3) Design and test method support; 4) Sub-scale nozzle testing and analysis and 5) Pre- and post-test evaluation of a mid-scale nozzle. Blue Origin provided the requirements for a lander rocket engine to NASA, the material coupons for material testing and three nozzles used in the hot fire test series performed at test stand 115 at MSFC. The material coupon testing was initially planned to be performed at LaRC but due to Covid-19 impacts the testing was done at NIAR. LaRC consolidated the results into a final report for all of the material coupon level testing. MSFC provided pre-test analysis of the nozzles that included taking pictures, weighing and NDE. The hot fire testing was conducted at MSFC from May 18, 2021 to June 2, 2021. MSFC provided all the other hardware for the test including a chamber, injector and one additional C-C nozzle. The hot fire testing provided an appropriate thermal environment on nozzles. The NASA supplied subscale nozzle was initially tested to evaluate injector streaking followed by three Blue Origin supplied nozzles. All four of the nozzles were tested and several nozzle materials were identified as high performing lasting for numerous restarts, for hundreds of seconds of duration, and with wall temperatures exceeding 3000° F. A final report of the material coupon testing was completed by LaRC titled “Coupon-Level Mechanical Test Results for Lunar Lander Advanced Nozzle Game Changing Development Program Announcement of Collaboration Opportunity (LAN GCD ACO) Test Report of Mechanical Testing of Carbon-Carbon Composites”. A final report on the hot fire test series was completed by MSFC titled “Test Summary Report for Test Programs PK111 Blue Origin Uncooled Subscale Nozzle Material Testing. Both reports were delivered to Blue Origin. Blue Origin and NASA successfully completed all the tasks established in the Space Act Agreement (SAA) and met both party’s objectives under this partnership.
