Overcoming Kinetics Limitations in Materials And at Interfaces for Low-temperature, High-energy Batteries

Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

This research project is focused on developing high-capacity electrodes for use in batteries at temperatures as low as -80C, which will enable the creation of rechargeable batteries with higher energy density and specific energy suitable for use in space applications. The primary objectives of this research are to understand how high-capacity electrodes dynamically evolve under varying temperatures and in different electrochemical environments, and to control the chemistry and structure of electrochemical interfaces for improved kinetics at low temperatures. This research will be carried out via the use of electrochemical investigations, physical/chemical characterization of bulk materials and interfaces, and the development of new interfacial coatings. The knowledge generated by this project will establish the scientific and technological foundation for high-capacity electrodes with fast kinetics and improved electrochemical cycling stability at low temperatures.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Understand and control morphological transformations and electrochemical kinetics within high-capacity electrodes for low-temperature secondary batteries.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The overall objective of this research is to understand and control the morphological transformations and electrochemical kinetics of high-capacity lithium metal electrodes operating at low temperatures in secondary batteries. This research will lead to new fundamental understanding of how low temperatures influence the evolution of the structure and morphology of Li metal and its interface with the electrolyte. This knowledge will then be used to engineer interfaces for maximal charge transfer and transport kinetics. The project involves three specific research objectives: • Objective I: Investigate the effects of low temperatures on structural, chemical, and morphological transformations in Li metal and its interfaces during charge and discharge. • Objective II: Identify the parameters that govern the electrochemical kinetics of Li metal anodes as a function of temperature with electrochemical techniques. • Objective III: Engineer Li metal interfaces for improved charge transfer kinetics and stability at low temperatures. Over the course of this project, we have made a variety of discoveries that have advanced the field’s knowledge and understanding of how to enable high-capacity electrode materials for batteries that can operate at low temperature. A key set of findings within this work was the development of new electrolyte systems that improve reversibility of anode materials at low temperature. In addition, the team has made significant contributions to understanding the fundamental electrochemical reaction mechanisms and material evolution of alkali metal electrodes at low temperatures. Finally, we have also branched out to compare alkali metals to other anode materials, with the important finding that alloy anodes can show improved rate behavior and reversibility at low temperatures compared to graphite. Overall, this body of work advances the materials science of low-temperature batteries, indicating the promise of alternative anode materials for improved performance at low temperatures.
