UPper STAge Recovery (UPSTAR-ACO)

Project Description

The UPper STage Recovery (UPSTAR) system is a deployable aerodynamic decelerator built into the fairing of a launch vehicle upper stage, enabling the recovery and reuse of the majority of that stage, including the payload if desired. The fairing initially encloses the upper stage and payload, then for reentry splits into "petals" and deploys radially, supporting a lightweight ceramic fabric decelerator. Unique design features of UPSTAR minimize the mass and performance impact of adding recovery capability to an upper stage. The fairing structure shields the stage itself from aerodynamic and heating loads on both ascent and descent, allowing a very lightweight tank design. Dual use of the fairing structure as support for the ceramic fabric minimizes added mass for the decelerator.

Objectives of this technology include significant reduction of launch costs through nearly full reusability, and enabling a new mission class through recovery and reflight of high-value payloads (without requiring individual recovery systems). The work proposed under this collaborative opportunity will advance the technical maturity of the UPSTAR concept by anchoring our CFD analysis with surface heating results measured in a NASA Langley Research Center (LaRC) Mach 10 wind tunnel.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The UPper STage Recovery (UPSTAR) project developed a deployable aerodynamic decelerator built into the fairing of a launch vehicle upper stage. This system enables the recovery and reuse of most of the the vehicle’s upper stage, including the payload itself. The fairing encloses the upper stage and payload, and upon reentry it then splits into “petals” which deploy radially and while providing support to a lightweight ceramic fabric decelerator. As a result of the UPSTAR system there was a reduction of mass while at the same time providing a recovery capability to the upper stage. This was done by using a fairing structure that shields the stage itself from aerodynamic and heating loads on both ascent and descent and allows for a tank with reduced mass. Sierra Nevada Corporation (SNC) developed UPSTAR with a combination of internal and government (DARPA) funding. In addition to high-fidelity Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) analysis for aerodynamics and heating performed at critical Mach numbers, SNC completed high-temperature material testing and finite-element structural analysis to establish material choices, sizing, internal stresses, and deformation under loading. SNC, in collaboration with LaRC, performed hypersonic testing with the objective of anchoring the CFD-based analysis of hypersonic aerodynamics and surface heating with test results to verify critical elements of UPSTAR performance analysis.
