Ultrafast, High-Temperature Sintering of Mechanically Robust, Spectrally Selective Coatings for Cryogenic Propellant Storage

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186811
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This project will develop mechanically robust, spectrally selective particulate coatings for cryogenic propellant storage using ultra-fast high-temperature sintering (UHS). The goal is to rigidize particulate scatterer layers while preserving the micro- and nano-scale morphology required for particle-scale optical scattering, enabling durable coatings that maintain high solar reflectance and favorable infrared emission. The baseline coating architecture consists of a sub-micron particulate scattering layer deposited on a continuous infrared-reflective silver backing. Barium fluoride (BaF2) will serve as the primary scatterer material due to its demonstrated optical performance, with additional oxide and fluoride systems (e.g., Y2O3 and MgF2-based materials) explored to assess durability and processing compatibility. Coatings will be fabricated as flat, tile-like coupons with dimensions and thicknesses consistent with prior selective-surface studies, ensuring optical opacity and mechanical integrity while isolating material and processing effects. Ultra-fast high-temperature sintering will be used to consolidate the particulate layers. UHS is a Joule-heating-based process, developed by the proposing students lab, in which carbon heaters deliver rapid, localized thermal energy over second-scale durations. Processing parameters including peak temperature or power, dwell time, heating rate, atmosphere, and contact geometry will be systematically varied to establish process windows that achieve sufficient interparticle bonding while suppressing diffusion-driven grain growth and morphology evolution. Coating performance will be evaluated through coupled optical, mechanical, and environmental characterization. Spectroscopic measurements across solar and infrared wavelengths will quantify spectral selectivity before and after processing and exposure. Mechanical integrity and property uniformity will be assessed using nanoindentation and adhesion testing. Environmental stability will be evaluated through repeated thermal cycling between cryogenic and ambient temperatures to probe cracking, delamination, phase stability, and interfacial degradation. Microstructural and chemical analyses using SEM, XRD, and XPS will relate processing conditions to morphology retention, phase stability, and surface chemistry. Together, these efforts will establish processing--structure--property relationships for UHS-enabled spectrally selective coatings and demonstrate a scalable fabrication route for mechanically robust particulate scatterer layers suitable for cryogenic thermal-control applications.
