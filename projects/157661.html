Acoustic Sensors In Situ Space Debris Detector

NASA TechPort Project ID: 157661
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This is a feasibility study to determine whether acoustic sensors can be used to sense and measure benign impacts on solar array panels by small orbital debris. This is a continuation of work begun in FY 24 as a mid-year IRAD. The majority of the feasibility study involves drop testing of spherical projectiles to simulate impacts onto a flight-like solar arrays panel substrate in the range of 0.5 to 65 millijoules impact energy. FY 25 advancement is expected to include hypervelocity ground testing and design of a suitable data collection and transfer box.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

By measuring the impacts by small benign debris, the existing environment models for such debris can be confirmed or refined. Benign debris strikes can be used as a proxy for the flux of larger (but still not trackable) damaging debris objects. By measuring impacts in situ in real time, the first-ever data on potential variations based on seasons or related events can be measured.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This project further developed the work begun in FY24, and met the objectives for FY25. A sample solar array substrate panel was populated with four acoustic sensors and tested across a range of impact energies, impact velocities, and impact locations indicative of expected benign space debris strikes encountered in low Earth orbit. It was found that integrated voltage-time product was a better indicator of sensor response than simple maximum voltage or peak-to-peak voltage, and it was possible to correlate the sensor response to imposed impact energy. Smaller panels were also tested in a vacuum, with hypervelocity (3-7 km/sec) projectiles, to simulate a relevant use environment, confirming that the sensors could detect such impacts. Next steps include designing a data collection box and identifying a space flight demonstration opportunity.
