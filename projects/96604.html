Degradation of Vitro-Lube after environmental exposure (Tribology Testing)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 96604
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Vitro-Lube is a ceramic-bonded dry film lubricant that is used on many flight hardware components (ex: BISTRA ball joint, sliding bellows). Flight hardware may often sit on a test stand or launch pad for extended periods of time while being exposed to a corrosive environment. There is currently no performance data on Vitro-Lube that accounts for these conditions (humidity, sunlight, salt, etc.) during sustained environmental exposure. We propose to remedy this by performing Tribological testing on nickel alloy 718 samples with Vitro-Lube coating applied. The end goal being to create a database of the wear/degradation properties of Vitro-Lube, this database would be created using samples evaluated before and after exposure to some of the aforementioned environmental conditions.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Having a database that catalogs the behavior of the Tribological properties of the Vitro-Lube, summarizing how this dry lubricant degrades after exposure to the test stand environment, is of great interest to the aerospace community. It is well known that this coating does not inhibit corrosion and it is highly recommended by the manufacturer to use corrosion-resistant substrates to mitigate this problem. It is also known that contamination of the wear surfaces will trigger detrimental wear behavior of the coating.
