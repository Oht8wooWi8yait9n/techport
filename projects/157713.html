3D Cloud Reconstruction in Multi-Angle Polarimetry with a Foundation Model

NASA TechPort Project ID: 157713
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Using AI/ML for remote sensing analysis is challenged by the expense of training a neural network (NN) and adapting it to be useful for various measurement types. Foundation models address this by creating a universal NN, only requiring training of a downstream (adaptation) model. Building upon a previously funded IRAD effort which created a MODIS all-sky radiance based foundation model (led by J. Gong, 613), we propose to test if it can benefit multi-angle polarimeter observations as well. A successful result will position GSFC to lead in AI/ML applications in the Earth Sciences, thus aligning with Strategic Focus Area (SFA) priorities. We will leverage our expertise with the recently launched PACE/HARP2 multi-angle polarimeter, and as a side benefit will make a 3D cloud structure product.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Foundation models are highly adaptable and can be applied to multiple datasets. AI/ML techniques are rapidly evolving, so this early-stage innovation IRAD will strengthen GSFC’s dominance in the use of AI/ML for remote sensing of the atmosphere and provide for ROI in proposals to NASA ESTO’s Advanced Information Systems Technology (AIST) Program, specifically the Analytic Collaborative Framework (ACF), which seeks to fully utilize diverse datasets (among other programs). A new 3D cloud mask product will be generated for PACE, and in the longer term, AOS, both GSFC managed missions. Because of adaptability, the proposed work will also position GSFC scientists to better compete in a variety of mission or topic specific ROSES solicitations.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This project successfully demonstrated the use of the SatVision TOA foundation model to reconstruct 3D cloud structure from multi-angle observations such as PACE/HARP2 using neural networks (NN). Foundation models are universal, and can increase the efficiency of NN training by providing a pre-trained neural network. Thus, only a smaller adaptation neural network needs to be trained. The study tested the limits of data similarity, as SatVision TOA was trained with (not multi-angle) MODIS data. We found that the use of SatVision TOA met our accuracy and efficiency requirements, but did not dramatically exceed the capability of standalone NN's for this particular application. Subsequent efforts, if funded, will investigate other, recently developed, approaches to designing the adaptation neural network. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies. Goddard Space Flight Center’s IRAD program is managed under its Office of Chief Technologist. Activities develop concepts, reduce technology risk, and advance human capital and technological capabilities. Goddard’s IRAD program is highly competitive, opportunity-driven, and 100% strategically aligned with NASA's and Goddard's strategic priorities. A significant portion of the program is focused on Early Stage Innovations for high-risk, strategically aligned, potential high-payoff technologies that are longer-term or lower TRL.
