Autonomous Operations at Small Solar System Bodies

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95678
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The practical operation of spacecraft about asteroids and comets has been extensively studied by the world's space agencies and from an academic perspective, resulting in significant advances in our understanding of the laws of orbital mechanics in these environments and robust approaches to the design and implementation of spacecraft navigation. While research on this topic continues, four distinct modes of dynamical motion in the small body environment can be identified that encapsulate and enable the fundamental activities of a space vehicle in the small body environment: initial characterization, detailed surface observations and body parameter estimates, touch-down or surface deployment of packages, and long-term monitoring. Each of these modes has unique dynamical constraints that may differ significantly from each other and may require different instrument measurements and autonomous algorithms. Our research will consider the implementation of autonomous navigation, characterization and guidance techniques that are built around these different operational models. In addition, we will identify measurement needs and possible instrument capabilities that are required for these modes. Our purpose is to identify commonalities between the needs of each mode that can be captured with common instrumentation needs and algorithmic approaches. A main goal is to identify an appropriate menu of instrumentation and algorithmic implementations to support combinations of these operational modes that can be tuned to achieve a range of different scenarios of interest in the small body environment.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To (Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency): PI and Co-I collaborated with JAXA to use research products developed under ESI to help develop rover insertion strategy.
  • Closed Out: Our research is focused on advancing our ability to develop, design and implement autonomous navigation and guidance techniques for the exploration, scientific study and characterization of small solar system bodies. Our research considers the implementation of autonomous navigation, characterization and guidance techniques that are built around fundamental dynamic operational modes that have been identified in the small body environment. Our research will also focus on the autonomous creation and utilization of fundamental navigation models of the small body environment. The research has 4 main objectives: 1. Map Creation and Use Develop autonomous algorithms for the creation and subsequent utilization of a shape model and spin state of a small body upon rendezvous, utilizing a suite of sensors that go from coarse to high resolution. 2. Autonomous Navigation of Modes Define and implement autonomous navigation and guidance schemes and algorithms to cover the identified small body dynamics modes and transitions between them. 3. Autonomous Planning Demonstrate the autonomous development of goal motivated mission activities, utilizing navigation approaches from Objective 2. 4. Advanced Navigation Techniques Explore advanced applications of navigation techniques to address shortfalls identified in our research. These can include the use of landed probes on a small body surface for autonomous navigation in the system, novel applications for incorporating ground-based radiometric data into an autonomous system, or other techniques to be identified. Achievement of these objectives will provide an architecture for the autonomous exploration of small bodies, driven by a variety of mission operation goals. We will measure progress in these objectives through the development of algorithms that are tested using high-precision simulations and, where appropriate, use of measurement data from past missions.
