ISRU-Hydrogen/Methane Separator for Mars (HMSM-BAA)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 116391
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The HMSM task aims to develop and demonstrate a H2/CH4/H2O separator for NASA’s Martian ISRU processing at a TRL 5. The task will also develop a micro-channel separator based out of permeable copper where the walls are coated with a thin Pd-Ag film to selectively separate hydrogen from methane-hydrogen-water gas mixtures.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Production plants to produce oxygen from lunar regolith or that produce oxygen and methane from Mars atmosphere and soil-water are multi-step chemical processing plants that have several places where separation of intermediate species is required. The HMSM separator will be able to extract a pure hydrogen stream for recycling back to the primary reactor from a mixed-gas stream of hydrogen, methane, and water. This small and lightweight device will perform this key separation step at low mass and requiring no power or regeneration. In addition, by operating at higher temperatures typical of the primary reactor outlet temperature, this separator allows for a thermally efficient integration of components.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Hydrogen/Methane Separator for Mars (HMSM) was a Broad Agency Announcement (BAA) contract awarded to demonstrate hydrogen separation from a mixed-gas stream in a single copper microchannel. The Contractor’s goal was to create a lightweight hydrogen separator using a palladium (Pd) metal membrane that uses the solution-diffusion effect that is highly selective for hydrogen (H). The Contractor also developed several approaches for fabricating multi-channel units that would be able to achieve the flow rate requirements. However, the Contractor had difficulties with developing a method to consistently coat the porous copper tubes with a leak-free layer of pallidum-silver (Pd-Ag) to prevent contamination of the hydrogen permeate stream with residual methane and water.
