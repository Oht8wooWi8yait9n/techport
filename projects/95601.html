Chip-Scale Low-Noise Optoelectronic Synthesizer for the EHF-Band

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95601
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

A chip-scale oscillator that directly generates low phase-noise signals in the EHF-band (30-300GHz) can enable transformational advances in subsystem design for multiple NASA applications in telecommunication, remote sensing and navigation. Currently, microwave or mmW signals at the EHF-band are generated by frequency-multiplication from oscillators that deliver lower frequencies (e.g., at X-band), resulting in phase noise degradation and increased size, weight, and power (SWaP). Our project is concerned with the development of a photonic integrated circuit (PIC) that can directly generate a EHF-signal (the fundamental) from a regeneratively mode-locked (ML) laser. Leveraging the high quality-factor (Q) derived from the ML laser, we expect this fundamental EHF-signal to be of high spectral purity. Furthermore, we can program the PIC to synthesize additional EHF frequencies that are harmonics of the fundamental via optical heterodyning. This is accomplished by filtering pairs of lines from the optical comb emitted by the ML laser, and then photodetecting them with a wideband detector. We plan to maximize the spectral width of this optical comb via use of on-chip pulse compression, so that the frequencies covering the entire EHF-band can be synthesized by optical heterodyning.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The objective of our research was to develop a chip-scale optoelectronic (OE) synthesizer that can generate EHF-signals (30-300 GHz) with low phase-noise and SWaP for space-applications in telecommunication, navigation and remote sensing. Our approach leverages the use of optical heterodyning at a high frequency photodiode (PD) to generate EHF-signals formed as beat-notes between incident wavelengths. To accomplish the above objective, we developed(1) a regeneratively (regen) mode-locked (ML) laser, so that a coherent optical comb can be generated near the wavelength of o~1550nm. We also developed photonic integrated circuits (PICs) designed to filter out selected comb-lines for use in optical heterodyning or injection-locking. We demonstrated the generation of EHF-signals (at 42GHz and 62.9GHz) as: (i) Photodetected harmonics of the ML pulse train, and (ii) Products of photomixing between two laser diodes (LDs) injection-locked by filtered comb-lines. In addition, we developed a theoretical model(1) to estimate the jitter of regen ML lasers, and measured a timing-jitter of 60fs for our laser. Treating the regen ML laser as a coupled OE oscillator, we measured(2) a microwave quality-factor (QRF) of 5.8x104 for its ring-resonator. The high QRF attained by the resonator enabled us to generate EHF-signals of high spectral purity from the ML comb source.
