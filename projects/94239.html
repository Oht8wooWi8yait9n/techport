Combined Cycle Mode Transition (AAVP21)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 94239
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The Combined Cycle Mode Transition challenge demonstrates autonomous control and establishes performance/operability assessment methodologies for future reusable hypersonic propulsion systems that use turbine engines at slow speeds while transitioning to scramjets for high-speed operations. This challenge addresses the technology barrier of propulsion system mode transition via ground tests.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Besides significantly reducing the risk of mode transition of a Turbine-Based Combined Cycle (TBCC) propulsion system for a potential flight demonstration, HTP will added to the research area as follows:
1) Mode Transition framework: Create a basis of knowledge for transient mode transition characteristics that is structured to guide future TBCC development.
2) TBCC components: Discover critical interaction between the propulsion components of a highly instrumented TBCC research model.
a. Inlet-to-inlet interaction: Understand and document effects of low-speed inlet flowpath changes on the high-speed inlet. Develop methodologies to trade the performance and operability design space for the dual inlets of a TBCC propulsion system.
b. Low-speed inlet / turbine engine: Understand airflow matching and dynamic distortion compatibility between the low-speed flowpath components for performance and operability.
c. High-speed inlet / isolator / Dual-Mode Ramjet (DMRJ): Understand the interplay between the high-speed flowpath components for performance and stability.
3) Autonomous Mode Transition: Researching controllers as a step towards autonomous control of a TBCC propulsion system. For the first time, control will be developed with a relevant mode transition path based on transient data and analytical modeling.
4) Advanced Controls: Exercise state-of-art control algorithms to enhance system response, robustness and noise rejection.
5) Transient validation database: Acquire high quality data for time-accurate analyses and CFD code validation.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: For the past 15-20 years hypersonic research has been largely dedicated to expendable vehicles which are primarily propelled via a rocket-boosted dual-mode ramjet (DMRJ) propulsion system. The next evolution in hypersonic flight is reusable vehicles. HTP is working to develop enabling technologies for reusable point-to-point hypersonic vehicles and space access platforms. These applications will require a reusable propulsion system. One such reusable propulsion system uses a turbine-based combined cycle (TBCC). TBCC is a promising propulsion concept that combines highly efficient airbreathing cycles across a wide Mach number (Mn) range, enabling the design of reusable high-speed vehicles that exhibit “aircraft-like” characteristics. This includes runway takeoff and landing, efficient cruise, and seamless airspace integration. One of the technological hurdles that needs to be overcome for a TBCC system to become a reality is mode transition (MT), where the overall propulsion system transitions between the turbine and DMRJ while maintaining thrust/operability throughout the transition. Managing the various variable geometry elements through these transition points while maintaining engine operability at acceptable performance levels is extremely challenging. The TC-2 aimed at 3 areas of development: 1) expanded Mach operation, 2) control algorithm development and automated control demonstration, and 3) performance/operability assessment methods and database development. Key accomplishments include: New experimental techniques may decrease wind tunnel time by 10x. Multiple controllers transferred to the Hypersonic International Flight Research and Experimentation (HF2-C) activity. Silicon carbide sensor technology has been transferred to HF2-C and to a Sandia National Lab Hypersonic Wind Tunnel test.
