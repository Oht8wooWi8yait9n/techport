Project Description

The innovative approach is to do Doppler shift interferometry with cubesats. This work will explore the viability of the approach through analyisis and simulation.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Current approaches are not effective at detecting and characterizing orbitl debris particles at the 1mm size, although they could be very damaging. Large orbital debris particles are well known but there is little to no data on debris particles 1mm to a few cm in size. Orbital debris particles in this range are assessed as one of the highest risk factors for spacecraft because of their predicted abundance and penetration capacity. Lack of measurements force debris models to rely on extrapolations from measurements of large debris (>3mm) and in-situ measurements of micrometer scaled debris (for instance from space shuttle impacts). This has prompted calls for better characterization of the orbital environment and validation of debris models, particularly of debris 1-3mm in size and at altitudes between 600-1000km.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The goal of this project was to further develop the concept of a novel radar instrument for characterizing orbital debris 1mm and larger in size. Orbital debris particles greater than a few cm in size are easily observed, but there is little to no data on smaller particles. To that end, we developed a CW (continuous wave) radar design with a novel multi-channel interferometric measurement concept we call Doppler Shift Interferometry (DSI) that will make it possible to detect millimetric debris particles, determine their size and orbital trajectory, and estimate their size. On this task we verified the DSI concept with a time-domain forward simulator developed from basic principles. We also outlined aspects of the on-board processing algorithm and identified the need for machine learning (ML) based event detection algorithms. Initial implementations of ML algorithms with simulated data show promising results, however more work is required. We derived some initial hardware requirements, analyzed previously identified hardware threats to performance, and identified a new photonics-based radar front end architecture. Initial feasibility shows promising improvements in radar performance over a traditional front-end design based on RF components.
