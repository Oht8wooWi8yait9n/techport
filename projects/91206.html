Mission Trade Space Evaluation through Multiphysics Design and Optimization

Project Description

In recent years, modeling and simulation tools have enabled engineers to design highly complex systems while taking into consideration constraints across multiple physical domains (e.g., mechanical, electronic, thermal, optical, etc.). With the current focus on improving the simulation techniques, little attention has been paid to developing CAD tools for multiphysics optimization so far. The need for powerful optimization is highlighted for instance by recent experiences with deep-space systems that have shown cost-growth issues during integration and test, and operation. These include changes in the design late in the life-cycle which often result in a ripple-effect of changes in the other areas and cause cost and schedule growth during system development. This is a key part of the Space Technology Roadmap Technical Area , Technology Area Breakdown Structure TABS 2.2.2.2. In this work our main objective is the development of new search algorithms for multiphysics design and optimization. We propose to investigate new stochastic optimization algorithms that could be used in a multiphysics design automation tool. Such an optimization tool would be able to take into account multiple objectives (such as size, power consumption, RF emissions) across multiple components and find global solutions, alleviating the costs of system engineering. With respect to space technology, this would allow system analyses to be done early enough and to be incorporated into various space-trade evaluation processes making space exploration more affordable and capable.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: In this work, we plan to investigate new stochastic optimization algorithms that could be used in a multiphysics design automation tool. Our main goal is to develop new stochastic search algorithms (SSAs) that will be able to optimize complex engineering systems across two or three physical domains. Focusing on electromagnetic and mechanical optimizations, we will test and analyze algorithm performance on a small set of real-world problems. One of such complex engineering problems is the design of a rectifying antenna (rectenna) that can be optimized for high DC conversion efficiency and small size. Another research thrust is the coupled electrome chanical optimization of a load bearing metallic structure. We propose an approach in which parts of the metallic frame are designed to act as antennas. Such an antenna system should be able to withstand large structural loads while maintaining its electromagnetic performance and must be optimized to be mechanically rigid, lightweight and provide stable radiating patterns over a broad range of frequencies. We will apply our SSAs to the optimization of a single rectenna element and a load-bearing antenna and validate algorithm performance by building prototypes of the best-found designs. The research investigation sponsored by the 4-year fellowship grant includes six interrelated research projects, namely: • Evaluation of stochastic search algorithms (SSAs); application of SSAs to singleobjective optimization • Design and optimization of a rectenna system • Theory of Characteristic Modes for Automated Antenna Design and Feed Placement • Development of multi-objective algorithm with local search (MOLS) • Benchmarking MOLS • Application of MOLS to design and optimization of a CubeSAT antenna across mechanical and electromagnetic domains In this chapter, we apply the developed methods to the optimization of a CubeSat, a real-world problem in which mechanical and electromagnetic properties are critical to the mission success. Our goal is to design a CubeSat antenna that utilizes the existing cubic structure to radiate. This allows for structure reuse and weight reduction, while freeing up mass for the payload. By modifying the CubeSat, we must make sure that it meets the mass and volume constraints and is still able to support loads during the launch. We investigate the trade-offs between the structural integrity and electromagnetic performance that can be achieved with modifications of the initial structure. We formulated the optimization problem that includes the objectives and constraints in the mechanical and electromagnetic (EM) domains. In the mechanical domain, we seek a structure with the prescribed shape and volume that can support forces along its longest dimension, which can be formulated as a classical topology optimization problem and solved using mathematical programming methods. In the EM domain, the CubeSat antenna must produce a directional pattern with most of the power transmitted in the direction of the ground station. It must achieve 80 degrees Half Power Beamwidth (HPBW) and 5 dB gain in the boresight direction and be matched to 50 Ohm at 2.2 GHz. MOLS was used to solve the multi-objective optimization problem. The trade-off desigs obtained by the algorithm after 20,000 evaluations were closely inspected in the design and objective space. In a high-dimensional search space, MOLS identified several trade-off designs, that balanced structural resistance to deformation and its performance as an S-band antenna. In the mechanical domain, a single locally optimal topology was obtained: small variations on this structure contributed the majority of non-dominated solutions. In the EM domain, the goal of matching the antenna to 50 Ohm was met easily, unlike the radiation objective. The results suggested that the CubeSat structure was capable of generating multi-lobe patterns but was less suitable for uni- or omni-directional radiation in S-band under the given volume constraints.
