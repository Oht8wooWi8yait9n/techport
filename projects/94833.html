Project Description

Rising greenhouse gas concentrations are accelerating climate warming and associated changes carbon, water, and energy cycling in the Earth system. The 2017-2027 Decadal Survey for Earth Science and Applications from Space recognized the essential role of measurements of ice topography and vegetation structure to understand our changing planet. The Canopy Height and Glacier Elevation (CHANGE) Mission concept development study will explore avenues to sustain measurements of Earth's ice and vegetation cover and directly observe changes in ice sheet elevation, sea ice thickness, and vegetation cover over time.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Earth's ice and vegetation cover provide essential benefits. Rapid loss of land and sea ice in response to climate warming will raise sea levels worldwide, risking life and property for the billions of people who live in coastal cities. Similarly, natural and planted vegetation provides a range of ecosystem services, including runoff and landslide protection, carbon storage, food and fiber resources, and wildlife habitat. Satellite-based observations of rapid changes in ice and vegetation cover therefore contribute to our long-term understanding of the Earth system response to climate change and support forecasting and response efforts for specific disaster events and long-term planning for climate-driven changes in land cover and land use. NASA satellite data contribute to global data needs on our changing planet. In the US, key partner agencies for sustained measurements of ice and vegetation cover change include the USDA, USGS, NOAA, and DHS.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The Canopy Height and Glacier Elevation (CHANGE) mission concept combines coincident lidar and stereo imaging capabilities to address Decadal Survey objectives covering ice topography and vegetation structure for the Earth System Explorer (ESE) competitive solicitation. This project included technology and measurement concept advancement, as well community engagement and building across academia and industry. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry and other Government Agencies.
