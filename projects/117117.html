SpaceCube Robust Adapter Card for Ethernet in Space (RACES)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 117117
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

RACES will expand high-speed interface options for Goddard-developed electronic card families by developing a 10/100/1000 Mbps Ethernet interface card rated for use in space, and then incorporating advanced networking IP into the SpaceCube architecture for networking. This design will be achieved by integrating a newly released radiation-hardened (rad-hard) PHY transceiver into an existing IO card in a 1U (10cm x 10cm) cubesat form factor.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

RACES will not only be essential for Lunar Gateway (and Lunar experiments), but it will also be beneficial for other future instruments, since many rapidly integrated instrument prototypes commonly use Ethernet in testing and development but must switch to another interface for flight. RACES would allow developers to use a consistent interface throughout the project lifecycle while increasing throughput by 5-100× over traditional solutions such as RS-422 or SpaceWire.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Over the course of this project, we successfully engaged with stakeholders to develop the overall board architecture, performed trade studies to select and procure EEE parts, designed the PCB schematic and simulated key subcircuits, and performed an initial PCB layout placement. This effort resulted in significant progress in maturing the design for future fabrication, assembly, and integration into next generation SpaceCube solutions for instruments. Looking forward, RACES has already been adopted for use on the Lunar Experiment Survey System and Handling (LESSH) as the primary IO card and main wired data interface between the Common Service Module (CSM) and the Human Landing System (HLS). The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
