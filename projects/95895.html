Project Description

JOEEE (Jumping Orientable Extreme Environment Explorer) is a novel planetary surface mobility platform that uses internal brake-able flywheels to enable cartwheeling, hopping, and precision pointing. JOEEE’s unique maneuvering capability enables deployment of scientific and engineering payloads in terrain and environments inaccessible to traditional wheeled rovers or rocket hoppers. The goals of this proposal are to complete the demonstration of the mobility system in hardware prototypes (TRL maturation), refine the system design, and work with instrument developers and mission planners on mission-specific variations.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

JOEEEs enable a number of new mission classes for planetary surface exploration. JOEEEs are designed to hop, cartwheel, and re-orient after tumbling. They are natural mobility solutions for exploring inside PSRs, caves, and craters as they are not only robust to tumbles and falls, but tumbling and subsequent reorientation is the fundamental system design and concept of operations. Further, with no external moving parts, the JOEEE system design allows for the entire explorer to be hermetically sealed; this prevents dust intrusion/wear and minimizes thermal leak paths.

While this project specifically targets mobility for the Lunar surface, it can readily be applied to the Martian surface, surface of other moons, or even small bodies such as comets and asteroids as demonstrated by MINERVA-II on the asteroid Ryugu.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: By the end of this fiscal year, a custom motor+brakes control electronics board was designed and fabricated as PCBs, custom firmware for the motor+brakes control electronics board was written and tested, a greatly-improved braking mechanism was designed and fabricated, and new components for both a planar mobility demonstrator and a full 6 DoF mobility demonstrator were fabricated and/or procured. Flywheel motor control and braking mechanism tests were conducted. Documentation packages and hardware demonstrations are being prepared for future proposal efforts. This mobility technology development and maturation effort enables instrument mobility into extreme environments on the Lunar surface and Martian surface such as across rocky terrain, into craters, and into lava tubes. This mobility technology also enables instrument mobility on surfaces in microgravity, such as on the surface of asteroids and comets. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies. Goddard Space Flight Center’s IRAD program is managed under its Office of Chief Technologist. Activities develop concepts, reduce technology risk, and advance human capital and technological capabilities. Goddard’s IRAD program is highly competitive, opportunity-driven, and 100% strategically aligned with NASA's and Goddard's strategic priorities. A significant portion of the program is focused on Early Stage Innovations for high-risk, strategically aligned, potential high-payoff technologies that are longer-term or lower TRL.
