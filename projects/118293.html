Hypergolic rotating detonation rocket propulsion with low pressure-loss injection and advanced thermal management

NASA TechPort Project ID: 118293
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

Detonation-based propulsion systems have potential for increased performance and reduced mass leading to extended spacecraft mission durations. In-space rotating detonation rocket engine (RDRE) systems using space-storable propellants may offer a relatively short overall timeline to technical and flight readiness compared to launch systems given the smaller scale and typical pulsed-mode operation. Based on work with other propellants, critical technology limitations noted to-date include (1) under-mixed propellant injection yielding relatively low wave speeds, (2) extreme heat transfer and associated lack of chamber durability, and (3) undesirably high pressure-loss feed systems required to avoid injection backflow and to shorten propellant refresh timescales for continuous operation, which offset the pressure gain associated with detonation combustion. All three of these issues can be related to injector design. In this project, we will explore novel additively-manufacturable injection schemes aimed at enhancing propellant pre-mixing, film cooling of chamber walls, and minimizing feed system pressure loss. This parametric injection analysis will be conducted via a coupled computational and experimental approach, with hypergolic space-storable propellant combinations. The effort aims to improve fundamental understanding of rotating detonation engine operation and provide a pathway to technology transition.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This project looks at increased in-space chemical propulsion specific impulse. This will lead to reductions in thruster system dry-mass, extended NASA mission durations, and an expedient path to technical readiness for advanced pulse-mode in-space chemical propulsion for NASA missions

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The primary research objective of this project was to advance rotating detonation rocket engine (RDRE) technology using space-storable propellants. This supports broader national interests to increase spacecraft chemical propulsion specific impulse and to decrease in-space chemical propulsion system dry mass. Despite promising theoretical performance, rotating detonation rocket engines are at a low technical readiness, particularly with regards to space-storable liquid propellants. Until very recently, no RDRE research and development efforts had been dedicated to the most common hypergolic space storable propellants (i.e. MMH and NTO), and for the many other propellants assessed over the past decade, operation has proven difficult and, in most cases, performance remains far from ideal. Critical technology limitations noted to-date include (1) under-mixed propellant injection yielding relatively weak detonations (2) extreme heat transfer and associated lack of chamber durability / burn time, and (3) undesirably high pressure-loss feed systems required to avoid injection backflow and shorten propellant refresh timescales for continuous operation, which offset the pressure gain associated with detonation combustion. All three of these issues can be related in part to injector design. Additive manufacturing (AM) reduces the cost of design complexity, and provides opportunity to incorporate functional injection and thermal management features advantageous to RDRE operation. The specific tasks for this project aim to develop and evaluate novel RDRE injector designs that leverage additive manufacturing to reconcile the multi-physical challenges to rotating detonation engine operation and performance, while maturing RDRE operability with space storable propellants. A particular focus is given to lowering pressure loss and increasing diodicity. The injector design performance is assessed holistically using both computational analysis and experimental testing. Over the duration of this project, several novel AM impinging injection schemes were designed (leveraging CFD), fabricated using Laser Powder Bed Fusion (L-PBF) with various metallic alloys, and tested experimentally through both RDRE hot-fire testing and cold-flow testing. Between the two hot-fire test campaigns conducted using modular liquid-liquid hypergolic RDRE, over 120 hotfire tests were executed. Key achievements from these hot-fire campaigns include but are not limited to: hypergolic-initiated and sustained RDRE operation using various aerospike nozzles and various AM injector designs, high-speed detonation wave imaging and characterization of wave dynamics, sustained RDRE operation with relatively low injector stiffness using a highelement density AM injector design, and demonstration of RDRE pulse-mode operation with short deflagration-to-detonation transition. Novel injection schemes with AM-enabled features exhibited enhancements in backflow-resistance and reduced injector pressure-drop, as predicted by CFD F3 and experimentally validated by several phases of testing. Overall, this project provided substantial data supporting the potential of hypergolic rotating detonation rocket propulsion systems while increasing technical readiness.
