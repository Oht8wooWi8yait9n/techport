Development of InAlAs Top Cell for High Specific Power Multijunction Photovoltaics

NASA TechPort Project ID: 91475
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The semiconductor material InAlAs has the potential to improve upon current space photovoltaics in a number of ways. InAlAsSb lattice-matched to InP would operate as the top cell in a triple-junction design with an AM0 efficiency of 37.1%, a cell-level mass specific power >1000 W/kg, and panel-level mass specific power of 662 W/kg. Development of InAlAs for engineered substrates would result in a lattice-matched triple-junction cell with a 1-sun AM1.5 efficiency of 40.4%. Additionally, InAlAs lattice-matched to InP has the appropriate bandgap for operation in low-intensity low-temperature conditions. Development of these proposed photovoltaic cells is particularly warranted since the InP materials system is known to be exceptionally radiation tolerant, which is ideal for space operation. Furthermore, lattice-matched cells are lighter and more mechanically stable than their metamorphic counterparts. The technology proposed in this application would increase capability and durability for missions needing onboard power or electric propulsion, and would also correspond to technology gains for terrestrial concentrator photovoltaic systems. The materials proposed in this study have undergone little to no development. Development of these materials would occur via semiconductor growth methods of metal organic vapor phase epitaxy or molecular beam epitaxy. Growth conditions such as temperature, gaseous precursors, and gas ratios can be adjusted to target desired material properties. This research would initially focus on materials development. Once the desired material are grown, they can then be fabricated in complete photovoltaic cells and tested for radiation and temperature tolerance which are important considerations for space applications.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The semiconductor material InAlAs has the potential to improve upon current space photovoltaics in a number of ways. InAlAsSb lattice-matched to InP could operate as the top cell in a triple-junction design with an AM0 efficiency of 37.1%, a cell-level mass specific power >1000 W/kg, and panel-level mass specific power of 662 W/kg. Additionally, InAlAs lattice-matched to InP has the appropriate bandgap for operation in low-intensity low-temperature conditions. Development of these proposed photovoltaic cells is particularly warranted since the InP materials system is known to be exceptionally radiation tolerant, which is beneficial for space operation. Alternatively, quantum structures can be used to both increase current matching in multijunction cells as well as increase radiation tolerance. Combining quantum structures with light management at the rear of a cell enables a thinner cell to have the same power output, where a thinner cell represents both an increased radiation tolerance and higher mass-specific power. This technology could increase capability and durability for missions needing onboard power or electric propulsion, and also supports technology gains for terrestrial concentrator photovoltaic systems. The InAlAs/InAlAsSb materials proposed in this study previously had little to no development reported in literature. Growth of these materials by metal organic vapor phase epitaxy (MOVPE) was studied by varying growth conditions such as temperature, gaseous precursors, and gas ratios in order to target desired material composition and quality. Bandgaps are verified by photoreflectance spectroscopy, and Hall effect measurements are used to quantify mobility and inform an optimal solar cell device design. InAs/GaAs and InAs/AlGaAs quantum dot solar cells (QDSCs) were also grown by MOVPE in an inverted configuration so the rear of the cell could be textured before metal deposition, after which the cell would be separated from the substrate via an epitaxial lift off technique and placed upright. This metallized rear texture serves as a back surface reflector that permits more passes of light through the device than a flat back surface reflector. The performance of the reflector is quantified by measuring the sub-band spectral response from a QDSC with a textured reflector as compared to QDSC with a flat reflector. Finally, a life cycle assessment of these III-V photovoltaic designs in terrestrial concentrator settings will be executed to determine the environmental feasibility of manufacturing these technologies for terrestrial use as a function of cell material and power conversion efficiency. Ultimately, a single-junction InAlAs device was designed, fabricated, and tested to show a 17.9% efficiency under 1-sun AM1.5 which represents a 26% improvement over what was previously published in literature. The same device design was grown using alternative MOVPE precursors and showed a 16% short-circuit current enhancement. Multiple compositions of InAlAsSb material were grown using these alternative precursors, where the highest Sb content was 48% verified by secondary ion mass spectrometry and the bandgap measured by photoreflectance was 1.82 eV. Previously the highest Sb content of InAlAsSb on InP was only reported to be 15%. A textured back surface reflector was developed in AlGaAs for quantum dot solar cells which resulted in a 30% increase in sub-band current as compared to a flat back reflector, where this texture had previously only been demonstrated in GaAs for quantum well solar cells. Furthermore, MOVPE growth conditions and device design were studied in order to maximize InAs QD emission in an AlGaAs host material, which identified an optimal growth temperature, benefits from an alternative aluminum precursor, and benefits from a 3 nm GaAs interlayer below the InAs QDs. This represents an improvement from previous studies with similar emission that used a 90 nm AlGaAs interlayer since thinner interlayers enable quantum dot state coupling which is required for the creation of an intermediate band solar cell. Finally, the life cycle assessment of MOVPE growth of III-V photovoltaic devices in terrestrial concentrator applications involved detailed modeling of both MOVPE precursors and III-V substrates. The environmental performance of the precursors was found to be 4 to 10 times larger than had been previously estimated in literature, while the substrates were found to be 4 times larger. Ultimately, however, it was found that all cell types analyzed were shown to improve the overall life cycle performance of the concentrator system as cell efficiency increased.
