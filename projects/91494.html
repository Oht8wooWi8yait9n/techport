Assistive Free-Flyers with Gecko-Inspired Adhesive Appendages for Automated Logistics in Space

Project Description

Gecko-inspired adhesives provide a new capability for assistive free fliers (AFF) in space. In comparison with conventional grippers, they make it possible to attach and detach from surfaces with very low effort, simplifying control and motion planning for grasping and manipulating objects. The research includes (i) design and analysis of new adhesive grasping appendages, (ii) development and testing of new algorithms that leverage the adhesive capabilities for coordinated AFF motion planning and control and, (iii) testing prototypes in representative operations on zero-gravity test beds. The work aims to greatly reduce human time spent unpacking supplies, fetching tools and positioning and reading sensors inside space vehicles. It also paves the way for future extravehicular maintenance and exploration tasks.

  • Advanced From

  • Closed Out: The goal of this research has been to integrate adhesion-based grasping with control and planning to enable assistive free-flyers to grasp and manipulate payloads, supplies, and tools inside and outside spacecraft. The technical objectives are: Design, analyze, and test mechanisms and procedures that employ controllable adhesion to manipulate payloads within space vehicles and ultimately in extravehicular operations. Devise control and planning algorithms that allow free-flyers to navigate complex space structures and manipulate payloads with adhesion-based appendages. Validate adhesive manipulation technology for representative tasks on a planar testbed at Stanford, emulating the dynamics of assistive free-flyers in microgravity through collaboration with NASA Ames.
  • Transitioned To (International Space Station): The Stanford ESI15 extension award was a one-year effort to build and integrate a gecko gripper with Astrobee, upmass the gripper to the ISS, then test its capabilities on ISS with Astrobee. The intent was to lay the foundation for more complex robotic operations using gecko-inspired adhesive grippers, which may include space operations such as improved astronaut assistance, space station maintenance, satellite servicing, orbital debris removal, and on-orbit construction. The gecko gripper was upmassed to ISS (on SpaceX-18) in July 2019. ISS testing was originally planned for August 2019. However, lack of ISS crew time in 2019 prevented Astrobee commissioning from completing and then of course the pandemic made 2020 a total loss. As a result, the ISS Astrobee Facility team (led by Jose Benavides at Ames) has been working to schedule test sessions for 2021 subject to prioritization inputs, including STMD. - Terry Fong gecko-adhesive gripper is a flight certified payload.
