Project Description

Continues the development of an approach for autonomous inspection, maintenance, and repair (IMR) activities using robotic systems for ground operations. It is intended to support operations and maintenance (O&M) activities of ground systems in remote locations, hazardous operations, and other activities where it is desirable to minimize or remove human interactions/interventions.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Anywhere that uses robotics for operations. One example is the manufacturing industry.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: As NASA space flight missions continue to extend farther from Earth, for longer periods of time, these missions require increased independence from the crew and ground control mission support to operate efficiently, safely, and reliably. The need to develop sustainable systems and operations that support NASA exploration initiatives is of paramount importance. Fiscally sustainable and affordable exploration of space and other planetary surfaces, without compromising safety, is also needed. These requirements promote increased automation and autonomy of systems that require minimal human intervention during operation and maintenance (O&M) activities. The inspection, maintenance and repair (IMR) tools for autonomous ground operations task is a continuation of a previous task that pursued the use of robotics and smart tools for the autonomous operation, maintenance, and sustaining of ground based systems and subsystems. In support of these tasks, several key capabilities are identified: an open system architecture that is easily scalable, capable of working with different operating systems and hardware platforms; the standardization of systems and components design, interfaces and processes that support maintainability, reusability and reduction of logistics needs; software commonality and reusability; ability to reconfigure components and systems without major redesigns; and integrated health management capabilities to enable fault prediction, diagnosis, and resolution. Integration of these capabilities will reduce operations and logistics costs while providing common frameworks for implementing future autonomous O&M activities. The primary objectives of the current IMR task were to continue standard interface formulation and design, develop a set of functional prototype IMR Smart Tools, integrate them with a Commercial Off The Shelf (COTS) robotic arm equipped with a standardized mechanical/electrical/communications interface, and demonstrate the functionality of these prototypes performing representative IMR activities against a scaled down mockup of a representative area of an operational system. The IMR team researched and designed three separate smart tools. They designed, prototyped, and demonstrated operation of the Smart Camera, Smart Wrench, and Smart Sniffer on the benchtop, including embedded software. Using additive manufacturing technologies, they designed and prototyped housing and packaging components for all three smart tools. Due to COVID-19 pandemic restrictions and mandatory telework rules that were enacted during the final months of the project schedule, the IMR team was unable to complete the 3D printing of the final packaging designs, nor the integration of the three smart tools with the laboratory robot, nor the operational demonstration of the integrated system in the laboratory.
