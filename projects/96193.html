Fabricating Next Generation Diffraction Gratings Using Electron Beam Lithography

NASA TechPort Project ID: 96193
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

As current X-ray observatories age, development of highly sensitive X-ray instrumentation is necessary to meet the increasing demands of the astronomical community. High resolution soft X-ray spectroscopy (0.2 - 2.0 keV) is required to address important questions raised in the 2010 decadal survey. The only technology that has the resolution and sensitivity necessary to answer these questions is X-ray gratings, which have been rated as priority tier 1 by the NASA Physics of the Cosmos program. The goal of this proposal is to perform an in-depth study of fabricating radially grooved off-plane X-ray reflection gratings that meet the performance requirements for the next decade of X-ray astronomy. Electron beam lithography is the only nanofabrication method with the resolution and control necessary to produce the radial groove pattern required to achieve high resolution. However, no study has been conducted to fully optimize the e-beam tool for grating fabrication. Furthermore, there is no fabrication process that can currently produce an off-plane grating with high efficiency and resolution, only one or the other. Using the nanofabrication lab in the Materials Research Institute at Penn State I will determine the optimal e-beam tool parameters to have tight control over the grating features. This will allow us to write accurate radially grooved X-ray gratings with high resolving power, and to make highly efficient blazes using additional fabrication techniques.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This first phase of this fellowship worked towards improving the current state-of-the-art nanofabrication processes for producing off-plane X-ray reflection gratings which use Electron Beam Lithography (EBL) to create the groove patterns and profiles. There were two primary efforts in this phase of the fellowship – expanding on previous work which investigated creating blazed groove profiles using greyscale EBL; and producing large-format variable line space X-ray reflection gratings. The first effort involved analyzing diffraction efficiency data acquired prior to the start of the fellowship of the first greyscale blazed X-ray reflection grating with a 160 nm period. The results of the analysis demonstrated diffraction efficiency performance between 30-40% across the tested bandpass, approaching levels necessary for next generation observatories. While this was a promising result for a first attempt at this type of groove profile and period, additional work is necessary to improve grating performance. Greyscale EBL is one technique being studied to produce variable line space, blazed, gratings for upcoming missions. The second effort was an investigation in fabricating large-format, approximately 100 x 100 mm, variable line space gratings. Large-format reflection gratings are necessary to achieve the effective area and variable line spacing is needed to achieve the resolving power required by next generation X-ray observatories. Extensive studies were carried out using EBL to optimize tool and pattern parameters to produce high-fidelity large-format gratings. This culminated in the fabrication of two of the largest off-plane X-ray gratings with one of the most complex variable line space profiles attempted to date. These gratings were used as masters to produce replicas for a suborbital rocket flight.
