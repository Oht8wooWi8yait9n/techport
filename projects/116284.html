Project Description

This project is a collaboration between the Fluid Dynamics Branch (ER42) at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center and Gloyer-Taylor Laboratory (GTL) to evaluate GTL's Universal Combustion Devide Stability (UCDS) and Simulation Data Analysis (SimDAT) for relevance, ease of use, bug tracking, and feature requests for combustion instability modeling.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

These tools have the potential to enhance NASA missions by providing a cost-effective approach to model, understand, and design for stable propulsion before hardware is manufactured.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: To achieve insight into combustion instability, GTL has developed two complimentary technologies: The Universal Combustion Device Stability™ (UCDS™) process, and the Simulation Data Analysis Tool™ (SimDAT™). UCDS is a physics-based model that employs a hybrid analytical-computational analysis wherein energy transport in and out of unsteady fields is tracked. It is composed of a suite of computational multiphysics models that can be easily tailored to suit the unique challenges associated with combustion instability. UCDS serves as a predictive tool to facilitate rapid design iteration before more resource-intensive approaches are employed. On the other hand, SimDAT can be used as a standalone data processing tool for high-fidelity computational fluid dynamics or as a complement to UCDS. In either use-case, SimDAT stands to fill the gap between computational solutions and engineering design by identifying and quantifying the physical processes and design decisions that lead directly to instabilities. By applying these techniques, GTL can analyze a wide scope of aerospace systems, including rockets (liquid, solid, and hybrid), turbojets (combustors, augmentors), ramjets, scramjets, combined cycle engines and so on. This means that potential customers for GTL’s software and services benefit from a validated and ergonomic toolkit to develop stable propulsion systems. GTL delivered a package of demonstration cases along with the UCDS and SimDAT software suite to MSFC. A series of demonstration cases were exercised in order to evaluate the UCDS and SimDAT software. Realtime feedback was provided to engineers at GTL on usability, organization, and bug tracking. GTL engineers provided updates, improvements, and repackaging in order to facilitate project completion. Collaborative efforts and software enhancements were undertaken to develop a model for solid rocket motor simulations. This partnership with the Fluid Dynamics Branch (ER42) at NASA Marshall Space Flight Center evaluated these tools for relevance, ease of use, bug tracking, and feature request. GTL supplied MSFC with the most up-to-date version of their software and, in turn, MSFC provided feedback to support continued refinement and higher TRL levels. The personnel in ER42 at MSFC are experts in combustion instability and have pursued a continual working relationship throughout the development of these tools. The Exploration Systems Development Branch (ST24) at MSFC served as a partner to provide project management for this activity.
