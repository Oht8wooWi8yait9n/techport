Mechanically Strong Polymide Aerogels (MSPA-ACO)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 116269
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Aerogel Technologies and NASA GRC will work together to improve materials properties of polyimide aerogels to make them suitable for rocket fairing and other aerospace applications and to characterize the improved polyimide aerogels for the specifications required for adoption in commercial space vehicles. Materials properties improvements include: introduction of hydrophobicity and water uptake resistance through monomer selection and patterning, and statistical design of experiments (DoE); optimization of acoustic and vibration attenuation properties, thermal insulating properties, and lower density through statistical DoE; formulation of materials using cost-effective, scalable, domestically sourceable monomers; and formulation of materials that pass flammability requirements of FAR 25.853. Materials characterizations include: sound transmission loss and absorption properties; materials properties as a function of temperature from 20 to 100°F; transient liquid water uptake and properties upon drying; properties after exposure to humidity; encapsulated heat flux; outgassing mass loss; non-volatile cleanliness; molecular contamination; charging, electrostatic discharge, and electric field response; shelf life estimates from accelerated testing; and material tolerance to vibration environment.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Optimizaiton of properties of polymer aerogels with respect to acoustic applications for use as multipurpose structures for launch vehicles (acoustic mitigation, thermal insulation) will have multiple benefits to GRC and the community. GRC polyimide aerogel technology advanced in the area of acoustics and in-space conditions, establishes GRC as a leader in acoustic materials development, provides commercial entity licensing polyimide aerogels with acoustic and other properties of the aerogels, advances tech transfer; acoustic properties are cross-cutting technology needs in aero as well as space (aircraft interiors, lightweight multifunctional structures).

The proposed effort is an extension of the partnership between NASA GRC and Aerogel Technologies in commercializing NASA's mechanically strong aerogels with the joint goals of producing improved products, lowering manufacturing costs, on-shoring supply chains, and enabling adoption for space vehicles at NASA and by the commercial space industry. NASA's expertise in materials and characterization would enable the ability for Aerogel Technologies to bring the benefit of these materials back to NASA. Fabrication of multi-functional polyimide aerogel with properties tailored for aerospace will enable design simplification by consolidating disparate functions into a single material envelope. The proposed materials would benefit NASA by light-weighting of rocket payload fairings, aviation interiors, and UAVs via improved sound transmission loss and thermal super-insulating properties unique to aerogels in a dust-free and low-density material envelope.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: As the propulsion capabilities of launch vehicles increase, so does the likelihood of exposure to high intensity acoustic and mechanical vibrations. The harsh vibroacoustic environment, especially at liftoff, by modern heavy lift launch vehicles may damage the launch pad, launch vehicle, and/or payloads. While the external acoustic environment can be mitigated through the deflection of acoustic energy away from the pad and vehicle, it is the internal acoustic environment which must be mitigated through acoustic blocking/attenuation systems located within the vehicle. Future Space Launch System (SLS) configurations (e.g., Block 1-B Cargo, Block 2 Crew, Block 2 Cargo, etc.) will deliver payloads and co-manifested payloads to their desired trajectory, through that process exposing them to the internal acoustic environments within their respective enclosures. Therefore, NASA has a need to implement improved noise reduction systems to ensure crew safety, secure environments for sensitive payload, and overall mission success. The Mechanically Strong Polyimide Aerogels Project was an extension of the partnership between NASA GRC and Aerogel Technologies in commercializing NASA’s mechanically strong aerogels with the joint goals of producing improved products, lowering manufacturing costs, onshoring supply chains, and enabling adoption for space vehicles at NASA and by the commercial space industry. Polyimide aerogels are open-celled, three-dimensional structures formed by the self-assembly of polymeric oligomers resulting in a superior, advanced material well-suited for aeronautics and aerospace applications in extreme environments. The primary goal of MSPA was to advance the state of the art in aerogel materials and quantify how a melamine aerogel composite (MAC)-(PCT/US2020/05036) would perform in diffuse field absorption and transmission loss testing targeting a space launch environment. More specifically, the aerogel formulation infused into the compressed melamine foam is of a polyimide base. Final project results demonstrated improved acoustic absorption at low frequencies (<500Hz), improved reflectance above 500Hz, and significant transmission loss outperforming SOA melamine foam at similar thickness to the aerogel composite. In addition, MSPA achieved other technical advances. This included the introduction of hydrophobicity and water uptake resistance through monomer selection and patterning, optimization of acoustic and vibration attenuation properties, thermal insulating properties, improved structural integrity, formulation of materials using cost-effective, scalable, domestically sourceable monomers; and formulation of materials that pass flammability requirements of FAR 25.853. Materials characterizations performed included: baseline property analysis, sound transmission loss and absorption properties; materials properties as a function of temperature from 20 to 100°F; transient liquid water uptake and properties upon drying; properties after exposure to humidity; encapsulated heat flux; outgassing mass loss; non-volatile cleanliness; molecular contamination; shelf life estimates from accelerated testing; and material tolerance to vibration environment. Overall, MSPA successfully met its goals with the exception that it was unable to perform any of the agreed upon fabrication and optimization due to inability to access the laboratories due to COVID. Also, the project lifecycle was extended by a year and had had a cost overrun, both, primarily again due to COVID. A noteworthy item is that the success of this project has resulted in a new pilot plant built by Aerogel Technologies, LLC in Boston, MA. The new material has an improved structural integrity and as the technology and manufacturing methods matured through the project, large sheets of this material are now available for installation on aerospace vehicles, providing acoustic and thermal insulation while reducing weight and volume.
