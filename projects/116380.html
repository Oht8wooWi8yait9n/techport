Project Description

PRIME-1 (Polar Resources Ice Mining Experiment-1) will develop a flight-ready instrumentation package that can assess the volatiles at a polar lunar landing location. PRIME-1 is the combination of two high-TRL instruments; Mass Spectrometer observing lunar operations (MSolo) and The Regolith and Ice Drill for Exploring New Terrain (TRIDENT).

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

An initial detailing of the vertical extent of volatiles at a ice-stable polar location from a static CLPS lander will inform insitu resource models and design parameters.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The goal of this task was to compare, through thermal vacuum (TVac) testing, the ability of two, high-TRL drills to deliver water-enriched, lunar regolith simulant (LHT-3M) samples for identification and quantification of water using the high-TRL mass spectrometer, MSolo (Mass Spectrometer observing lunar operations). The two drilling configurations are TRIDENT (The Regolith and Ice Drill for Exploring New Terrain) and PVEx (Planetary Volatile Extractor). Likewise, two configurations of MSolo were also tested in parallel to determine the best configuration to be paired with the drill; an Open Ion Source and a Cross Beam Ion Source. Both drills were fully capable in terms of drilling operations including; achieving drill depths at repeatable timelines, delivering sample to the appropriate location (for TRIDENT - the soil surface, for PVEx - in the drill core), operating at repeatable and measured power profiles, and drilling multiple times at thermal vacuum conditions. However, the signal response from the MSolo units was more repeatable for the TRIDENT drill.
