MOSAIC: Miniature Optical Steered Antenna for Intersatellite Communication

NASA TechPort Project ID: 96198
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

MOSAIC will develop a CubeSat-compatible module capable of receiving and transmitting laser beams in any direction over a hemisphere using a combination of liquid lens technology and fixed lenses, and without using a gimbal or mechanical actuation. This project seeks an alternative to mechanically-based steering systems by using new beam steering technologies, such as liquid lenses, that have not yet been applied to satellites. Laser beams are passed through a series of liquid lenses that are offset from the optical axis, which allows the liquid lens to deflect the beam a few degrees by adjusting its focal length (by applying a voltage to the lens). After the liquid lens relay a fisheye fixed lens then amplifies the angular deflection.This architecture can address over 1.5 pi steradians, nearly an entire hemisphere, and two pairs of transmitters on opposite sides of a spacecraft would allow the spacecraft to address the entire 4 pi steradian sphere, without the use of mechanical actuation or requiring the spacecraft to body-point.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

MOSAIC will allow for laser transmission over a hemisphere without the need for mechanical actuators or spacecraft body-pointing.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Conventional beam steering technologies lie on two extreme ends of an axis of SWaP, power handling, and angular throw. Gimbals have poor size, weight, and power (SWaP), but excellent power handling and high angular throw, whereas MEMS fast steering mirrors (FSMs) have excellent SWaP, but limited angular throw and cannot couple large amounts of power. We envision liquid lenses as a technology that lies in between these two options, although until this project, they were not qualified for space applications. The MOSAIC project has completed a detailed and rigorous space environmental testing campaign, consisting of thermal vacuum, radiation, and microgravity testing. Detailed analytic and simulation-based models were constructed and qualified using a laboratory prototype. Through these methods, we proved that hemispherical steering using liquid lenses and additional optics is possible. Simulation results also fed into a detailed link budget, allowing full system sizing and performance evaluation. Overall, the liquid lens components used in this project have been brought to TRL 5-6. We see many side applications to liquid lenses as well, for example in increasing beaconing performance in lasercom missions such as CLICK, as well as compensation for thermal effects in telescopes and other optical systems.
