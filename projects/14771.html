Broad Bandwidth Meta-Material Antireflection Coatings for Measurement of the Cosmic Microwave Background

NASA TechPort Project ID: 14771
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

We propose to develop broad-band metamaterial antireflection (AR) coatings for the far-infrared and millimeter wave bands. The proposed coating technology could realize an unprecedented combination of low reflectance, negligible dielectric loss, and broad bandwidth for large diameter cryogenic refractive optics. The projected performance of the proposed technology matches the desired attributes for optical coatings intended for astrophysical instrumentation. The effort's objective is to realize broadband low-reflectance coatings. The two key technical challenges are the design of the precision engineered materials and their characterization.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Broadband meta-material coatings are applicable in the millimeter and sub-millimeter for astrophysical instrumentation (e.g., CMBpol, SOFIA, SPIDER) and remote sensing. This technology also enables compact THz imagers for concealed threat detection and spectrometers for explosives detection.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To (National Science Foundation): This CIF effort was completed. Manufacturing was then transitioned to NASA and National Science foundation (NSF) project funding.
  • Advanced To
  • Advanced To
  • Transitioned To (Astrophysics Research and Analysis): Direct extensions of this manufacturing approach for polarization modulation structures and thermal-blocking filters have been accepted for Astrophysics Research and Analysis (APRA) Program funding and test structures deployed in the field.
  • Transitioned To (Department of Energy): Refractive optics antireflection (AR) coatings based on this technology have been proposed for use in the Simons Observatory and Department of Energy (DOE)/NSFs Cosmic Microwave Background Stage 4 (CMB-S4) project.
  • Infused To: The technology has been adopted by NASA's Primordial Inflation Polarization Explorer (PIPER).
  • Infused To: The technology has been adopted by the Atacama Technology Telescope.
  • Infused To: The technology has been adopted by the Goddard-IRAM (Institut de Radioastronomie Millim\xe9trique) Superconducting 2-Millimeter Observer (GISMO II).
