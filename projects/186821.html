Predicting RDRE Thermal Loads and Performance through Accurate Detonation Modeling with High Fidelity Reactive Computational Fluid Dynamics

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186821
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This goal of the research project presented in this proposal is to develop an efficient computational tool to predict wall heat fluxes in rotating detonation rocket engines (RDREs). This is to be accomplished by first developing a computational fluid dynamics (CFD) model of an RDRE fluid domain which accurately captures the mixing behavior between the fuel and oxidizer as well as deflagration occurrences within the combustion chamber, as these are vital for obtaining physically accurate detonation wave characteristics and thus a physically accurate combustion chamber environment. Precise heat loads will be obtained and applied to a finite element model of the RDRE to obtain the wall heat fluxes. The tool developed will be adapted to a variety of cases representing realistic RDRE operating conditions to assess robustness. The current CFD model of a methane-oxygen RDRE created by the BEFAST lab at NC State university will be further developed to accurately model mixing and deflagration, with specific CFD considerations including turbulence models, mesh refinement and boundary layer modeling, chemical reaction mechanisms, turbulent flame models, and flame thickening methods. Precise heat loads at the walls will be obtained using a boundary-layer resolved CFD mesh. A finite element model of the RDRE will be developed and refined in the wall regions near the detonation wave. Temperature profiles from CFD will be applied to this model to obtain wall heat fluxes. Experimental data will be collected from BEFAST's RDRE using a load cell, PCB pressure sensors, surface thermocouples, high speed cameras, and accelerometers. Comparisons between CFD and experiments will further inform the computational model. The proposed project directly addresses the low TRL space technology research area identified by STMD of Efficient Physics-Informed Computational Fluid Dynamics Simulations of Propulsion Systems, specifically relating to rotating detonation rocket engines, by seeking to develop tools which will allow for the fast and accurate prediction of wall heat fluxes in newly designed RDREs. This is crucial for designing cooling systems which will allow RDREs to operate for prolonged periods of time and enable the benefits they offer to be realized in practical applications, including higher efficiencies, higher specific impulses, and smaller engine architectures. This developing propulsion technology has potential for broad applicability to any NASA space endeavors, including those in the near future such as NASA's Moon to Mars vision.
