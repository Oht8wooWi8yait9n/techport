Project Description

​Precision Combustion, Inc. (PCI), in conjunction with our NASA partners, is proposing to develop and demonstrate a TRL-5, air-independent, solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) stack for generating power directly from CH4/LOX propellants and scavenged resources (e.g., in-situ resource utilization) for lunar surface applications​. The design is also suitable for regenerative operation to support the Moon-to-Mars initiative. The effort will address key technology gaps of electrochemically reacting CH4 and LOX to generate electricity (with only CO2 and water as byproducts) for space applications. The proposed approach offers improved performance, reusability, and contamination tolerance for surface operations, as well as fluidcommonality with propellants, scavenged resources, and ISRU gases of interest. The effort will integrate multiple innovative elements,providing a significant adva​ncement in technology maturation with system demonstration, and building upon substantial prior NASAsupport and private sector development with commercial intent. Testing of the deliverable component to meet robustness requirements(e.g. life, thermal cycle/vacuum/vibration tolerance) for space applications will be confirmed. System integration designs will also bevalidated.The effort will address key technology gaps of electrochemically reacting CH4 and LOX to generate electricity (with only CO2 andwater as byproducts) for space applications. Suitability of system operational conditions to support mission requirements will beassessed. The research goal is to develop and advance ISRU technologies which enable acquisition of lunar resources and processinginto mission consumables and develop lunar surface power generation. Our proposal will develop a lunar surface tipping pointtechnology and integrated system capability for demonstration in early lunar payloads for Sustainable Surface Power. Our SOFCminimizes the components required for sustainable power generation directly from propellants or methane obtained from in-situsources, and is preferable over current state-of-the-art systems, which cannot operate directly with CH4. Novel packaging designsfor vacuum and pressurized operation and materials that comply with air-independent operation will be implemented. Our novelsystem and sub-components advance the ability to utilize planetary resources for surface operations while reducing risks for humanexploration. The power generator system architecture includes thermal management and turnkey system integration (e.g., controls,electronics, and packaging) for significant improvements in system robustness and reliability.​​

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

​The proposed approach offers improved performance, reusability, and contamination tolerance for surface operations, as well as fluid commonality with propellants, scavenged resources, and ISRU gases of interest. The PropFC-SBIR project will develop a SOFC stack for generating power directly from CH4/LOX propellants enabling sustainable surface power for lunar missions. This implementation of SOFC technology will also enable lunar surface power generation and storage capabilities from available lunar resources with focus on power generation.​ Using residual propellants eliminates dedicated tanks for fuel cell system reactants. High temperature solid oxide technology operation compatible with high temperature ISRU processes and tolerant of probable ISRU contaminants. Crygogenic temperature cold soak capability may provide a pre positioned power source for crewed missions.This will help allow and support NASA's mission to establish a human presence on the moon and help with power in situ resource utilization (ISRU) hardware used in space applications. Also this technology will help enabling sustainble surface power for lunar missions.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: During implementation of the Propellant-fueled Solid Oxide Fuel Cell (PropFC) Project, Precision Combustion, Inc. (PCI), with support from the NASA Glenn Research Center (GRC) and the NASA Johnson Space Center (JSC), implemented a stack-level test campaign and system-level trade study to assess and advance solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) technology for directly converting residual CH4/O2 propellants into electrical power for lunar surface applications. Based on a power-dense, 250-Watt modular SOFC stack design developed with prior NASA support, PCI developed an air-independent SOFC stack generating power directly from propellant-grade CH4/O2propellants. For sustainable power generation directly from methane propellants, this SOFC technology minimizes the components required relative to current state-of-the art (SOA) systems which cannot operate directly with CH4and advances the ability for surface operations while reducing risks for human exploration. The implementation approach improved performance, reusability, and contaminant tolerance for surface operations as well as fluid commonality with propellants, scavenged resources, and In Situ Resource Utilization (ISRU) product gases of interest. Tasks focused on developing a lunar surface technology by demonstration in representative environments for Sustainable Surface Power applications. System-level designs were generated, reviewed, and assessed. The power generator system architecture and design configuration included thermal management with turnkey system integration (e.g., controls, electronics, and packaging) for improved system robustness and reliability over SOA. The SOFC system trade study identified many required system elements, and support capabilities are being developed for other surface applications (e.g., freeze-tolerant avionics and power electronics, freeze-tolerant water reservoirs, fluidic connectors, dust mitigation, etc.). Although viable SOFC system designs for scavenging CH4/O2 propellant to generate heat, water, and electricity were established using current SOA balance of plant (BOP), they exceeded the imposed energy storage mass limit for this study (25 kW·h within 190 kg). Tradeoffs between stack size, efficiency, and BOP were examined and determined that operating the fuel cell stack at a lower current density reduced the overall system mass. System level technical and design challenges include improved SOFC cell performance durability, efficient CO/CO2 separation, compatible materials for recirculation components, and an improved thermal management system. Finally, assessment of air-independent operation (i.e., suitable operating conditions, materials of construction, and safety parameters) was completed. A vibration-tolerant stacking design was assembled and evaluated. Ceramic SOFC cells were used as a stand in for the advanced metal-supported SOFC cells under development in a parallel effort. Metal-supported cells offer a path to overcoming technical gaps and risks for achieving high efficiency, reliable long-term operation, tolerance of deep thermal cycling, and mitigation of contamination risk for lunar operation (e.g., dust, iron, and aluminum). It should be noted that commercially available terrestrial SOFC stacks cannot safely operate with propellant-grade oxygen nor tolerate vibration, shock, or cyclic thermal environmental stresses experienced during Space missions. The lessons learned from the PropFC effort include detailed performance information resulting from vibration, shock, and cyclic thermal loading tests. These are directly applicable for improved future advanced SOFC stack designs. This project resulted in a successful SOFC hot-box design with improved mechanical support and reduced leakage over SOA. PCI infused this hot-box design into multiple efforts for commercial and research hardware.
