Project Description

This effort will develop and test advanced Artificial Intelligence/Machine Learning (AI/ML) techniques for path planning/adaptive observing using a suite of airborne terrestrial and ocean remote sensing (RS) instruments. An airborne computational and instrument capability would be used for both reactive and limited memory AI tasks. The present focus is to transform the observing capability of a gimbled dual-axis hyper spectrometer (180-1200nm, 4096 wavebands) from that of a deterministic programmed scanning capability to an Ai-directed scanning process. The effort would demonstrate using AI/ML to direct remote data collection, test several AI/ML embedded developer packages (Google TPU, NVIDIA GPU), and finalize the requirements and a plan to move the capability forward for supporting NASA in its goal to develop autonomous observing instruments and systems. This year's effort will focus on development and testing of AI applications using observations obtain from aircraft surveys obtained in 2022 and it will develop a plan embedding the AI components into a prototype aircraft system.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The technology that is being developed aims to support the capability of carrying out fully autonomous scientific observation and/or applied surveillance or Earth features. The technology aims to use embedded AI/ML components to derive information, such as inference maps, from real time observations from thermal, visible, and hyper spectral imagery in order to enable a decision-making software to control an optical pointing instrument for collection of finer-scale hyper spectral observations. This effort will support NASA Decadal Survey Incubation opportunities for the Planetary boundary Layer (PLB), Surface Topography and Vegetation (STV) and Surface Biology and Geology (SBG) science pathfinder and incubation funding opportunities (i.e., ESTO Decadal Survey Incubation).

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This IRAD project successfully established the foundational technology for autonomous, AI-controlled hyperspectral observations using actively-pointing spectrometer systems. The effort achieved its primary objective of developing and validating software frameworks for enabling artificial intelligence to control a 2-axis gimballed mirror system for science-driven data collection. Key technical accomplishments included creating Python-based data ingestion routines for Headwall Nano-Hyperspec systems, developing servo motor control algorithms, and implementing an Asynchronous Advantage Actor-Critic (A3C) reinforcement learning model within the gymnasium simulation environment. The project overcame significant technical challenges in AI implementation across diverse computing architectures and established innovative training methodologies using optimal interpolation techniques to evaluate autonomous observation strategies against flight data. While procurement requirements were minimal, the allocated time was effectively utilized to advance the technology from concept to testable algorithms ready for hardware integration. Collaborative partnerships with the WFF Navy UAV team and Code 513 have positioned this technology for potential demonstration flights, representing a critical step toward operational deployment. This work provides NASA with proven capability foundations for autonomous science exploration that extend beyond traditional mapping approaches to enable real-time, intelligent observation strategies. The technology demonstrates significant potential for applications ranging from Earth science anomaly detection to planetary surface exploration, establishing a versatile platform for AI-enabled autonomous vehicles across multiple mission domains. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
