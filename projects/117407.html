Project Description

The Digital Astronaut Simuation for Lunar Surface Operations project is important for providing pre-flight insight into the representativeness of our ground-based training. ADP areas will be addressed through assessment of relevant task kinematics & kinetics to ultimately inform lunar sampling and surface science approaches. Phase 1 – ADP 6: Lunar sampling R&D for Reqts & Approach; ADP 7: Surface science prioritization, planning, and ops prioritization development & integration. We regularly apply human modeling capabilities in exercise related projects. In CIF Year 1, we explored the terms which can be used to inform lunar applications and incorporated them as available outputs from our tool, investigated human model improvements, and completed limited unsuited laboratory testing. Current biomechanics modeling efforts in industry, academia, or the government are generally limited to 1G environments and primarily target rehabilitation and sports performance.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The Digital Astronaut Simuation for Lunar Surface Operations will use data collection paired with human biomechanics modeling to observe the lunar surface task motion in 1/6G and assess dynamic feasibility, including indicators of falling. It is our intent to test this technology in relevant settings and increase fidelity of models to represent xEVA aspects. This capability can be used as a tool for assessing ground-based training. Enhanced understanding of potential differences between training and flight will benefit mission safety & success.
