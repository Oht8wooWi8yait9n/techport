Mars Entry Descent and Landing Instrumentation 2 (MEDLI2)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 16854
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

MEDLI2 will reduce uncertainty in aeroshell TPS response with the potential to reduce TPS mass by up to 130 kg for a Mars 2020 class mission. MEDLI2 will reduce uncertainty in supersonic aerodynamic predictions with the potential to reduce the landing footprint size by 30%. MEDLI2 fills critical EDL knowledge gaps which represent a technology advancement in EDL. MEDLI2 advances instrumentation development essential for EDL analysis.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Improved understanding of risk and performance leading to the ability to reduce entry mass and reduce size of landed footprint.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To (Science Mission Directorate): Flew on Mars 2020 Perseverence
  • Closed Out: The Mars Entry, Descent, and Landing Instrumentation 2 (MEDLI2) project provides critical environmental and system performance data for entry vehicles during the Entry, Descent, and Landing (EDL) phase. MEDLI2 derives its implementation approach and heritage from the highly successful MEDLI sensor suite flown on the Mars Science Laboratory (MSL) Entry Vehicle (EV). MEDLI2 is a Mars 2020 Flight System managed payload installed on the Mars 2020 mission entry vehicle with the MEDLI2 data being returned through the Mars 2020 system. The MEDLI2 project’s main objective is to measure aerothermal environments, Thermal Protection System (TPS) material response, vehicle orientation, aerodynamics, and atmospheric density and winds for the atmospheric entry – through the sensible atmosphere down to heatshield (HS) separation – of the Mars 2020 mission (see MEDLI2-0002 – Instrument Requirements Document). The funding programs have concurred that MEDLI2-0002 is sufficient to capture their performance goals and objectives. MEDLI2 delivers data which extends the data set from MEDLI, to include aftbody effects (heating and drag), increased spatial observations of the heatshield aerothermal environment and associated TPS response, and direct observations of the aerodynamic loads applied during the supersonic flight phase. MEDLI2 is also designed to record atmospheric density during the entry and descent phases and atmospheric winds from the start of supersonic flight (freestream Mach Number 5.0) through the end of the MEDLI2 experiment. Coupling the MEDLI and MEDLI2 data sets allows EDL systems to be assessed across the critical entry and descent phases which could lead to reductions in TPS mass (reduced uncertainties with associated reductions in TPS), reductions in the size of the landed uncertainty footprint (reduced aerodynamic uncertainties with higher fidelity trajectory estimation), and reductions in the predicted or design loads for the entry vehicle and its payload (reduced aerodynamic uncertainties with higher fidelity trajectory estimation).
