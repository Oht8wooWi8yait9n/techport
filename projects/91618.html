Electrostatic Droplet Dispensing System

NASA TechPort Project ID: 91618
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Develop and test an electrostatic spray device, including new control electronics and temperature control and dispensing of different fluid types

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

3D Printing of microcircuits. Breakthrough in 3D technology; opens up many new types of ISS and other experiments in colloidal physics, colloidal engineering, particles in viscous colloidal suspensions, and liquid crystals.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To: FY18 Annual Report Comments: Last year (2018) part of the proposed LCF Liquid Crystal Facility, an ongoing NASA flight project, included the CIF work that we worked on , i.e. Femto Jet Dispenser One of the PI s from Kent State University is improving this device to be integrated into the LCF. The CIF was the proof of concept to show that the femto jet is capable of dispensing 1) high viscous fluids 2) the dispensing droplet size is less than femto liter It was demonstrated with 8CB liquid crystal, the required voltage for the Tayler instability to draw the droplet from the dispenser needle was 2 KV LCF needs low voltage dispensing and the droplet noozle head must be miniaturized. The improvement is ongoing effort at the PI lab and it should go down to 100s of Volt from 2KV , the dispenser can be miniatured now. -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- The femtojet from the CIF is being proposed to develop in the LCF (Liquid Crystal Facility) series of space experiment. The CIF work demonstrated the concept and feasibility of using a high viscous liquid with the droplet volume of sub femto liter. The LCF facility Principal Investigators will make use of this femto jet droplet dispenser for making near identical island domains on a freely suspended liquid crystal to study the dipole quadrupole interactions of the stacked structures.
