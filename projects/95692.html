PIPES: Piezoelectric Instrument for Precision Exploration Sampling

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95692
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The objective of this project is to develop a Piezoelectric Instrument for Precision Exploration Sampling (PIPES). PIPES is a miniaturized liquid sample acquisition and handling system, which looks like a straw and is powered by a hand-held box. The “straw” contains multiple flow paths for parallel sampling. Enabled by 3D printing, PIPES integrates acoustic actuation to achieve multiple sampling functions, including pumping, filtering, and mixing, in one compact unit. The PIPES system seeks to fill a gap in NASA’s current in-flight sensing capabilities: the lack of miniaturized fluidic sampling and handling systems. The PIPES will make dramatic improvements at the system level in terms of weight, cost and operational simplicity. Moreover, as a versatile micro- to meso-scale fluidic sampling system, the PIPES can work with multiple meso- and micro-scale detection technologies such as electrochemical sensors, micromachined chromatographs and miniature mass spectrometers. Therefore, PIPES has great crosscutting potential in biomedical and environmental applications.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

PIPES as an integrated end-to-end in situ system will make sampling in space exploration more effective. The low-weight, low-cost and simple operation is particularly suitable for space exploration. PIPES as a versatile system that can work with multiple detection instrument systems has great crosscutting potential in biomedical and environmental applications.

  • Closed Out: The developed Piezoelectric Instrument for Precision Exploration Sampling (PIPES) is a state-of-the-art miniaturized liquid sampling system actuated by acoustic waves and is potentially suitable for space applications. Compared to existing sample handing techniques, the PIPES system is the first integrated end-to-end in situ sample acquisition and handling system that capitalizes on acoustofluidic phenomena and is suitable for detecting analytes in spaceflight environment. During the project, we studied the key mechanisms employed in the PIPES system: the microbubble-induced microstreaming and secondary radiation force under the acoustic actuation. We tackled the problem using theoretical, numerical and experimental approaches. By study of the interactions among acoustics, microbubbles, microflows and microstructures with the help of innovative 3D fabrication methods, we successfully developed systems to pump, filter and mix samples for various applications, such as bidirectional fluid transportation, cell separation, analytes mixing, enrichment, single cell trapping and bacteria detection. Overall, the project is very fruitful, and a number of important results have been obtained. Briefly, the major accomplishments include: (1) Established analytical and numerical models to describe the working frequencies of microbubbles for both microstreaming and secondary radiation force; (2) Developed the optimized pumping, filtering, mixing system using acoustically actuated microbubbles and applied the system in various biological and chemical applications; and (3) Prototyped a highly integrated and compact sample acquisition and handling system (PIPES) via innovative 3D printing technology. The high controllability (flow velocity control, flow direction control, sample trajectory control), versatility (multi-functional sample processing: pumping, filtering and mixing) and the flexibility (relying on the resonance of microstructures, which are rapid-prototypable via 3D printing in space) of PIPES makes it suitable for space sample acquisition and handling applications. On the other hand, the integration of PIPES with a biosensor demonstrated the adaptivity to various detection methods for detecting a wide range of analytes. It is also benefit on-earth analytical procedures in many areas such as biomedical detection and environmental monitoring. Therefore, with development of this technology, potential technology transition into NASA and industrial applications is promising. Lastly, this project also contributes to fundamental research by advancing the state-of-knowledge in both the microfluidics and the biosensing fields.
