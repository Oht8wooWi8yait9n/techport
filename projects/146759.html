Multipurpose Coherent Optical Ranging and Communications between CubeSats

NASA TechPort Project ID: 146759
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

We propose to investigate high precision coherent optical ranging on communication links, intended as a demonstration between a pair of CubeSats. The targeted outcome of this effort is the capability to provide range and range-rate measurements from the optical link at varying levels of precision to meet the requirements of a variety of end users. We will 1) determine the requirements for each end user/application (navigation, Earth science, planetary science), 2) define the system architecture based on these requirements, and 3) identify and characterize key components and subsystems. In the future, we seek to further mature and validate the relevant technologies for the different end use cases, with the ultimate goal of a CubeSat demonstration mission.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This effort will enable high precision ranging on coherent optical links with small form-factor terminals, and these range and range-measurements can be leveraged for improved spacecraft navigation and for scientific applications. For the former, this technology will advance autonomous navigation for constellations, and for the latter, this technology will enable satellite-to-satellite optical ranging-based Mass Change (MC) missions on Earth and other planets. Beyond planetary geodesy, these high precision range measurements can also lead to the enabling of scientific missions for fundamental physics and the enabling of Distributed Spacecraft Missions (DSMs) for applications in heliophysics.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: By the end of this effort, we determined and documented the ranging requirements and relevant system considerations for several application cases in navigation, Earth science, and planetary science. Based on these cases, we defined a target mission application, for which we developed a preliminary system architecture and conducted trade studies to select the components critical to the ranging performance. This effort lays the foundation for a future lab-based demonstration of the high precision ranging capability. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
