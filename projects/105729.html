Model-based Approaches to Distributed Systems Missions Capability Development and Demonstration (DSM-MBSE)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 105729
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Applying model-based systems engineering concepts and approaches to guide a variety of Distributed System Mission concepts and research.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Better coordinate GSFC's Distributed Systems Mission research portfolio.

Improve understanding of the implications of precision formation flying and constellation management.

Advance Center's state of Model-Based Systems Engineering practice, including templates and reference models, to lower the barrier for entry for future projects.

Help define community standards for constellation and precision formation coordination.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Utilized the Distributed Systems Mission Radical Innovation Initiative to document reference mission and supported field drone mission (NAMASTE) to establish modeling constructs to accelerate future mission development. Constructs include support for Requirements management (and product generation), Organizational modeling, Concept of Operations documentation, Test Planning documentation and Interface management. Requirements Management capabilities include track requirements, documenting and assessing completeness of traceability between requirements, as well as generation of the associate requirements products for project usage and milestone reviews. Generated a sample mission concept of operations for an Enceladus design reference mission, which provides a reusable template for future mission conOps products. Generated Test Planning documentation to support evolution of the DSM capabilities to support the NAMASTE field test campaign, as well as lay the foundation for the Center template system model to document and track verification and validation efforts. Instantiated representative interface diagrams for the drones used for NAMASTE. Products generated are being leveraged by a number of projects including from multiple centers across the agency, as well as helping provide recommended practices at the Agency Model-Based Systems Engineering community of practice. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
