Long-Wave Infrared Upgrades for the CHARMS Refractometer

NASA TechPort Project ID: 157699
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This project will extend the measurement capabilities of the Cryogenic High Accuracy Refraction Measuring System (CHARMS) facility into the long wave infrared (LWIR) spectral region. The CHARMS refractometer is one of the distinctives capabilities within the Optics Branch at GSFC. This refractometer was originally developed in the early 2000’s to provide highly accurate cryogenic index of refraction measurements of the various glass materials that were used in the Near-Infrared Camera (NIRCAM) instrument of the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST). Since then, CHARMS has contributed critical index data of materials, particularly at cryogenic temperatures of a large variety of glass substrates (over more than 30 different types) for which data had not previously been available. These data have enabled successful design and implementation of refractive both developed by NASA and other institutions around the world.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This project will address multiple Goddard 2040 Strategic Vectors (1, 2, 3, and 5). For example, developing the proposed LWIR upgrades for CHARMS will augment Goddard’s distinctive capability of instrument building and oversight. Prospective CHARMS customers from NASA and beyond will be able to develop more accurate optical models based on the extended wavelength coverage provided by the LWIR upgrade in the CHARMS refractometer. Optical designs with refractive components operating at cryogenic temperatures in the LWIR spectral range are expected to be developed by NASA and outside institutions such as university and aerospace agencies around the world.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The capability performing cryogenic index measurements in the LWIR will bring further Space Act Agreement opportunities with the private sector. This is an area where competitiveness is lacking in the private sector given the expertise that is required to perform these measurements with the required precision that CHARMS can provide. Currently there is interest in performing accurate and precise refractometry from 1-12 µm. There is only one major commercial competitor in this field. However, CHARMS is the only refractometer which provides higher resolution than the technique used by the commercial competitor. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies. Goddard Space Flight Center’s IRAD program is managed under its Office of Chief Technologist. Activities develop concepts, reduce technology risk, and advance human capital and technological capabilities. Goddard’s IRAD program is highly competitive, opportunity-driven, and 100% strategically aligned with NASA's and Goddard's strategic priorities. A significant portion of the program is focused on Early Stage Innovations for high-risk, strategically aligned, potential high-payoff technologies that are longer-term or lower TRL.
