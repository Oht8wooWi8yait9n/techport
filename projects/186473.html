Project Description

There currently exists a significant gap in the experimental literature for heat transfer efficiency of propellant spray impingement on superheated flight-like surfaces. Because of this knowledge gap, modeling uncertainties in heat rates during planned operations, such as in-orbit tank chilldown, force conservative propellant margins and prolonged potential loiter times which lead to ineffective mission planning and potentially unfeasible mission success.

This work aims to address some of these unique cryogenic microgravity issues through reduced order ground experimentation using existing MSFC testbeds and development of novel CFD methods with partners in academia. Additionally, this work is uniquely positioned to be generalized for lunar thermal management systems including in-situ resource utilization, heat pump systems, and data center computing operations.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

NASA’s anticipated use-cases for liquid sprays in chilldown and thermal management systems will often operate at scales where spray volumetric fluxes are orders of magnitude lower than those commonly reported in open literature. Compounding this challenge is the limited availability of research using true cryogenic fluids and flight‑representative surface materials. To address this gap, the proposed work will derive a novel predictive correlation tailored to current modeling deficiencies for spray heat transfer coefficient by performing experiments at MSFC.

CFD simulations directed at addressing these same problems are currently in need of efficient methods for capturing temperature gradients and transport phenomena near multi-phase (liquid-vapor) interfaces. Through collaboration with UF, this proposal seeks to validate and mature this model. Additionally, this work will demonstrate that coupling machine‑learning techniques to the phase‑change model can reduce computational cost significantly. Leveraging these approaches can enable broad parametric sweeps that are currently infeasible with conventional CFD runtimes.
