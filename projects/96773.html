Electron Spectrometer Testing and Demonstration for Rocket Altitudes of 100 to 200 km

NASA TechPort Project ID: 96773
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Our objective is to test a magnetic electron spectrometer in a simulated vacuum environment for an altitude range of 100-200 km and then to also demonstrate it in flight.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Testing the operation of such an instrument, which employees high voltage, for lower altitudes would open up avenues for its use at those altitudes. This is important because such measurements at those altitudes, on board sounding rockets, have not typically been possible.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: We designed and built the basic set up of an MCP assembly that we could test. We bench tested it at atmospheric pressure with positive results. We also flew two complete instruments with a similar MCP assembly below 200 km with positive results (at about 164 km altitude, the lowest we have ever turned on the high voltage for such an instrument). The degree to which we can operate this set up at even lower pressures, which correspond to the intermediate altitude range of about 100 km, is still uncertain and requires further amendment of our test set up and testing.
