Linking Thermophysical Properties and Electrochemical Behavior in Molten Regolith Electrolysis

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186825
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Future sustained lunar missions will require the ability to produce materials directly from lunar soil to reduce reliance on Earth-supplied resources. Molten regolith electrolysis (MRE) is a high-temperature process that can convert lunar regolith into useful metals while releasing oxygen as a byproduct, making it a promising technology for sustainable lunar infrastructure. However, current MRE research lacks experimentally validated understanding of how regolith composition, temperature, and electrochemical operating conditions influence metal production behavior, limiting the technology's readiness for surface deployment. This research aims to advance MRE from laboratory feasibility toward subsystem-level validation by experimentally linking material properties, electrochemical response, and process performance under lunar-relevant conditions. The work is organized into three phases. First, the thermodynamic and thermophysical properties of south polar--relevant lunar regolith simulants and their mineral components will be measured to understand melting behavior and reaction pathways and to establish compositional analogs relevant to expected lunar regolith variability. Second, electrochemical experiments will systematically vary temperature, current, voltage, and pressure to map voltage--current and impedance behavior and relate electrical response to metal product formation across diverse simulant inputs. Third, these experimental results will be integrated into a process-level performance mapping and design-oriented optimization model grounded in measured thermodynamic and electrochemical response, enabling prediction of metal production rates, energy requirements, and sensitivity to feedstock variability and operations conditions. Ultimately, this model will be publicly available and will inform engineering decision-making for future MRE systems. By focusing on metal feedstock production rather than oxygen alone, this research directly supportsNSTGRO26 NASA's Surface Sustainability and Logistics goals. The outcome will be a validated framework that advances molten regolith electrolysis toward Technology Readiness Level 5 and provides design-relevant insight to inform future engineering and deployment of MRE systems for lunar surface manufacturing.
