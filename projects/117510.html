Plasma Assisted Nutrient Recovery from Inedible Biomass via Ash Leaching

NASA TechPort Project ID: 117510
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Sustaining a human presence on the Moon, Mars, or deep space will require closing loops on many life support systems. Some form of agriculture will be required because plants produce the vitamins, antioxidants, and essential oils in our diets that degrade over time in stored foods. In addition, they provide dietary fiber, restore air, and purify water. It is estimated that 93 kg of plant nutrients are required to support one crew member per year. Growing plants will require recycling nutrients trapped in inedible vegetation. Researchers at KSC have investigated the use of a thermal plasma with various carrier gases to thermally degrade inedible plant biomass for nutrient recovery. Previous work demonstrated a thermally degrading environment such as a muffle furnace improved nutrient recovery from inedible biomass prior to an acid leaching process. However, a muffle furnace is an inefficient process. We have explored the use of a small scale, thermal plasma for degradation of pellets to enhance the breakdown of plant stems, leaves, and debris to further close the nutrient loop. Plasma carrier gases such as carbon dioxide (CO2), nitrogen (N2), and air were used to explore variations in recovery and potential chemical by-products. Plasma processed inedible biomass was added to varying concentrations of acid solution for leaching of nutrients (e.g. potassium (K), magnesium (Mg), calcium (Ca), and phosphorus (P)) for reuse in the crop production cycle. We also examined total N2 recovery. Results are presented showing the impact of plasma processing prior to acid leaching on recovery of plant nutrients.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Plasma gasification is an emerging, developing technology in waste treatment. It is of interest due to its nature of a nearly complete breakdown of waste material. These systems and processes could be scaled up to industrial measures for plant nutrients or other valuable compounds. Such a technology could eventually create a closed loop sustainability path and reduce reliance on chemical manufacturing processes.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Researchers at KSC investigated the use of thermal plasma discharge for processing of inedible biomass as a pretreatment for nutrient recovery via acid leaching. For the plasma system employed in the work, a 1 M concentration of hydrochloric acid (HCl) was required in order to obtain greater nutrient recovery than a muffle furnace used in previous works. The combination of plasma treatment and leaching led to a minimum of 17% improvement in the recovery of mineral nutrients. An unexpected discovery during the effort was that plasma activated water (PAW) from another project generates weak nitric acid (HNO3) solution when N2 or air is used as the plasma carrier gas. This may be used as an acid source in space, replacing the consumed acid. Plasma degradation of the pellets enable recovery of carbon (C), oxygen (O2), hydrogen (H2), and N2 that is otherwise lost within muffle furnace digestion. The findings of this work promote further closure of the space crop production cycle during the one year investigation. Future efforts may lead to optimization of the plasma water for acid replacement, increasing throughput of plasma treatment of inedible biomass, and performing closed cycle growth studies for viability.
