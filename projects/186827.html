Integrated Opto-Electronically Mode-Locked Laser for Space Applications

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186827
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Optical frequency combs are essential for various space technology applications including for precision timing references, ranging, wavelength-multiplexed communications, astronomical spectrograph calibration, fly-by planetary spectroscopy, gravitational wave detection, among many others. Current bulk free space or fiber-optic frequency comb sources are not suitable for satellite or airborne applications due to their large size and weight. Photonic integrated devices offer a promising solution to this problem, as the small, chip-scale devices can be packaged and produced at large scale. Among modern frequency comb sources, mode-locked lasers (MLL) are particularly appealing because they can produce ultrashort optical pulses with high peak powers. These peak powers enable parametric nonlinear optical devices, which are important for frequency conversion and spectral broadening toward frequency comb coverage in important wavelength regimes, such as the mid-infrared. Here we propose an integrated opto-electronically mode-locked laser which produces ultrashort pulses and low-noise frequency comb. The opto-electronic mode-locking approach leads to significant noise reduction due to RF opto-electronic oscillation, and this approach removes the need for bulk signal generators to produce the modulation signal for laser mode-locking. Phase 1 of the proposal focuses on demonstrating opto-electronic mode-locking of a thin-film lithium niobate integrated semiconductor laser. This demonstration includes characterization of pulse and frequency comb formation as well as noise reduction due to opto-electronic oscillation. Phase 1 also includes design optimizations of the laser, intracavity electro-optic modulator, and RF feedback components toward goal metrics of sub-picosecond pulses and 1 W peak power on chip. The aim of phase 2 is integration and co-packaging of the photonic and opto-electronic components to improve operational stability and toward a small-volume ultrashort pulse and frequency comb source. The goal of phase 3 is to demonstrate the monolithic integration of the opto-electronically mode-locked laser with an optical parametric oscillator (OPO) on the same thin-film lithium niobate chip. The opto-electronic mode-locking approach can enable stable and robust synchronous pumping of the OPO by the MLL. This combination can produce highly broadband frequency combs into the technologically-important mid-infrared, and the opto-electronic oscillation provides noise reduction across the comb spectrum. The outcome of this proposal is a fully-integrated, small-volume source of low-noise and broadband frequency combs compatible with space-based, airborne, or other portable applications. More specifically, low-noise optical frequency combs are critical for precise clock synchronization across all spacecraft and satellites, which is important for Global Navigation Satellite Systems (GNSS) for navigation and positioning. Low noise frequency combs are also at the forefront of next-generation precision detection and ranging systems. Furthermore, GHz repetition rate, coherent comb sources enable high speed and capacity communication systems based on wavelength division multiplexing. Lastly, the development of space-based, low-noise frequency combs into the mid-infrared opens new avenues for fly-by spectroscopy of planetary atmospheres, in addition to the current and compelling applications of astronomical spectrograph calibration and space-based gravitational wave detection.
