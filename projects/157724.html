Project Description

The highest priority at Wallops Flight Facility is the safety for all employees, customers, and the public for all Project Operations. Per NASA/Goddard Values – the first listed items is SAFETY. “We accomplish amazing feats often involving things that have never been attempted. In all we do, we first ensure our employees, the infrastructure entrusted to us, and most importantly, the public, are safe. Safety permeates and informs all that we do.” However, with increasing launch rates comes great resource challenges, especially in a time where new technologies are being tested at the [desired] fastest pace possible. This paradigm creates a continuous resource deficit with the limited staff in the ground safety office. Furthermore, surges in commercial and government activity, new FAA regulations, and quasi government partners, create higher workloads for seemingly repeat activities. Wallops Ground Safety Team is looking to discover the capabilities and limitations for Artificial Intelligence (AI) to supplement current workloads/workflows.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The overall goal of this project is to explore the ability for AI to augment the Wallops Ground Safety team in the data receiving organization, processing, and product development. The candidate products currently are Ground Safety Data Packages, Ground Safety Plans, Procedures, Fueling Procedures, Payload Safety Data Packages, and similar safety products. Hazards that are desired to be evaluated include Explosives, Pressure, Chemical, Lifting, Electrical, Facility, Occupational Health, Laser, and Radio Frequency. Wallops Ground Safety is looking achieve this goal by working through the three “levels” of AI to create a useful and usable solution. These are (1) level 1 minimum success – approved “Librarian” AI capability, (2) success level 2 – “cognitive reasoning/co-pilot” capability, and (3) level 3 full success – “Generative/Convertive” capability.

Sub-goals per phase include:

Level 1 “Librarian” – Discovery the capabilities and limitations of AI and build the knowledge database for the AI. This Phase is already in work with active internships within Wallops Ground Safety Branch.

  • Determine the host location for the AI (Assuming closed system at this time)
    • Determine the data structure for the host material
      • Evaluate software development requirements within the Safety Office
        • Determine the cybersecurity solutions needed to officially host/implement a solution
          • Develop connections to internal NASA AI partners and possibly other agency partners
          • Determine what makes it “certified” AI (not necessarily have a certified AI in this phase)
          • Consider other Safety Use Cases (Flight, Range, Institutional)

Level 2 “Co-Pilot” – Implement its usage in calculations and operations scheduling/deconfliction. Stretch to support flight safety team calculations. This Phase is part of the proposal request.

  • How to take input of customer data
    • Evaluate cases for Ground Safety hazard calculations
      • Evaluate operational schedule support visualization
        • Evaluate the use of AI for Flight Safety calculations and analysis

Level 3 “Convertive” – Using generative AI to perform compliance review, predict hazards, or offer new solutions. This Phase is part of the proposal request.

  • Determine if AI can be used to develop new mitigation plans/strategies for new hazards or combination of hazards
    • Evaluate the use of AI for “General Institutional Safety” – this means to extend the use of AI for preventative safety measures in tasks that are generally considered “non-hazardous” per NASA range requirements but would still benefit from additional safety review. Examples, construction, electrical connections, maintenance, etc
      • Contract review for safety compliance
        • Determine if AI can be used to deconflict multiple customers hazard areas/processing schedules

Current project timelines for a new customer entry to operations vary from 330 days to 120 days minimum. It is envisioned that this timeline could be reduced by 50-80%, depending on the level of AI involvement.

Efficiency rate improvements are unknown at this time, but estimating between 25-50% efficiency in generating documents, compliance reviewing documents, and complex/multi-hazard deconfliction scenarios. These efficiency rates could equate to 1 – 4 FTE. Or better stated – make a 12-person workforce function as a 13-16 person workforce.

Other efficiencies could exist in prioritizing the work for Ground Safety Analysts so that more time is spent on complex/multi-hazard/high hazard tasks, rather than low hazards that may consume valuable human time. Prescriptive solutions may see significantly higher efficiency rates from AI support.

We also must remain open minded that AI may result in an even higher level of safety than what we are currently operating as it might be able to be “throttled” for quality, predict/recognize trends, or look for solutions that are unknown by local staff but are available in larger databases.
