High-fidelity laser-based imaging of mixing effects on rotating detonation combustion for space propulsion

NASA TechPort Project ID: 118336
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

An experimental and computational study of a modular rocket RDE operating at various mixing conditions, from non-premixed to fully premixed is proposed. The combustor will be designed to allow for optical access to the flow passages and accurate modeling of the flow field at low computational expense. MHz-rate Acetone PLIF and kHz-rate hybrid fs/ps CARS will be performed inside the combustion chamber for reliable time-resolved fuel mixture fraction imaging and point thermometry. The data will be used in a collaboration with NASA to create a CFD model that reflects the combustor geometry to understand the effects of propellant mixing on wave speeds and detonation structure. This work will help to determine the mixing and combustion physics behind the wave speed discrepancy between experiments and simulations. With this knowledge in hand, modeling efforts can focus on improving RDE design for pressure gain, longer-term operation, stable combustion, efficient power extraction, and propulsion for rocket applications.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: As the rate of improvements in gas turbine and rocket engine technology slows [1], researchers have increasingly sought solutions to provide step changes in efficiency and thrust-to-weight ratios. One of the most promising proposed solutions is the rotating detonation engine (RDE), which employs a novel thermodynamic cycle that can theoretically create a net increase in total pressure across the combustor. The additional total pressure represents increased potential for gas expansion and thrust or shaft work. The mechanism for this ideal pressure gain is the quasi-constant pressure combustion that takes place behind a detonation front that travels azimuthally about the chamber. Extremely high reaction rates create a reaction that occurs too quickly for gas expansion to take place, resulting in fluidic confinement and pressure increase. However, a net total pressure gain has yet to be documented in open literature, partially due to a lack of understanding of complex loss mechanisms and an inability to accurately model the detonation cycle. Improvements in computational modeling will require multi-dimensional high-speed experimental data for comparison and validation. In addition, any experimental reduction in the complexity of the RDE cycle provides opportunities for model tuning and enhanced agreement between simulations and experiments. In an effort to address these needs, a variably premixed H2/air RDE was developed and tested as part of a NASA NSTGRO grant. The objectives of the study were to study the reactant mixing process with laser-based and conventional diagnostics by varying mixing conditions from non-premixed to fully premixed and to provide a simplified experiment for model tuning and validation.
