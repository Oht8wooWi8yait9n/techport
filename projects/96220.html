Electronically Controllable Metasurface Omnidirectional and Multiple Access Optical Antennas for Free-Space Near-Earth Satellite Communication

NASA TechPort Project ID: 96220
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This proposal seeks to exploit rapid advances in chip-based nanophotonics technology to create future laser-based beam steering space communication systems that enable greatly increased spacecraft communications bandwidth, while dramatically decreasing the required size, weight and power (SWaP). In particular, we will leverage our recent development of tunable metasurface chip-based nanophotonic antenna phased arrays, operating in the C-band wavelength regime (1530-1565 nm) to create ultracompact low-power optical phased arrays capable of electronic beam steering. This chip-based optical beam steering component technology will be used to experimentally demonstrate a prototype omni-directional optical antenna whose active elements enable operation over an angular range of 4pi steradians, with no moving parts. Also, multi-bands multiple access will be investigated.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To (Department of Defense): Time Modulated Flat Optics
  • Closed Out: Free space optical communications hold the potential to bring internet-like speeds (exceeding Gb/s) to data transmission in space. One of the major challenges of free space optical communications is accurate pointing and tracking of directed beams. Within the scope of the NASA ESI project, we developed chip-scale electronically steerable active metasurface apertures, which enable accurate beaming with ultralow size, weight and power, and with no mechanical moving parts. Our active metasurface chip-scale steerable apertures are flat optical elements that can dynamically control the wavefront of the reflected or transmitted light using arrays of subwavelength scale tunable antennas. By electro-optical control of the phase of the scattered light from each identical individual metasurface element, we demonstrated a prototype multifunctional programmable metasurface that is capable of both dynamic beam steering and reconfigurable light focusing. The active metasurface chip enables ultrafast ultrafast (~1 μs) beam pointing with a field of view of ±70° and is capable of steering Watt-level beam power. Non-mechanical beam steering eliminates the need for bulky gimbals and actuators that interfere with CubeSat or small satellite attitude control and are prone to failure. Active metasurface-based optical communications transceivers with low size, weight, and power (SWaP) can potentially revolutionize the field of optical inter-satellite links in future NASA missions.
