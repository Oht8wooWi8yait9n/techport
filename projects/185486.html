Autonomous Paragliders for Martian Landings

NASA TechPort Project ID: 185486
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio

Project Description

Outpost is proposing Phase I research and development including design and manufacturing of a scaled paraglider system to be used in future flight test activities for verification and validation of system performance and scalability. This development effort would focus on the design of a passively stable paraglider system capable of deployment in a low density and pressure environment similar to that of the Martian atmosphere. Successful inflation and stable flight are prerequisites for precision guidance of the paraglider system. The largest risks of deploying a paraglider system at higher altitudes, and therefore lower pressures and densities, are the potential for aggressive surge leading to stall or failure to inflate and insufficient ram pressure for tip inflation.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Outpost will conduct an investigation of suitable parachute system designs, and assessment of any prior testing of complete assemblies showing low altitude or wind tunnel proof of concepts. Outpost will also provide reports documenting analysis and development results, including description of any materials, hardware, or prototypes investigated or developed. The proposed R&D will allow Outpost to provide targeted and guided precision landings in support of commercial logistics and DoD logistics missions through Earth Reentry and down mass.
