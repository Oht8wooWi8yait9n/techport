Autonomous Trajectory Anomaly Detection and Replanning in Cislunar Space

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186817
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Future NASA missions in cislunar space and beyond will require increased onboard autonomy to ensure safety, resilience, and affordability. Current operations rely heavily on ground-based teams and the Deep Space Network (DSN) to monitor trajectories and correct deviations -- an approach that is becoming unsustainable as mission volume grows through programs such as Artemis, Gateway, and expanded robotic exploration. Limited communications availability and increasing delays pose particular risks for crewed and deep-space missions, where rapid response to unexpected events may be required without timely Earth contact. This proposal addresses this challenge by developing and testing a new onboard capability for autonomous trajectory detection, corrective maneuvering, and trajectory replanning in the cislunar environment. The goal of this research is to develop and test a new approach to detect trajectory anomalies and respond using current mission constraints and spacecraft capabilities to inform maneuvers or replanning. This goal will be achieved by the following objectives: 1) adapt an isolation-based methodology for detecting anomalous trajectories to spacecraft motion of various geometries and sensitivity in cislunar space; 2) formulate adaptive anomaly maneuver responses and implement a post-execution verification system to detect and correct action anomalies; 3) develop an autonomous trajectory replanning method to reach the desired final state based on current system constraints and ensuring navigational efficiency; and 4) perform processor-in-the-loop testing to validate real-time operability and performance on spacecraft-representative hardware. By moving these activities onboard, the work aims to reduce reliance on the DSN, improve mission robustness during critical phases, and enable lower-cost missions with greater operational flexibility. To accomplish these objectives, the research combines isolation-based anomaly detection with motion-primitive-based trajectory planning. A compact onboard database of representative spacecraft motions will be constructed using motion primitives that capture typical expected trajectories of the mission. Real-time spacecraft motion will be compared against this database to compute an anomaly score, enabling early detection of unexpected behavior in the sensitive multi-body environment near the Moon. By avoiding computationally intensive machine learning models, the method remains suitable for flight processors. Following anomaly detection, the system will autonomously generate and evaluate candidate corrective maneuvers that respect mission constraints such as fuel limits, time-critical events, and reduced control authority. If corrective maneuvers are infeasible, the system will initiate rapid trajectory replanning using a motion-primitive-based graph search. Replanned trajectories will prioritize navigational efficiency to improve orbit determination accuracy and future anomaly detection. A post-maneuver verification step will assess whether the commanded action was executed as intended and update system constraints accordingly, closing the loop for continued autonomous operation. The proposed algorithms will be validated through processor-in-the-loop testing on spacecraft-representative hardware, such as processors used on CubeSat missions. This testing ensures that the methods are practical, efficient, and robust under realistic computational and timing constraints. The significance of this work lies in its direct relevance to NASA's goals for deep-space autonomy, scalable exploration infrastructure, and reduced operational costs. By enabling real-time onboard detection and response to trajectory anomalies, the proposed research supports safer crewed missions, more resilient robotic exploration, and greater mission independence from ground systems. It aligns strongly with NASA's priorities in autonomous navigation, low-SWaP-CP technologies, and cislunar space operations.
