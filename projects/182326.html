Advanced Spectrometer for Stable, Low ppb Hypergol Detection

NASA TechPort Project ID: 182326
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The Applied Chemistry Laboratory (ACL) at NASA KSC explored the development and validation testing of an advanced, low parts per billion (ppb) hypergol detection system with desired high stability. The team coordinated the development and integration of a plasma-based ion source and a dual stage, differential mass spectrometer for testing. The NASA team performed a variety of tests that determined the limit of detection and any interferences under differing sample gas flow rates, humidity content, and concentrations to elude the operation regime of the device.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The technical goal is to develop an advanced, tandem differential mobility spectrometer (DMS) for hypergol detection with a goal of maintaining the calibration over a 90-day period with +/- 25% accuracy at 10 ppb. The aim is to provide an alternate hypergol monitoring system that is more stable than the currently installed systems with a low limit of detection. Wide success of the system will provide a portable unit that does not require a radioactive source (as is needed in traditional DMS) and allow for shelf-life stability of calibration greater than a 2 week period. Thus, this would greatly reduce personnel time in maintaining calibrated devices as well as unit swaps at each installed location.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: As NASA’s launch cadence and KSC’s spaceport demands are ever increasing, new technology is needed in order to ensure the safe installation, storage, and use of equipment and vehicles that use hypergolic fuels. These fuels are known to be corrosive, toxic, carcinogenic, flammable, and have the potential to be explosive. Therefore, it is important to provide wide area monitoring to low level concentrations with accurate, stable detectors to ensure a safe working environment. The team at NASA integrated a micro-plasma ion source with a tandem dual-stage, differential mass spectrometer in order to examine the integrated technologies as a solution for stable, low level ppb hypergol detection for area monitoring. The team found that limit of detection for the instrument was approximately 5.9 ppb with a limit of quantification to be 19.96 ppb using a signal-to-noise ratio of 3 and 10, respectively. Signal drift was not able to be investigated. Several interferences common to hydrazine detectors were also tested, including humidity, ammonia, and isopropyl alcohol (IPA). This report discusses the successes and challenges of the developed unit within the scope of effort the team was able to provide. Future work is recommended to understand the device’s complete capabilities more fully and with guidance toward field implementation. The TRL of the project is determined to be a 3 by the team with a lab prototype unit built. Further work is necessary to pursue additional validation of the equipment for future design considerations.
