Project Description

Polycatenated Architected Materials (PAM) for Origami-Inspired Space Docking Ports Docking interfaces for small spacecraft must meet strict requirements in alignment tolerance, impact mitigation, structural load transfer, and long-term reliability with further constraints in mass, volume, and power. Current systems rely on rigid mechanical guides, compliant structures, dampers, or magnetic capture elements which have narrow misalignment tolerances and limited impact absorption. These systems are vulnerable to shock loads, rebounds, or off-axis torque or rotation during docking procedures. These issues are further amplified once they are placed on small spacecraft, where minor errors in relative velocity or alignment are a significantly greater risk. Origami-inspired structures offer a different approach to docking interface design. These structures use geometric folding to provide controlled and distributed deformation which have robust impact absorption and passive alignment correction. During docking contact, an origami-based docking interface can reduce sudden impact forces and transform them into equally distributed folding modes while simultaneously guiding the mating interfaces. Furthermore, these origami structures can reconfigure during deployment to adjust stiffness, port apertures, and energy dissipation with high precision, allowing for the docking interface to adapt to other interface designs. For small spacecraft, origami-inspired structures are attractive options for docking interface design as they are passive, low-power, lightweight, and reconfigurable structures. Despite these advantages, origami structures are greatly limited in their material selection due to the folding nature of the designs. Durable and strong materials traditionally used in space systems, such as titanium, cannot bend and fold as origami structures would require them to. To surpass this limitation, origami systems rely on thin polymers or composite laminates to permit bending at creases, but those materials lack the strength, wear resistance, and environmental durability required for docking interfaces. Polycatenated Architected Materials (PAMs) describe a type of geometric structure which can surmount this challenge while using rigid materials. A PAM structure consists of many smaller interlocked geometric structures to create a soft superstructure with folding and bending capabilities that are material-agnostic. Using PAMs for the hinges and joints would allow for the origami-inspired structure to maintain its deformability through folding while using materials which are incapable of folding. The incorporation of PAM structures into origami-inspired design allows for the use of high strength materials with deformability. An origami-inspired docking interface incorporating PAMs has the potential to unify alignment, impact absorption, and structural capture within a single passive mechanical system. Such a system could have greater misalignment tolerances, reduce contact-induced torque, and survive more launch loads and repeated docking cycles without reliance on delicate flexures or high-power actuators. This approach addresses a critical gap in current small-spacecraft docking technology by combining origami-based geometry and PAMs for a docking interface with greater precision, durability, impact absorption, and reconfigurability than what is achievable with existing systems.
