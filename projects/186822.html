Rapid Robust Trajectory Design via Motion Funnels in Multi-Body Systems

NASA TechPort Project ID: 186822
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The goal of the proposed research is to develop a new technology for rapidly designing spacecraft trajectories in a multi-body system that are robust to dynamical uncertainties and missed thrust events using motion funnels. Funnels are used in terrestrial motion planning for the rapid development of agile air and ground vehicle trajectories in the presence of model uncertainty and environmental disturbances. Similar to how motion primitives as used in robotics, funnels are assembled in sequence using graph-based searches. The inherent robustness of funnel connections make them highly promising for spacecraft trajectory design, but a new data-driven method for "motion funnel" computation and initial guess generation is needed in chaotic and sensitive multi-body systems where trajectories possess complex geometries. Innovative graph and optimization methods are also needed to fully leverage the motion funnel concept. My project narrative addresses these critical needs and outlines a new approach to rapidly design robust trajectories in a multi-body system. This goal will be achieved via the following objectives: 1. Generate a library of motion funnels to guarantee set-invariance of fundamental natural and low-thrust motion types under disturbance with bounded control authority. 2. Construct and efficiently search a hierarchical graph of motion funnels to rapidly assemble initial guesses for trajectories prioritizing robustness, propellant mass, and mission objectives. 3. Formulate, implement, and validate a new robust trajectory optimization framework informed by motion funnel sequences to reduce computational complexity. 4. Test and validate framework performance on flight representative low SWaP-C hardware. This proposed work would support future onboard autonomous navigation and decision-making. Additionally, it would provide ground-bound designers with a new approach to rapidly generate robust trajectories, in turn reducing the cost, complexity, and response time of ground operations.
