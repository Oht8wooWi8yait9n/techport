Project Description

This modest research program completes the effort that had to be terminated early in FY17. The focus is on completing testing of the previously developed micro-channel coolers. Specific efforts in this restart include the initial development of a miniature two-phase flow loop to be flown aboard a suborbital flight awarded by the NASA Flight Opportunities Program. Flight validation of the ground-testing results will complete the technology maturation required for near-term mission infusion.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

This technology, once demonstrated in a zero-gravity environment, will allow direct cooling of high heat flux chips or laser heads, thus allowing use of higher power devices in a compressed space.

  • Transitioned To: FY18 Annual Report Comments: (Notable Update) Research into flow boiling in microgap coolers as a way to cool power-dense electronic devices - initially at TRL 2 - was supported by the CIF program beginning in FY15. The progress made during that and follow-on CIF awards ultimately led to a proposal to the NASA Flight Opportunities Program (FOP) aimed at validating the technology. The FOP proposal was awarded in early 2017. The award included the costs of the suborbital flight aboard the Blue Origin New Shepard vehicle and $50K for payload development. The first flight is scheduled for late 2018 and follow-on flights may be awarded based on the outcome of the first flight. The payload development and microgravity validation collectively will advance the technology's TRL to the point that it may be included in future proposals for flight projects. The CIF-supported research has been the subject of multiple publications that include improved tools for predicting the behavior of two-phase flows in microgap channels and mitigating the effect of gravity on such flows. Additional publications are planned, including those relating to the upcoming suborbital flight. Furthermore, this research is the topic of the PI's doctoral dissertation, which will be completed in 2019. Finally, four summer interns have had opportunities to work on this research topic, which improved their understanding of fluid mechanics, heat transfer, and electronic packaging, and provided them with real-world experience developing technology. (Summary) Project/technology received funding from another NASA or other US government organization for a future fiscal year. A suborbital flight is planned for late 2018, with potential follow-on flights to test different microgap channels and flow parameters in 2019. The flight cost and payload development costs were awarded by the NASA STMD Flight Opportunities Program.
  • Closed Out: After completing a comprehensive, multi-year study into the thermofluid performance and effect of evaporator orientation on two-phase microgap coolers, the primary goal for the FY18 effort was to validate the terrestrial results by developing and flying a suborbital flight payload centered around a microgap cooler. The suborbital flight will expose the two-phase flow to microgravity and high-g; similar performance throughout various g-environments would indicate that the system is insensitive to g-effects, thereby reducing risk and development costs of future two-phase systems. Much of the payload development and testing was completed in FY18, but the launch date has been delayed. Final payload development activities are underway and the launch is expected at the end of 2018
