Polarization-free Cubic Phase GaN Ultraviolet Laser Diodes for Space-based Light Interferometry

NASA TechPort Project ID: 94106
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Laser diodes have many advantages over other forms of lasers: extremely compact (<1cm in length), inexpensive and simple designs that can achieve high power, high gain, high modulation speed, high monochromaticity, and excellent reliability and longevity. Aluminum gallium nitride (AlGaN) is material of choice to emit laser in the ultraviolet range due to its wide bandgap. However, conventional AlGaN devices are fabricated on III-Nitrides' thermodynamically stable hexagonal phase, which has properties (polarization fields, poor p-doping efficiency, low hole mobility, lack of cleavage planes) that are detrimental to the characteristics of the laser. GaN can also be engineered in its metastable cubic phase that has superior properties (polarization-free, higher hole mobility, higher gain, better p-doping efficiency, cleavage planes) for stimulated emission. My approach of growing cubic phase GaN via metalorganic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD) on carefully patterned Si(100) mitigates the common issues (phase mixing, defective) found in epitaxy on planar substrates, such as GaAs and 3C-SiC, while adding the benefits of inexpensive substrate, scalability, and the ability to be integrated with Si devices. This proposal opens up a path to polarization-free III-Nitride laser diode devices.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Cubic phase gallium nitride (GaN) offers many fundamental advantages over the conventional hexagonal phase GaN that are beneficial for radiation hard ultraviolet-blue-green laser and light emitting diodes, but its synthesis has shown to be difficult and of poor quality. The goal of this research project is to develop the semiconductor processing techniques to growth a stable and high-quality cubic phase GaN to exploit its superior optical properties for next generation wide bandgap photonics. Crystallographic modeling of the III-nitrides growth dynamics in U-shaped anisotropically etched grooves on Si(100) substrates will yield the patented critical dimensions required for complete phase transition of GaN from the thermodynamically stable hexagonal phase to the cubic phase. Nanopatterning of the substrate is completed in the cleanroom using photolithography, plasma etching, and wet chemistry, while the growth of the III-nitrides is accomplished using metalorganic chemical vapor deposition (MOCVD). The cubic phase GaN’s structural quality is assessed using electron microscopy, X-ray diffraction (XRD), and Raman spectroscopy, while the optical performance is assessed using time-resolved photoluminescence and temperature-dependent cathodoluminescence. Electron backscatter diffraction corroborated the crystallographic modeling, and selective area electron diffraction patterns shows the phase transition material is single crystalline cubic phase with no observable hexagonal phase inclusions. Both XRD and Raman spectroscopy showed the cubic phase GaN is under a very small tensile strain (0.4%). Internal quantum efficiency of the cubic phase GaN is found to be 2.6 times higher than that of conventional hexagonal phase GaN emitting at the near ultraviolet. This level of quality crystal growth is achieved with less than 500 nm of material deposited that corresponds to approximately 10 minutes of MOCVD disposition time
