High Rate Delay/Disruption Tolerant Network (DTN) (HRDTN)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 94820
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Develop high speed (>600Mbps) DTN Bundle Protocol implementation in flight like hardware with interfaces to NASA's core Flight System (cFS) storage, telemetry and management applications. Common implementation capable of supporting multiple NASA mission scenarios including orbiter/lander and formation flying missions.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

DTN has the potential to improve science return, increase data rates, reduce operational/implementation complexity, and enable new mission concepts. Unlike current spacecraft data transfer mechanisms, DTN is a standard suite of network layer protocols functionally equivalent to UDP/IP and TCP/IP including end to end routing over multiple hops. DTN differs in that it is designed to operate over the long delays and disruptions common in space communications

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: The high-rate DTN project designed and implemented algorithms that significantly enhanced the state of art for store and forward DTN networks. The implementation was able to achieve data rates well in excess of the goal for unidirectional block level transfers on a representative flight board. During the design phase several performance limiting and fault tolerance issues were found with the current Bundle Protocol Version 6 RFC and the CCSDS standard which the project team documented in an interplanetary network requirements whitepaper. The whitepaper included potential solutions to the issues and was the basis for discussions with members of the IETF and CCSDS DTN standards working groups. These discussion led agreements to update the standards and document a more complete set of human exploration and robotic systems use cases. Although this was not the primary objective of the high-rate DTN project, this was a critically important result. The DTN project is continuing with the high speed implementation based on the recent Bundle Protocol Version 7 RFC, Bundle-in-Bundle Encapsulation RFC, Concise Binary Object Representation (CBOR) RFC, and changes to the CCSDS Bundle Protocol Specification. These updates help ensure that DTN can form the basis of the interplanetary internet and allow implementations on the widest range of systems, from low power CubeSats and rovers to multi-gigabit optical store and forward relay satellites. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry and other Government Agencies.
