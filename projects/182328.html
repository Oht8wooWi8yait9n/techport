Autonomous Measurement Readout of Multiple, Non-instrumented, Analog Gauges Using Artificial Intelligence

NASA TechPort Project ID: 182328
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

This project developed a system that can read analog gauges using a video source. The low TRL work implemented a combination of algorithms that included artificial intelligence (AI), computer vision, and statistics. This work developed a framework for the processes of collecting and pre-processing gauge image data, selecting the structure and parameters of a convolutional neural network, and optimizing them for deep learning. The work also included the development of algorithms and processes to recognize a particular gauge, from others, on a ground support equipment (GSE) panel visible from a single camera field of view. In addition to the implementation of the gauge system in MATLAB, this work implemented the machine learning and AI algorithms in C-language for deployment in small form, low-cost embedded systems such as a raspberry pi single board computer.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The main application of this research in machine learning is to assist humans with the task of reading, recording, and transmitting analog gauge value data from locations where current systems are not capable or unavailable. For example, the autonomous system framework and the implemented algorithms developed for this research can be customized to alert personnel if gauge values are outside nominal range. Whether that is on the Moon or at KSC, having this capability can enhance efficiency and productivity by offloading these tasks to a portable smart machine.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: Manual reading of analog gauges at KSC is widespread and routinely done in support of work across the center including the cryogenics test laboratory and Space Launch System/Artemis operations during GSE tests, vehicle checkouts, and launch countdown processes. To help automate manual reading of analog gauges, this research developed a system that can read analog gauges using AI and computer vision technologies. This work was fully implemented in MATALB language with a subversion of the algorithms written in C-language for deployment in small form and low-cost embedded systems and robots. The efforts implemented algorithms to detect and recognize a particular gauge, from others, on a GSE panel visible from a single camera field of view. The detection of a region of interest (ROI), such a particular gauge or area on the gauge, was done by using statistical methods that detect speed-up robust features and “blobs” in an image ROI where each pixel is tested to see if it is significantly different from its neighbors. Once the ROI is detected in the image data taken from the camera, a polygon encompassing the ROI is computed and the enclosed pixel data information is extracted and passed to a trained neural network that can analyze it, read it, and report the value of the analog gauge. Several processes were used to design, test, and train a deep neural network that can learn on analog gauge positions to be able to understand and read gauge values effectively. The neural network that performed the best on learning and reading values was a custom regression convolutional deep neural network with 24 layers. In addition to the input and output layers, the designed neural network implemented five convolutional layers, three 2D max pooling layers, two normalization layers, three fully connected layers, and seven neuron activation functions. The neural net was trained using hundreds of images depicting the gauge needle at different positions on the gauge dial. Two different performance parameters were analyzed: the performance on the AI to learn on training gauge data, and the performance on correctly reading a particular gauge from a “realtime” data camera feed. Results show this neural network design can learn on gauge images with a high degree of performance as measured by relative percent error between predicted and actual value. Depending on the settings, some of the net training sessions produced prediction errors less than a fraction of one percent. “Realtime” end-to-end tests, consisting of “realtime” image acquisition, ROI detection and segmentation, neural net data processing, and value prediction show a distribution of relative errors ranging from five percent to less than one percent. This range of values suggests the AI system could be prone to inaccuracies similar to the causes of intrinsic human eye limitations, such as parallax errors - whenever the camera view is not directly in line with the measurement marking and the gauge pointer. The results from this research look promising and suggest that additional work is needed to further study the feasibility and limitations of designing a low cost system to read analog gauges based on technologies that implement AI algorithms. These machine learning algorithms, processes, and methods developed by this research evolved from a TRL of 3 to an approximate TRL of 4.5.
