Project Description

PEaRLS refers to a collection of 11 studies and prototype development efforts selected from an HLS Next Step Solicitation. These uncoupled efforts were to be completed within six months and results were used to refine the architecture, functional allocation options, standards, and common interfaces required to enable the aggregate system to provide human landing capability on the Moon. These 11 efforts focused on refueling elements of the architecture.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Advances technologies needed to support Human Landing and sustainment capabilities on the Moon

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: NASA’s Human Exploration and Operations Mission Directorate and Space Technology Mission Directorate funded the 11 companies that led the Appendix E work: Aerojet Rocketdyne of Canoga Park, California; Blue Origin of Kent, Washington; Boeing of Houston, Texas; Dynetics of Huntsville, Alabama; Lockheed Martin of Littleton, Colorado; Masten Space Systems of Mojave, California; Maxar Technologies of Westminster, Colorado; Northrop Grumman of Dulles, Virginia; Orbit Beyond of Edison, New Jersey; Sierra Nevada Corporation of Louisville, Colorado; and SpaceX of Hawthorne, California. Seven out of the 11 companies conducted demonstrations or built prototypes to address cryogenic fluid management. Cryogenic propellants are gases such as oxygen, methane, and hydrogen that are cooled to very cold (cryogenic) temperatures until they are in a liquid state that requires less volume—i.e., more usable energy in smaller tanks. Cryogenic space propulsion systems exhibit very high performance, but even the slightest source of ambient heat generated by the vehicle itself, or changes in pressure due to the vacuum of space, can lead to “boil off” or loss of fuel as it reverts to its gaseous state and vents from the tank.
