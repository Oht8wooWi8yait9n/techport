Near-Zero Power Photoswitches for Free-space Optical Communication (FSO) and LIDAR (NZPP)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 94826
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

We propose to develop Near-Zero Power Photoswitches (NZPP) for Free-Space Optical (FSO) communication and LIDAR in NASA applications. The applications will be but not limited to NASA future flight opportunities from free space laser communications, altimetry, trace gas sensing to in-situ spectroscopic instruments. The photoswitches are Micro-Electro-Mechanical-System (MEMS) switches that remain dormant with zero-power until woken up to action by incoming optical signals like infra-red, laser and others. The feature of near zero power consumption will tremendously benefit our missions in space where resources are limited.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Future missions for NASA are most likely going to be dominated by SmallSats or CubeSats. As such the available resources will be limited. One of the major problems in sensor networks is the electric power needed to drive individual sensors for sustainable and maintenance-free operation. This new technology with multifunctional applications will provide a path to further lower the size, weight and power (SWaP) for the instrument, and a way to minimize background noise and simplify the receiver design.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: This project was successful in partnering with Northeastern University (NEU) in the fabrication and packaging of Near Zero Power Photoswitches in Professor Matteo Rinaldi’s Lab. We were able to fabricate, package, and test several variations of MEMS switches. Recommendations for design improvements were made to NEU to increase the sensitivity to levels needed for free space optical communications, LIDAR, altimetry, and spectrometry applications. The next steps would be to investigate the technology for photonic integrated circuit (PIC) implementation. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
