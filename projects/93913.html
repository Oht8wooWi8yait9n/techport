Co-Operative Blending of Autonomous Landing Technology (COBALT)

NASA TechPort Project ID: 93913
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

The project is a partnership between STMD-GCD, STMD-FOP, and HEOMD-AES, to perform open-loop and closed-loop flight testing of Terrain Relative Navigation (TRN) and Navigational Doppler LIDAR (NDL) systems on a Masten xodiac launch platform. The main objective is to integrate these systems into a seamless guidance, navigation and control capability.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

NASA Funded: Reduces risk for infusion of precision landing GN&C technologies into near-term human and robotic exploration missions. NASA Unfunded: Promotes inclusion of new precision landing GN&C technologies to increase surface accessibility, and thus enable the formulation of new mission concepts. OGA: Provides non-GPS based precision navigation and sensors that could be utilized on non-spaceflight vehicles or in GPS-deprived environments. Industry: Provides integration and testing experience to Masten Space Systems, and also promotes the NASA technologies to other industry partners interested in precision landing capabilities. Nation: Promotes NASA's mission to explore new destinations, which will require the ability to precisely land in uncertain and risky terrain locations.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Advanced From
  • Advanced From
  • Advanced To: Expanding scope and continuing development as SPLICE in FY18.
  • Closed Out: At the system level, COBALT achieved TRL4 through bench top testing in a lab environment. Through the open loop flight test campaign, the Navigation Doppler Lidar component of COBALT achieved TRL5. The project was unable to perform the planned closed loop flight campaign due to technical difficulties with the system avionics. The project has reformulated into a FY18 new start called SPLICE. The COBALT Project integrated a navigation payload onboard the Masten Space Systems xodiac suborbital rocket vehicle and performed three tethered tests and two free flights. The COBALT navigation solution blends a NASA Doppler LIDAR velocity and range measuring capability with a inertial measurement unit and vision based lander system to provide terrain relative navigation technology. The COBALT integrated system acquires measurements and generates a navigation and landing solution in real-time. During the open loop, free flight test campaign it was determined that the integrated computer processing capability was insufficient for real-time calculation needs and needed to be increased to a parallel processing capability. The required technology scope was changed to include computer architecture enhancements which were incorporated into a new project named SPLICE.
