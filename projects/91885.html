Modeling, Testing, and Simulation of Heavy-Ion Basic Mechanisms in Silicon Carbide Power Devices

NASA TechPort Project ID: 91885
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

Power management and distribution systems in future NASA flights would benefit greatly from high-voltage DC power, both to reduce power losses and to facilitate solar electric propulsion, the use of ion drives powered by photovoltaic arrays. Silicon Carbide (SiC) power transistors are ideal electrical candidates for these applications, but heavy-ion radiation tests (in particle accelerators simulating the radiation environment experienced by space-borne electronics) of Silicon Carbide power diodes and SiC power MOSFETs reveal two unexpected phenomena: 1) discrete, permanent increases in off-state current with each ion strike; and 2) a single-event-burnout (SEB) type of hard failure where off-state current increases suddenly to destructive levels after an ion strike at bias levels only a fraction of the device maximum voltage rating. Experimentally observed heavy-ion behavior in SiC power devices is not currently captured in Poisson-solver Technology Computer-Aided Design (TCAD) finite-element simulation programs. It is unclear why the heavy-ion response of SiC devices differs so much from that of silicon power diodes and MOSFETs. Possible explanations include the wide band-gap and complex band structure of SiC, the anisotropic structure of the SiC crystal, lattice defects, and local disruption of the crystal structure by the energy deposition of the heavy ion. This work will seek to identify the physical mechanisms in the heavy ion response of SiC power devices through physical analysis, electrical and radiation testing, and device simulation, and to develop new and appropriate physics models for TCAD device simulation software that facilitate describing these physical mechanisms and their effect on device behavior. Once these mechanisms are well-understood, mitigation measures can be undertaken by device manufacturers to make the next generation of SiC devices less vulnerable to radiation effects, enabling NASA to use these power transistors to achieve their goals of high voltage, highly efficient, lightweight and compact space power systems.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

Reduction in size, weight, power for power management and distribution (PMAD) of future space missions. Road map to radiation qualification of SiC power devices for space missions. Radiation-robust SiC power diodes and transistors available from major device manufacturers.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Transitioned To (SBIR/STTR): Characterization and Mitigation of Radiation and High Temperature Effects in SiC Power Electronics (SBIR) and Modeling, Testing, and Simulation of Heavy-Ion Basic Mechanisms in Silicon Carbide Power Devices (LuSTR20 Witulski) In Phase II, we will develop further insight into physical mechanisms in the diodes via development and application of advanced physics models. We will parametrically analyze design features to identify promising hardness solutions, which will then be fabricated and experimentally characterized. LuSTR optimize SiC perormance for lunar applications.
  • Closed Out: Silicon carbide (SiC) power switching transistors and diodes are currently the most promising candidates for high-voltage and high-power density power conversion in space applications. Ion-beam experiments have demonstrated that SiC devices exhibit unacceptable leakage current increases and vulnerability to catastrophic events such as single-event breakdown (SEB) at low ion fluence and unexpectedly low percentages of the electrically rated maximum drain voltage. Why these heavy-ion effects occur in SiC power devices has been the focus of this work. This effort is to extend work on heavy-ion induces leakage current initiated in NASA ESI Grant NNX17AD09G. In that work, we were able to show that the onset of ion-induced reverse leakage current in silicon carbide (SiC) Schottky diodes depends on material properties, ion LET, and bias during irradiation, but not the voltage rating of the parts. This leakage current is also present in SiC power MOSFETs. In this work we have used statistical analysis of heavy-ion induced permanent leakage current degradation in silicon carbide power devices to enable evaluating on-orbit risk for flying this technology. This analysis will be used in Monte Carlo analyses to estimate expected leakage current under varying bias conditions, radiation environments, and mission lengths. This is important for evaluation of SiC power device reliability in the space radiation environment and for the qualification of SiC devices for space. Ultimately, this work will yield a reduction in the size and weight, and increase the reliability of space-borne power systems for power management and distribution (PMAD) of future space missions
