Integrated Quantum Cascade Laser to Drive THz Receivers

NASA TechPort Project ID: 95933
Official NASA Source: Space Technology Mission Directorate (STMD) TechPort Portfolio
View Official Project on NASA TechPort →

Project Description

To address the need for a high power source in the THz gap and enable the construction of a THz spectrometer, we are proposing to redesign the THz Quantum Cascade Laser (QCL) as a Local Oscillator (LO) source and integrate it with a feed horn and THz mixer. The resulting THz spectrometer will require significantly lower DC power, volume and mass (<1.5 W, 0.2 lbs) than the state of the art. This approach will allow us to integrate multiple pixel receivers without requiring an additional LO source.

Anticipated Benefits & Mission Applications

The proposed technology is one of the enabling components required to build a payload compatible with cubesat, suborbital, and space-based instruments. This approach can be used for astrophysics, heliophysics, Earth and planetary science missions that require a compact, tunable, and high spectral resolution spectrometer. Furthermore, NASA has directed that future Cosmic Origins (COR) strategic missions require continuous development and improvement of technologies beyond our current state-of-the-art. Raising the technology readiness level of the 1-5 THz LO was specifically given priority to be developed within the next few years in order to support a variety of instrumentation needs. Thus, this effort will have direct relevance for a wide variety of envisioned NASA science applications and future business opportunities.

Technology Transition & Outcomes

  • Closed Out: In this IRAD, we targeted to accomplish three tasks. The first task was fabricating Quantum Cascade Lasers diodes and Schottky diode-based THz mixer circuits. Both devices were fabricated and provided better than 80 percent fabrication yield. The second task was to perform the assembly of Quantum Cascade Lasers into a metallic block. In addition, we assembled a THz mixer and performed measurements. These tasks were successfully implemented, and the assembled components were tested (beam profile and total output power measurements). The third task was to assemble the QCL devices with the THz mixer and develop THz spectrometer. The assembly task was successfully completed. The purpose of the Goddard Space Flight Center’s Internal Research and Development (IRAD) program is to support new technology development and to address scientific challenges. Each year, Principal Investigators (PIs) submit IRAD proposals and compete for funding for their development projects. Goddard’s IRAD program supports eight Lines of Business: Astrophysics; Communications and Navigation; Cross-Cutting Technology and Capabilities; Earth Science; Heliophysics; Planetary Science; Science Small Satellites Technology; and Suborbital Platforms and Range Services. Task progress is evaluated twice a year at the Mid-term IRAD review and the end of the year. When the funding period has ended, the PIs compete again for IRAD funding or seek new sources of development and research funding, or agree to external partnerships and collaborations. In some cases, when the development work has reached the appropriate Technology Readiness Level (TRL) level, the product is integrated into an actual NASA mission or used to support other government agencies. The technology may also be licensed out to the industry. The completion of a project does not necessarily indicate that the development work has stopped. The work could potentially continue in the future as a follow-on IRAD; or be used in collaboration or partnership with Academia, Industry, and other Government Agencies.
